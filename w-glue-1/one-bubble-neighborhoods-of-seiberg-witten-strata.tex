\title[One-bubble neighborhoods]
{One-bubble neighborhoods\\
in $\SO(3)$-monopole moduli spaces}
\author{Bernd Johannes Wuebben}

\date{9 September 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{57K41 (primary); 57R57, 58J05, 58J37, 53C07 (secondary)}
\keywords{$SO(3)$ monopoles, Uhlenbeck compactification, gluing,
Seiberg--Witten moduli spaces, Donaldson invariants, Witten conjecture}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We prove an oriented neighborhood theorem at the first Uhlenbeck level
of $SO(3)$-monopole moduli spaces, over a compact regular
Seiberg--Witten moduli space of arbitrary dimension whose solutions have
nonzero spinor.  A finite-dimensional obstruction model admits an
equivariant compactified embedding that maps the obstruction zero set
homeomorphically onto an Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood of the ideal stratum;
its restriction to that zero set at
positive scale is a diffeomorphism onto its image.  The principal analytic
step is a strong approximation theorem in critical norms for arbitrary
convergent one-bubble monopole sequences.  Uniform gauge estimates and
summable decay along the neck recover the gluing parameters, including
the relative frame, and give reverse uniqueness.  A global equivariant
stabilization treats the background family without requiring a
constant-rank spectral cutoff.  Comparison with Donaldson's
instanton-addition map identifies the induced orientation with the
standard monopole orientation.  Under the additional topological
hypotheses of Feehan and Leness, the construction supplies the analytic
neighborhood input to their level-one link pairing.  We state the
resulting polynomial formula and its low-degree consequences for
abundant, effective manifolds of Seiberg--Witten simple type.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{The neighborhood problem}
Let $X$ be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented four-manifold, and consider
the Uhlenbeck compactification of a moduli space of $SO(3)$ monopoles.  Its
lower levels contain products of Seiberg--Witten moduli spaces with symmetric
products of $X$.  The $SO(3)$-monopole cobordism program compares the link of
the top anti-self-dual stratum with the links of these reducible strata.  A
link calculation has two distinct analytic inputs.  One must first construct
solutions of a finite-dimensional extension of the monopole equations from
splicing data.  One must then prove that the resulting zero set is an actual
neighborhood of the ideal stratum in the Uhlenbeck topology.

The first input was established in the local gluing theorem of Feehan and
Leness~\cite{FeehanLenessIII}.  That theorem constructs the gluing-data
bundle, a smooth solution map for the extended equations, and a finite-rank
obstruction section.  It gives the inclusion of the obstruction zero set in
the monopole moduli space.  Section~1.5.1 of the same paper separates from
this construction the remaining continuity and embedding statements and the
claim of surjectivity onto a Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood; it assigns this
package to a companion article.  In the one-bubble case,
\cite[Theorem~3.8]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} records the compactified embedding,
the obstruction sections, and the inclusion of their zero set in the monopole
moduli space, but it does not assert that this zero set maps onto an open
neighborhood of the ideal stratum.  That stronger conclusion is Property~(4)
of Hypothesis~7.8.1 in~\cite{FeehanLenessCobordism} and is retained there as
part of the local gluing hypothesis.

This paper constructs the neighborhood for one charge-one bubble
over a compact regular Seiberg--Witten moduli space of arbitrary dimension.
The standard orientation is identified by comparison with instanton
addition.  At this level there are no collision strata and no overlaps
among partitions of a symmetric product.  The family problem cannot be
reduced to the zero-dimensional case by choosing one spectral cutoff:
eigenvalue crossings can change the rank of the corresponding
small-eigenvalue spaces.  We instead stabilize the normal family globally by a smooth
finite-rank equivariant bundle.  Local cutoff reductions enter only through
a common enlargement, so neither the gluing chart nor its orientation
depends on a constant-rank spectral window.

\subsection{Results}
Let $b_1(X)=0$ and let $b_2^+(X)\geq3$ be odd.  Fix generic perturbation data,
a $\operatorname{spin}^u$ structure $\mathfrak t'$, and a
$\operatorname{spin}^c$ structure $\mathfrak s$ for which the level-one
reducible stratum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-stratum}
 M_{\mathfrak s}\times X
 \subset \overline{\cM}_{\mathfrak t'}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathfrak t$ denote the lower-level $\operatorname{spin}^u$ structure
containing $M_{\mathfrak s}$.  In the standard level-one notation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-ambient-structures}
 \mathfrak t=\mathfrak s\oplus\mathfrak s\otimes L,
 \qquad c_1(\mathfrak t)=c_1(\mathfrak t'),
 \qquad p_1(\mathfrak t)=p_1(\mathfrak t')+4.
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathfrak t'$ is the ambient structure obtained after splicing one unit
of instanton charge into a configuration for $\mathfrak t$.
Assume that $M_{\mathfrak s}$ is a compact regular smooth manifold, every
represented Seiberg--Witten pair has nonzero spinor, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-sw-dimension}
 \dim M_{\mathfrak s}=d_{\mathfrak s}=2d\geq0.
\end{equation}
The level-one construction has a compactified
virtual gluing space
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-virtual}
 \overline{\cM}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}
 (\varepsilon,\delta),
\end{equation}
an obstruction pseudo-bundle $\overline\Upsilon$, and a section $\chi$.
Their definitions are recalled in Section~\ref{sec:gluing-data}.
We write $\overline\cC_{\mathfrak t'}$ for the space of ideal
configuration classes with the Uhlenbeck topology, restricted to levels
zero and one.  The superscripts $*,0$ indicate an irreducible connection
and a nonzero spinor on the top stratum; a bar includes their ideal limits.
An Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood is open in this ideal configuration space,
or in the indicated moduli subspace when intersected with it.  The
instanton summand of $\overline\Upsilon$ has complex rank one at positive
scale and collapses at the zero-scale cone point.

Writing $N(\varepsilon)$ for the background virtual-normal disk, the
compactified gluing space has the three strata
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-three-strata}
 \cG^+\quad\sqcup\quad
 \underbrace{(N(\varepsilon)-M_{\mathfrak s})\times X}_{\text{middle
 zero-scale stratum}}\quad\sqcup\quad
 \underbrace{M_{\mathfrak s}\times X}_{\text{lowest stratum}}.
\end{equation}
At positive scale the obstruction section has background and instanton
components $\chi=(\chi_s,\chi_i)$.  At zero scale the instanton fiber and
$\chi_i$ collapse, while $\chi_s$ restricts to the background obstruction
section $b:N(\varepsilon)\to\Xi$.  Thus $b$ is transverse on the middle
stratum and vanishes on the lowest stratum.

Write $q_\lambda$ for the standard one-bubble pregluing and
$J_{s,\lambda}$ for the matched background obstruction embedding.
The background monopole map satisfies
$\mathfrak S(q)=\iota_{s,q}b(q)$.  Use $L^{\sharp,2;2}$ for
$L^{\sharp,2}$ on the self-dual-curvature component and $L^2$ on the
negative-spinor component; the critical norm is defined in
Section~\ref{sec:parametrix}.

We first state the analytic estimates and embedding used in the
neighborhood theorem.

\begin{thmletter}[uniform estimates and compactified embedding]
\label{thm:intro-analytic}
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Coulomb operator of the Euclidean charge-one instanton is
surjective on its homogeneous first-order domain and has kernel
equal to the eight-dimensional framed tangent space.
The augmented monopole linearization at $q_\lambda$ has a complement
determined by bounded integrals against background and framed bubble
modes.  On this complement its inverse, with ordinary $L^2$ gauge row
and mixed critical target, is bounded uniformly into the first-order
configuration space together with the finite-dimensional obstruction
space.

\item The inverse and its kernel projection have uniformly bounded first and
second derivatives in the graph norms on the fixed four-manifold $X$
defined in~\eqref{eq:graph-domain}, with center and scale
derivatives multiplied by $\lambda$ and with ordinary background and
frame derivatives.

\item The pair Laplacian has a uniformly bounded critical Green operator.
The full determinant-one gauge action admits a Coulomb slice of uniform
radius in the critical configuration norm~\eqref{eq:critical-configuration-norm}.
The Green operator, horizontal projection, and slice maps have two
uniformly bounded normalized parameter derivatives.
\item The pregluing family
admits uniform tubular coordinates locally in normalized parameter
charts, with complement determined by the configuration retraction $R_g$
of Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse}.

\item The projected splicing residual satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-residual}
 \left\|\mathfrak S(q_\lambda)-J_{s,\lambda}b(q)\right\|_{L^{\sharp,2;2}}
 \leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
There is a unique small correction in this complement, with
obstruction coefficients satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-correction}
 \|u_\lambda\|_{L^2_1}
 +\|\eta_{s,\lambda}\|+\|\eta_{i,\lambda}\|
 \leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
The correction is smooth at positive scale, and the corrected map
extends continuously at zero scale in the Uhlenbeck topology.
The residual and correction also have two normalized parameter
derivatives of order $O(\lambda^{1/3})$ in the target and augmented
graph norms; the configuration derivatives obey the same bound in
the critical configuration norm.
\item The corrected map is an equivariant compactified topological
embedding, smooth on the positive-scale stratum.  Multiplying its
correction by $t\in[0,1]$ gives an equivariant isotopy through such
embeddings from splicing to the corrected map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thmletter}

The Euclidean model, complemented inverse, and differentiated bounds are
proved in Propositions~\ref{prop:euclidean-fredholm},
\ref{prop:uniform-inverse}, and~\ref{prop:uniform-derivatives}.
Propositions~\ref{prop:residual}, \ref{prop:correction}, and
\ref{prop:correction-derivatives} give the residual and correction
estimates, including their derivatives; Propositions~\ref{prop:pair-green}
and~\ref{prop:pair-slice} give the critical gauge estimates and slice.
Proposition~\ref{prop:pregluing-modulation} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:modulation} prove uniform parameter recovery.
Lemma~\ref{lem:outer-frame} recovers the relative frame from the critical
gauge equation; Corollary~\ref{cor:embedding} and~\eqref{eq:isotopy}
give the embedding and isotopy.
Proposition~\ref{prop:strong-splicing} obtains critical approximation
for arbitrary one-bubble sequences by annular gauge patching and
summable cylinder estimates.  The neighborhood assertion below follows
by reverse uniqueness.  Proposition~\ref{prop:addition-orientation}
identifies the standard orientation by instanton addition.

\begin{thmletter}[level-one neighborhood; $=$
Theorem~\ref{thm:neighborhood}]\label{thm:intro-neighborhood}
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, there is an
$S^1$-equivariant map
\[
 \gamma:\overline{\cM}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}
 (\varepsilon,\delta)\longrightarrow\overline\cC_{\mathfrak t'}
\]
with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The map is a topological embedding, is smooth on the positive-scale
stratum, and is joined to the splicing map by an $S^1$-equivariant isotopy
through compactified embeddings.  On the zero-scale face it is the canonical
background-and-ideal-point map.
\item There is an Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood $\cU$ of
$M_{\mathfrak s}\times X$ for which the restriction
\[
 \gamma:\chi^{-1}(0)\cap\gamma^{-1}(\cU)\longrightarrow
 \cU\cap\overline\cM_{\mathfrak t'}^{*,0}
\]
is a homeomorphism and is a diffeomorphism on the positive-scale stratum.
\item The total obstruction section is transverse on the positive-scale
stratum, and its background component is transverse on the middle
zero-scale stratum.  Its norm is continuous along the compactified section.
No transversality is asserted on the lowest stratum.
\item For a homology orientation of $X$, the standard determinant-line
orientation agrees with the orientation of the transverse zero locus.
This orientation descends through
the frame and residual-circle quotients and induces the standard boundary
orientation used in the level-one link pairing.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thmletter}

The last qualification in (3) is forced by the geometry.  On the lowest
stratum the surviving background obstruction section is identically zero.
If its rank is positive, its restriction cannot be transverse.  This is the
statement used in the relative-Euler-class calculation of
\cite[Section~5]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}; it also agrees with the qualification
following Theorem~3.8 there.  The all-strata formulation in
\cite[Hypothesis~7.8.1(2)]{FeehanLenessCobordism} uses a coarser
stratification than~\eqref{eq:intro-three-strata}.

Under the additional topological hypotheses of the published
calculations, Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-neighborhood} supplies the deferred
analytic input to the level-one link pairing of
\cite[Theorem~6.1]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  Section~\ref{sec:pairing}
records its polynomial form, including zero Jacobi values, and its
zero-dimensional specialization.  Corollary~\ref{cor:degree} gives
the resulting degree comparison for abundant, effective manifolds of
Seiberg--Witten simple type.  These application statements retain their
stated topological assumptions; no
higher-level neighborhood result is used or proved here.

\subsection{Proof strategy}
The linear construction uses the charge-one instanton on Euclidean
$\RR^4$, with homogeneous first-order domain and ordinary $L^2$ target.
Proposition~\ref{prop:euclidean-fredholm} identifies its Coulomb kernel
with the eight-dimensional framed deformation space.  Extending the
rescaled physical metric on the bubble before constructing its inverse
makes the principal symbols agree on the localization region.  The
remaining errors are zero-order terms and cutoff commutators.
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse} gives the resulting complemented
inverse in mixed critical norms.  The projected residual has norm
$O(\lambda^{1/3})$, and contraction gives a correction of the same order.
Differentiating the explicit regional residual formulas preserves this
rate.  The square nonlinear equation obtained by adjoining the
configuration retraction then gives two derivatives of the correction
with the same bound.

To differentiate the inverse, we identify nearby gluing bundles over the
identity of the physical four-manifold.  The metric and principal symbols
then remain fixed.  Differentiated connection coefficients are uniformly
bounded critical multipliers, and differentiation of the retraction acts
on its smooth dual profiles.  The graph domains coincide in these local
identifications.  Adjoining the retraction makes the operator invertible,
so the inverse differentiation formula gives two uniform derivatives in
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-derivatives}.
The ordinary compact Coulomb operator on $S^4$ has only five ASD kernel directions;
the Euclidean homogeneous domain accounts for the three additional
framing directions.  Section~\ref{sec:parametrix} also explains why
separate compact conformal transports do not have uniform norms.

The nonnegative spinor potential in the pair Laplacian preserves the
critical Green estimate.  Combined with a uniform spectral gap, it gives
a uniform Coulomb slice for the full determinant-one gauge group in
Propositions~\ref{prop:pair-green} and~\ref{prop:pair-slice}.
The configuration norm controls both the first-order norm and the critical
norm of the divergence.  To exclude identifications by gauges outside the
local exponential chart, we use their first-order equation and a scalar
spectral gap to prove that they too have small critical norm.
For uniform parameter recovery, we compare pregluing derivatives with
explicit Euclidean Coulomb modes.  A bounded covariantly harmonic adjoint
section supplies the frame modes and cancels the large annular term from
differentiating the transition function.  The background comparison uses
the pair Coulomb connection and its induced connection on relative frames.
Differentiating the zero Coulomb row of the kernel lift then controls
the moving complement in the critical norm.  These comparisons prove
the tubular-map bounds and uniform modulation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:pregluing-modulation} and Lemma~\ref{lem:modulation}.
To recover the relative frame globally, we compare two constructed pairs
in the common exterior trivialization.  The gauge equation controls the
scalar Laplacian of the comparison gauge in $L^\sharp$.  Smooth limits at
the two ends allow a matrix-valued extension, to which the scalar critical
Green estimate applies.  Its bound is independent of neck length and
forces the inner constant gauge to agree with the exterior normalization.
This proves frame recovery for the corrected family and its interpolation
in Lemma~\ref{lem:outer-frame}.

For arbitrary one-bubble monopole sequences, local regularity improves
small-energy gauges to higher regularity on each normalized annulus.
The transition equation then permits patching across the complete neck
with uniform local bounds.  We impose Coulomb gauge on each round
three-sphere and remove the mean temporal gauge term by constant
rotations, retaining their inner endpoint as the relative frame.
The coexact tangential operator has a spectral gap.  Its two-sided
estimate bounds the sum of local connection norms by the endpoint
errors and a summable curvature source.  Scaled weighted estimates
and a separate spinor Weitzenb\"ock estimate give critical strong
splicing approximation in Proposition~\ref{prop:strong-splicing}.

With these inputs, contraction, modulation, and reverse uniqueness give the
neighborhood theorem.  For the orientation, the inverse also acts from
$L^{4/3}$ to a physical $L^2$ space in which bubble coordinates have
weight $\lambda$.  This estimate identifies the kernel synthesis
with orthogonal projection of cutoff modes.  We prove that this
comparison commutes with finite stabilization and with the homotopy
that turns the spinor off.  At zero spinor the Dirac blocks are
complex linear and the real block is Donaldson's instanton-addition
map.  Explicit quaternionic modes fix its scale and frame signs.
This gives the standard monopole orientation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:addition-orientation}; the horizontal tangent
comparison carries it to the corrected chart.
We use the residual-circle and outward-radial
convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-orientation}
 \lambda_{\cM}=-v_{S^1}\wedge\vec r\wedge\widetilde\lambda_L,
\end{equation}
from~\cite[Equation~(3.81)]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.

\section{Level-one gluing data}\label{sec:gluing-data}

\subsection{The monopole map and its deformation operator}
Let $\mathfrak t'$ be represented by a Clifford module
$V=V^+\oplus V^-$ with Clifford multiplication $\rho$ and adjoint bundle
$\mathfrak g_V$.  A configuration is a pair $(A,\Phi)$ consisting of a
compatible connection and a section of $V^+$.  We use the perturbed monopole
map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monopole-map}
 \mathfrak S(A,\Phi)=
 \left(
 F_A^+-\tau\rho^{-1}(\Phi\otimes\Phi^*)_{00},
 (D_A+\rho(\vartheta))\Phi
 \right).
\end{equation}
The perturbations are fixed throughout and belong to a compact generic
family.  For the determinant-one gauge group, our infinitesimal-action
convention is
\[
 d^0_{A,\Phi}\zeta=(d_A\zeta,-\zeta\Phi).
\]
Thus
\[
 d^{0,*}_{A,\Phi}(a,\phi)=d_A^*a-(\,\cdot\,\Phi)^*\phi,
\]
and the derivative of~\eqref{eq:monopole-map} is
\begin{align}\label{eq:d1}
d^1_{A,\Phi}(a,\phi)=\bigl(&d_A^+a-
\tau\rho^{-1}(\Phi\otimes\phi^*+\phi\otimes\Phi^*)_{00},\notag\\
&(D_A+\rho(\vartheta))\phi+\rho(a)\Phi\bigr).
\end{align}
The gauge-fixed operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-fixed}
 \mathscr D_{A,\Phi}=d^{0,*}_{A,\Phi}\oplus d^1_{A,\Phi}.
\end{equation}
At an approximate solution $d^1d^0$ need not vanish.  All parametrices below
include the gauge row and do not use a deformation complex at the preglued
pair.

Equation~(2.3) of~\cite{FeehanLenessIII} prints a factor $1/2$ in the
spinor-bilinear term of $d^1$, whereas the literal derivative of the displayed
monopole map, Equation~(9.3) there, and
\cite[Equation~(2.36)]{FeehanLenessI} have the normalization in
\eqref{eq:d1}.  We use~\eqref{eq:d1}.  Interpolating the discrepant
zeroth-order coefficient through $[1/2,1]$ does not change a determinant-line
orientation; the characteristic-class normalization is checked independently
in Section~\ref{sec:pairing}.

\subsection{The background finite-dimensional reduction}
The lower-level structure in~\eqref{eq:lower-ambient-structures} has the
reducible splitting
\[
 \mathfrak t=\mathfrak s\oplus\mathfrak s\otimes L.
\]
Along the reducible embedding of $M_{\mathfrak s}$, the gauge-fixed family
splits into tangential and normal summands
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-splitting}
 \mathscr D=\mathscr D^t\oplus\mathscr D^n.
\end{equation}
Regularity of the Seiberg--Witten equations gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangential-family}
 \ker\mathscr D^t=TM_{\mathfrak s},\qquad
 \coker\mathscr D^t=0.
\end{equation}
The normal family is complex linear, and the residual circle acts on its
domain and target through one complex character.

We use the extrinsic finite-dimensional reduction of
\cite[Theorem~3.19]{FeehanLenessTopLevel}.  Compactness of $M_{\mathfrak s}$
gives a finite-rank globally trivial complex bundle
$\Xi=M_{\mathfrak s}\times\CC^{r_\Xi}$, equipped with the normal
circle character, and an equivariant target embedding
$\iota_\Xi$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-augmentation}
 \widehat{\mathscr D}^{n}_p
 =\mathscr D^n_p\oplus(-\iota_{\Xi,p}):
 \operatorname{Dom}\mathscr D^n_p\oplus\Xi_p
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Tar}\mathscr D^n_p
\end{equation}
is surjective for every $p\in M_{\mathfrak s}$.  The cited theorem constructs a trivial smooth equivariant target
subbundle; this triviality is part of the choice, not a consequence of
cokernel spanning alone.  Fix a Hermitian trivialization and pull it back
over the tubular neighborhood.  Surjectivity persists after shrinking that
neighborhood.  No spectral projection is used.

The augmented kernels form a smooth complex bundle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bundle-definition}
 N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}
 =\ker\widehat{\mathscr D}^{n}\longrightarrow M_{\mathfrak s}.
\end{equation}
Thus the full augmented kernel is
$TM_{\mathfrak s}\oplus N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:index-family}
 \operatorname{Ind}_{\CC}(\mathscr D^n)
 =[N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}]-[\Xi]
 \quad\text{in }K^0(M_{\mathfrak s}).
\end{equation}
The projected convention of~\cite{FeehanLenessIII} is equivalent: if
$\Pi_{\Xi^\perp}$ is orthogonal projection, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-augmented-kernel}
 u\longmapsto\bigl(u,\iota_\Xi^{-1}\mathscr D^nu\bigr)
\end{equation}
identifies $\ker(\Pi_{\Xi^\perp}\mathscr D^n)$ with
$\ker\widehat{\mathscr D}^{n}$.

After choosing a sufficiently small lifted disk in
$N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}$, the implicit-function theorem gives a smooth
embedding into the thickened configuration quotient and a section
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-obstruction}
 b:N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow\Xi,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak S(q)=\iota_{s,q}b(q).
\end{equation}
Its zero set is the full nearby lower-level monopole moduli space
in this thickening; it contains $M_{\mathfrak s}$ and can also contain
irreducible monopoles.  Regularity of the Seiberg--Witten stratum does not
exclude those additional zeros.  The restriction of $b$ off the zero
section is transverse for the generic perturbations under consideration
\cite[Theorem~3.21]{FeehanLenessTopLevel}.

There is also a distinction between the derivative of this reduction and
the augmented operator with its embedding fixed at $q$.  In a smooth local
slice and the fixed trivialization of $\Xi$, differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-derivative}
 D\mathfrak S_q(v)-\iota_{s,q}Db_q(v)
 =(D\iota_s)_q[v]\,b(q),\qquad v\in T_qN(\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
The right side vanishes on the zero section and is $O(\varepsilon)$ on
the fixed disk; it is not an $o(1)$ term as the bubble scale tends to zero.
The literal augmented kernels therefore agree with the tangent bundle on
the zero section only.  Off it, projection of the configuration component
of $T_qN(\varepsilon)$ onto the augmented kernel defines a smooth bundle
isomorphism $A_q$ after shrinking $\varepsilon$: at the zero section it
is the identity, and invertibility is open.  All identifications below use
this isomorphism, with its uniformly bounded inverse, rather than dropping
the right side of~\eqref{eq:background-derivative}.

The term \emph{virtual-normal disk}
will always mean this disk bundle; it never means an eigenspace selected by
a spectral cutoff.  Since every pair on the zero section has nonzero spinor,
compactness and openness allow the disk to be chosen so that every pair in
it has trivial determinant-one infinitesimal stabilizer.  Put
\[
 r_N=\rank_{\CC}N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s},\qquad
 r_\Xi=\rank_{\CC}\Xi.
\]
The background parameter bundle therefore has real dimension
$d_{\mathfrak s}+2r_N$, while its obstruction variables have real dimension
$2r_\Xi$.

\subsection{The charge-one factor and compactification}
Let $M_1^{s,\natural}(S^4,\delta)$ be the centered framed charge-one ASD
moduli space with scale in $(0,\delta]$.  There are natural identifications
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charge-one-cone}
 M_1^{s,\natural}(S^4,\delta)
 \cong(0,\delta]\times\SO(3),
 \qquad
 \overline M_1^{s,\natural}(S^4,\delta)
 \cong c(\SO(3)).
\end{equation}
If $I$ is the standard centered instanton, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bubble-cokernel}
 \ker D_I=0,
 \qquad K_I:=\coker D_I=\ker D_I^*,
 \qquad \dim_{\CC}K_I=1.
\end{equation}
The unframed charge-one ASD deformation space on the round $S^4$ has
real dimension five, and the compact Coulomb operator is surjective
\cite[Sections~6 and~9]{AHS78}.  The tangent space of the framed moduli space
fits into the exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:framed-deformation-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(3)\longrightarrow
 T_{[I]}M_1^{\mathrm{fr}}\longrightarrow
 H_I^1(S^4)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Thus the framed space has four translation, one dilation, and three
relative-frame directions.  The last three are additional framing
variables, not extra elements of the compact Coulomb kernel.
We write $\cK_I^{\rm fr}=T_{[I]}M_1^{\rm fr}$ for this
eight-dimensional tangent space, allowing all centers and scales.
We also denote the full framed space with scale in $(0,\delta]$ by
$M_1^s(S^4,\delta)$; the superscript $\natural$ imposes the centering
condition.

We recall the quotient notation of
\cite[Equations~(3.48)--(3.54)]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  The plain letter
$s$ denotes the marked framing pole on $S^4$, using the south-pole
convention of that source.  In $\Fr(\mathfrak g_V|_s)$ the bundle is the
adjoint bundle of the charge-one spherical model; the other adjoint frame
factor is over $X$.  A tilde denotes the space before the corresponding
gauge quotient: $\widetilde M_1^\natural$ consists of centered instanton
connections, and $\widetilde N$ is the pullback of the normal disk to
Seiberg--Witten representatives.  The group $\cG_{\mathfrak s}$ is the
Seiberg--Witten circle gauge group, acting on this lift through its inclusion
in the determinant-one monopole gauge group.

The pre-splicing factor is
\begin{align}\label{eq:gluing-quotient}
\widetilde\Gl_{\mathfrak t}(\delta)
=\bigl(&\Fr(\mathfrak g_V)\times_X\Fr(T^*X)
\times\Fr(\mathfrak g_V|_s)\notag\\
&\times\widetilde M_1^\natural(S^4,\delta)\bigr)
/(\SO(3)\times\SO(4)).
\end{align}
After dividing by the charge-one gauge group, one obtains
$\Gl_{\mathfrak t}(\delta)$.  Replacing the instanton factor by the cone in
\eqref{eq:charge-one-cone} gives $\overline\Gl_{\mathfrak t}(\delta)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virtual-space}
 \overline\cM^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}
 =\widetilde N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}(\varepsilon)
 \times_{\cG_{\mathfrak s}}\overline\Gl_{\mathfrak t}(\delta).
\end{equation}
At the cone point the relative instanton frame is forgotten.  Hence
\eqref{eq:virtual-space} has three strata:
\begin{align}\label{eq:three-strata}
\overline\cM^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}
={}&\cG^+\notag\\
&\sqcup\bigl((N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}(\varepsilon)
-M_{\mathfrak s})\times X\bigr)
\sqcup(M_{\mathfrak s}\times X),
\end{align}
where $\cG^+$ denotes the positive-scale stratum.

Before quotienting, the splicing map is equivariant for the diagonal
$\cG_{\mathfrak s}$ and charge-one gauge actions and for the diagonal
$\SO(4)$ tangent-frame and $\SO(3)$ adjoint-frame actions.  These auxiliary
frame actions are divided out; the relative $\SO(3)$ frame in
\eqref{eq:charge-one-cone} remains.

The residual circle has the two equivalent presentations of
\cite[Lemma~3.6]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  It may act by the monopole circle on
the normal factor and trivially on the compactified gluing factor, or
diagonally with weight two on both.  We use the effective circle in the link
quotient.  Passing from weight two to its effective parametrization does not
reverse the orbit orientation.

\subsection{The obstruction pseudo-bundle}
At positive scale the obstruction space is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obstruction-fiber}
 \Upsilon_g=\Upsilon_g^s\oplus\Upsilon_g^i
 \cong\Xi_q\oplus K_I.
\end{equation}
The background summand extends as an ordinary bundle over
\eqref{eq:three-strata}.  The instanton summand is the associated line
\[
 (0,\delta]\times\SU(2)\times_{\{\pm1\}}\CC
 \longrightarrow (0,\delta]\times\SO(3).
\]
Its compactification is the cone map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obstruction-cone}
 c(\SU(2)\times_{\{\pm1\}}\CC)
 \longrightarrow c(\SO(3));
\end{equation}
the fiber collapses at the vertex.  This is a rank-dropping pseudo-bundle,
not a singular trivialization of a line bundle.

\subsection{Strong and critical-norm Sobolev completions}
For smooth dependence at a fixed positive scale, choose $k\geq4$ and use the
standard Fredholm map
\[
 \mathscr D_{q_\lambda}:L^2_k(\Lambda^1\otimes\mathfrak g_V\oplus V^+)
 \longrightarrow
 L^2_{k-1}(\mathfrak g_V\oplus\Lambda^+\otimes\mathfrak g_V\oplus V^-).
\]
Uniform estimates as $\lambda\downarrow0$ use
\begin{align}\label{eq:weak-spaces}
 \mathbb X_{q_\lambda}&=L^2_{1,A_\lambda},\notag\\
 \mathbb Z_{q_\lambda}&=L^2(\mathfrak g_V),\notag\\
 \mathbb Y_{q_\lambda}&=
 L^{\sharp,2}(\Lambda^+\otimes\mathfrak g_V)\oplus L^2(V^-).
\end{align}
Here $L^{\sharp,2}$ is the completion of smooth sections in the sum of
the $L^\sharp$ and $L^2$ norms, following \cite[Section~4]{FeehanCritical}.
All critical spaces below use this completion convention.
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse} constructs an inverse on these
spaces, first on smooth target data and then on the graph completion.  We do not
assert that the full first-order operator maps every arbitrary $L^2_1$
section into the mixed critical target.

Choose matched finite-dimensional embeddings
\[
 J_{s,\lambda}:\Xi_q\to\mathbb Y_{q_\lambda},\qquad
 J_{i,\lambda}:K_I\to\mathbb Y_{q_\lambda}.
\]
The augmented linearization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:augmented-operator}
\widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda(u,\xi_s,\xi_i)
=\left(d^{0,*}_{q_\lambda}u,
d^1_{q_\lambda}u-J_{s,\lambda}\xi_s-J_{i,\lambda}\xi_i\right).
\end{equation}
The nonlinear augmented map is
\begin{align}\label{eq:augmented-map}
\widehat\cF_\lambda(u,\eta_s,\eta_i)
=\bigl(&d^{0,*}_{q_\lambda}u,
\mathfrak S(q_\lambda+u)\notag\\
&-J_{s,\lambda}(b(q)+\eta_s)-J_{i,\lambda}\eta_i\bigr).
\end{align}

The retained parameter directions have real dimension
$d_{\mathfrak s}+2r_N+8$: the background tangent and normal variables, four
translations, dilation, and three relative rotations.  The obstruction rank
is $2r_\Xi+2$.  Hence the expected dimension of the obstruction zero set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimension-check}
 d_{\mathfrak s}+(2r_N+8)-(2r_\Xi+2)
 =d_{\mathfrak s}+2(r_N-r_\Xi)+6.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[level-one neighborhood]\label{thm:neighborhood}
Let $b_1(X)=0$ and let $b_2^+(X)\geq3$ be odd.  Fix generic perturbation data
and a level-one pair $(\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s)$ as in
\eqref{eq:intro-stratum}--\eqref{eq:lower-ambient-structures}.  Suppose that
$M_{\mathfrak s}$ is compact and regular and that every represented
Seiberg--Witten pair has nonzero spinor.
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, there is
an equivariant compactified embedding
\[
 \gamma:\overline{\cM}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}
 (\varepsilon,\delta)\longrightarrow\overline\cC_{\mathfrak t'}
\]
and a continuous compactified obstruction section
$\chi=(\chi_s,\chi_i)$ with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The positive-scale maps are smooth, and $\gamma$ is equivariantly
isotopic through compactified embeddings to splicing.
\item After restricting to an Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood of
$M_{\mathfrak s}\times X$, the obstruction zero set maps
homeomorphically onto the corresponding ideal monopole neighborhood and
diffeomorphically on the positive-scale stratum.
\item The section $\chi$ is transverse on the positive-scale stratum,
and $\chi_s$ is transverse on the middle zero-scale stratum.  Its norm is
continuous.  No transversality is asserted on the lowest stratum.
\item The determinant-line orientation
induces the standard level-one
boundary orientation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof occupies Sections~\ref{sec:parametrix}--\ref{sec:orientations}.
The local pregluing construction and the one-sided inclusion
$\gamma(\chi^{-1}(0))\subset\overline\cM_{\mathfrak t'}^{*,0}$ are quoted
from~\cite{FeehanLenessIII}.  The Euclidean model, the inverse with two
uniform normalized parameter derivatives, and the small correction are proved in
Sections~\ref{sec:parametrix} and~\ref{sec:correction}.
The critical gauge estimates, slice, and uniform
modulation, frame recovery, embedding, and isotopy in
Section~\ref{sec:embedding} give the compactified embedding.
Section~\ref{sec:reverse-gluing} proves strong splicing approximation
and neighborhood surjectivity.  Section~\ref{sec:orientations}
compares the kernel map with instanton addition and identifies the
orientation of the corrected chart and its link boundary.

\section{The Euclidean model and the augmented inverse}\label{sec:parametrix}

\subsection{Critical norms and the square partition}
For a bundle-valued section $f$ on $X$, put
\begin{align}\label{eq:critical-norms}
 \|f\|_{L^\sharp}
 &=\sup_{z\in X}\int_X\operatorname{dist}(z,y)^{-2}|f(y)|\,dV(y),\notag\\
 \|f\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
 &=\sup_{z\in X}
 \left(\int_X\operatorname{dist}(z,y)^{-2}|f(y)|^2\,dV(y)\right)^{1/2},\\
 \|f\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}&=\|f\|_{L^\sharp}+\|f\|_{L^2}.
 \notag
\end{align}
The critical multiplication and Sobolev estimates of
\cite[Lemmas~4.1 and~4.3]{FeehanCritical} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-products}
 L^2_1\hookrightarrow L^{2\sharp},\qquad
 L^{2\sharp}\cdot L^{2\sharp}\longrightarrow L^\sharp,
\end{equation}
uniformly for the compact family of background connections under
consideration.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-sobolev}
 \|v\|_{L^4}+\|v\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
 \leq C\|v\|_{L^2_{1,A}}.
\end{equation}

Fix $N>4$.  The logarithmic cutoff of
\cite[Lemma~5.4]{FeehanLenessIII}, pulled back by geodesic normal coordinates
at $x$, may be chosen to satisfy
\begin{align}\label{eq:log-cutoff}
 \beta&=0 &&\text{if }r\leq N^{-1}\sqrt\lambda,
 &\beta&=1 &&\text{if }r\geq\tfrac12\sqrt\lambda,\notag\\
 \|d\beta\|_{L^4}&\leq C(\log N)^{-3/4},
 &\|d\beta\|_{L^{2\sharp}}&\leq C(\log N)^{-1/2},
 &\|d\beta\|_{L^2}&\leq C(\log N)^{-1}\sqrt\lambda.
\end{align}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-partition}
 \beta_X=\sin(\pi\beta/2),\qquad
 \beta_I=\cos(\pi\beta/2).
\end{equation}
Then $\beta_X^2+\beta_I^2=1$, and both derivatives obey the
bounds in~\eqref{eq:log-cutoff}.  These estimates are uniform in $x$: near
$x$ this follows from normal-coordinate comparison, and for a base point
outside a fixed larger chart the last, scale-decaying bound in
\eqref{eq:log-cutoff} controls the supremum in~\eqref{eq:critical-norms}.

\begin{lemma}[cutoff commutator]\label{lem:commutator}
For $j=X,I$ and smooth $u=(a,\phi)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:commutator-identity}
 [\mathscr D_{A_\lambda,\Phi_\lambda},\beta_j]u
 =\left(-\iota_{\nabla\beta_j}a,
 (d\beta_j\wedge a)^+,\rho(d\beta_j)\phi\right),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:commutator-estimate}
 \|[\mathscr D_{A_\lambda,\Phi_\lambda},\beta_j]u\|_{
 \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}
 \leq C(\log N)^{-1/2}\|u\|_{\mathbb X_{q_\lambda}}.
\end{equation}
The estimate extends by density to the weak domain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All zero-order terms commute with a scalar cutoff.  The three principal
parts give~\eqref{eq:commutator-identity}.  The gauge row, the $L^2$ part of
the curvature row, and the Dirac row are bounded using
$L^4\cdot L^4\subset L^2$ and the first estimate in
\eqref{eq:log-cutoff}.  For the critical part of the curvature row,
\eqref{eq:critical-products} gives
\[
 \|(d\beta_j\wedge a)^+\|_{L^\sharp}
 \leq C\|d\beta_j\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
       \|a\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
 \leq C(\log N)^{-1/2}\|a\|_{L^2_{1,A_\lambda}}.
\]
Adding the rows proves~\eqref{eq:commutator-estimate}.  Notice that no
derivative of $d\beta_j$ occurs: the augmented operator is first order.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Framed bubble spaces and norm transport}
For the round compact model, the stabilized differential expression is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round-bubble-operator}
 (a,\phi,\kappa)\longmapsto
 (d_I^*a,d_I^+a,D_I\phi-\iota_I\kappa).
\end{equation}
On the ordinary $L^2_1(S^4)$ domain its kernel has real dimension five.
Adding the spinor cokernel restores surjectivity of the Dirac block but
does not add the three framing variables in
\eqref{eq:framed-deformation-sequence}.

There is a separate issue with conformal norm transport.  Write
$y=z/\lambda$, $\Omega(y)=2/(1+|y|^2)$ and
$g_{S^4}=\Omega^2\delta$.  A conformal spinor transport with scalar
normalization $a_\lambda$ has the domain and target weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conformal-transport-weights}
 T^+_\lambda\phi(z)=a_\lambda\lambda^{-1}\Omega(y)^{3/2}\phi(y),
 \qquad
 T^-_\lambda\psi(z)=a_\lambda\lambda^{-2}\Omega(y)^{5/2}\psi(y).
\end{equation}
Direct change of variables gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-norm-identity}
 \|T^-_\lambda\psi\|_{L^2(\RR^4)}^2
 =|a_\lambda|^2\int_{S^4}\Omega\,|\psi|^2\,dV_{S^4}.
\end{equation}
For target sections supported on a collar $|y|\asymp\lambda^{-1/2}$,
where the bubble cutoff is bounded below, the inverse target transport has
norm of order $|a_\lambda|^{-1}\lambda^{-1/2}$.  On fixed profiles near
$y=0$, the homogeneous first-order domain norm of the transported section
is of order $|a_\lambda|$.  No scalar normalization makes both bounds
uniform.  This calculation concerns the separate transport maps; it does
not rule out a bounded composite parametrix.

We instead use the Euclidean instanton with the homogeneous space
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-bubble-domain}
 \dot H_I^1=\overline{C_c^\infty}^{\,\|\nabla_I(\cdot)\|_{L^2}}
 \hookrightarrow L^4(\RR^4).
\end{equation}
The pure dilations $u(z)=\lambda^{-1}v(z/\lambda)$ and
$f(z)=\lambda^{-2}h(z/\lambda)$ preserve respectively the homogeneous
first-order norm and the $L^2$ target norm.  We first establish the
Fredholm assertion for this domain, then localize the resulting inverse.

\begin{proposition}[Euclidean framed inverse]\label{prop:euclidean-fredholm}
Let $I$ be the centered, unit-scale charge-one ASD connection on the
$\SU(2)$ bundle $E_I$ over $S^4$, restricted to the complement of its
framing pole with the Euclidean metric.  The operator
\[
 Q_I=d_I^*\oplus d_I^+:
 \dot H_I^1(\Lambda^1\otimes\ad E_I)
 \longrightarrow L^2((\Lambda^0\oplus\Lambda^+)\otimes\ad E_I)
\]
is surjective.  Its kernel identifies with $\cK_I^{\rm fr}$ and has
real dimension eight.  The operator
$D_I^+:\dot H_I^1(S^+\otimes E_I)\to L^2(S^-\otimes E_I)$
is injective, has closed range, and has complex one-dimensional
cokernel $K_I$.

Choose a smooth compactly supported embedding
$\kappa_0:K_I\to C_c^\infty(S^-\otimes E_I)$ whose pairing with
the cokernel is nonsingular.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-augmented}
 \widehat B_0(a,\phi,c)
 =(d_I^*a,d_I^+a,D_I^+\phi-\kappa_0c)
\end{equation}
is surjective, has kernel $\cK_I^{\rm fr}\times\{0\}\times\{0\}$,
and has a bounded inverse $P_0$ on the homogeneous orthogonal
complement of that kernel.  Kernel elements satisfy
$|a(y)|=O(|y|^{-3})$ and $|\nabla_Ia(y)|=O(|y|^{-4})$
in the exterior framing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We use the Dirac operator twisted by either $F=E_I$ or
$F=\operatorname{End}_0E_I$.  In the exterior frame obtained from radial
gauge at the pole, $I=O(|y|^{-3})$.  Choose $R$ so large that its
$L^4$ norm outside $B_R$ is small.  For sections $v$ supported there,
the flat Fourier identity and the scalar Sobolev inequality give
\[
 \|\partial v\|_2=\|D_0^\pm v\|_2
 \leq\|D_{I,F}^\pm v\|_2+
 C\|I\|_{L^4(\RR^4\setminus B_R)}\|v\|_4.
\]
Absorbing the last term controls $\nabla_Iv$ as well.  An exterior
cutoff and interior elliptic estimates yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-compact-remainder}
 \|v\|_{\dot H_I^1}
 \leq C\bigl(\|D_{I,F}^\pm v\|_2+\|v\|_{L^2(B_{2R})}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Kato's inequality gives $\dot H_I^1\hookrightarrow L^4$ and hence
compact restriction to $L^2(B_{2R})$ by local Rellich compactness.
Estimate~\eqref{eq:euclidean-compact-remainder} therefore gives finite
kernel and closed range.  Indeed, a unit sequence orthogonal to the
kernel whose images tend to zero has a locally $L^2$ convergent
subsequence; the estimate applied to differences makes it converge in
$\dot H_I^1$, contradicting orthogonality.  The orthogonal complement
of the range consists of $L^2$ distributional solutions of the
opposite-chirality equation.

Both the homogeneous kernel and this $L^2$ harmonic space can be
computed on $S^4$.  Under the isometric spin-bundle identification
$\beta_{\rm sp}$ for $g_{S^4}=\Omega^2\delta$, conformal covariance gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-conformal-covariance}
 D_{I,F,g_{S^4}}(\Omega^{-3/2}\beta_{\rm sp}\psi)
 =\Omega^{-5/2}\beta_{\rm sp}D_{I,F,\delta}\psi.
\end{equation}
This is the ordinary spinor covariance law
\cite[Section~3, after Proposition~3.3]{Fischmann13}; the twisting
term transforms by the same rule because Clifford multiplication
by a one-form acquires the factor $\Omega^{-1}$.
For a Euclidean harmonic spinor $\psi\in L^4$, its transform
$\psi_S=\Omega^{-3/2}\beta_{\rm sp}\psi$ satisfies
\[
 \int_{S^4}|\psi_S|^{4/3}
 =\int_{\RR^4}\Omega^2|\psi|^{4/3}
 \leq\left(\int|\psi|^4\right)^{1/3}
       \left(\int\Omega^3\right)^{2/3}<\infty.
\]
For $\psi\in L^2$, the same conclusion follows with upper bound
$(\int|\psi|^2)^{2/3}(\int\Omega^6)^{1/3}$.
To remove the pole, choose $\chi_\epsilon$ vanishing within distance
$\epsilon$ and equal to one beyond $2\epsilon$, with
$\|d\chi_\epsilon\|_4\leq C$.  Against a smooth test spinor the
commutator error is bounded by
\[
 C\|\psi_S\|_{L^{4/3}(B_{2\epsilon}\setminus B_\epsilon)}
 \|d\chi_\epsilon\|_4\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the compact equation holds distributionally across the pole,
and elliptic regularity makes $\psi_S$ smooth.  Conversely, a smooth
compact harmonic spinor pulls back with value $O(|y|^{-3})$ and
covariant derivative $O(|y|^{-4})$.  It belongs to $L^2$ and $L^4$;
cutoffs at increasing radii put it in the homogeneous completion.

For $F_I^+=0$, the positive scalar curvature of the round metric
and the Lichnerowicz formula give $\ker D_{I,F}^+=0$.  The index
calculation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-index-table}
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 F&\ind_{\CC}D_{I,F}^+&\dim_{\CC}\ker D_{I,F}^-\\ \hline
 E_I&-1&1\\
 \operatorname{End}_0E_I&-4&4
 \end{array}
\end{equation}
since $\operatorname{ch}_2(E_I)=-c_2(E_I)$,
$\operatorname{ch}_2(\operatorname{End}_0E_I)=-4c_2(E_I)$,
and $p_1(TS^4)=0$.  This is also the twisted calculation in
\cite[Section~10, pp.~459--460]{AHS78}, with chirality reversed to
match our ASD convention.

Choose parallel positive spinors $s_1,s_2$ forming a complex basis
on $\RR^4$.  The constant bundle isomorphisms
\[
 a\longmapsto(\rho(a)s_1,\rho(a)s_2),\qquad
 (f,v)\longmapsto((f+\rho(v))s_1,(f+\rho(v))s_2)
\]
identify the complexification of $Q_I$ with two copies of
$D^-_{I,\operatorname{End}_0E_I}$.  Indeed, the Clifford relation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-clifford-identification}
 D^-_{I,\operatorname{End}_0E_I}(\rho(a)s_j)
 =(d_I^*a+\rho(d_I^+a))s_j.
\end{equation}
Anti-self-dual two-forms act trivially on $S^+$.  The isomorphisms
preserve the relevant norms up to fixed constants.  Consequently
$Q_I$ is surjective and has real kernel dimension $2\cdot4=8$;
its kernel has the asserted decay.

It remains to identify this kernel with the framed tangent space.
Represent a framed infinitesimal ASD deformation $b$ by a family
in radial gauge at the pole.  Then $b_{\rm rad}=0$ there, and in
the exterior frame $b=O(|y|^{-3})$.  In particular $b\in L^2\cap
\dot H_I^1$.  Lax--Milgram on the scalar homogeneous space gives
a unique $u\in\dot H_I^1(\ad E_I)\subset L^4$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-based-coulomb}
 \int\langle d_Iu,d_Iv\rangle
 =\int\langle b,d_Iv\rangle\quad(v\in\dot H_I^1(\ad E_I)).
\end{equation}
We have $d_Iu\in L^2$ and $Q_I(d_Iu)=(d_I^*b,0)$.
Local regularity and~\eqref{eq:euclidean-compact-remainder}, transferred
to $Q_I$ and applied to expanding cutoffs of $d_Iu$, give
$d_Iu\in\dot H_I^1$: the additional cutoff term is at most
$CR^{-1}\|d_Iu\|_2$ and the interior remainder is uniformly bounded.
Hence $b-d_Iu\in\ker Q_I$.
Two radial-gauge representatives differ by a based infinitesimal
gauge vanishing near the pole, which~\eqref{eq:euclidean-based-coulomb}
removes.  If $b=d_Iu$, the radial components of $I$ and $b$ vanish
on exterior rays, so $u$ is constant along each such ray.
Its $L^4$ condition forces these constants to vanish.  It therefore
extends smoothly by zero at the pole and is a based infinitesimal
gauge.  The framed tangent map is injective; the dimension
$5+3=8$ from~\eqref{eq:framed-deformation-sequence} makes it an
isomorphism.

Finally, pairing the Dirac equation with its cokernel first determines
$c$, after which closed range gives a unique $\phi$.  Together with
the orthogonal complement for $Q_I$, this proves the assertion about
$P_0$.  A radial cutoff of the harmonic cokernel line provides
$\kappa_0$ equivariantly; invariant inner products define the complements.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Matching the metric on the bubble}

For use in the elementary coefficient estimates, normal coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-coordinate-metric}
 g_{ij}(z)=\delta_{ij}+O(|z|^2),\qquad
 \partial g_{ij}(z)=O(|z|),\qquad \partial^2g_{ij}(z)=O(1).
\end{equation}
In the exterior framing of the Euclidean instanton,
$|I(y)|\leq C|y|^{-3}$ and $|\nabla I(y)|\leq C|y|^{-4}$.
For its truncation outside radius $R\asymp\lambda^{-1/2}$, radial
integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:instanton-tail}
 \|I^{\mathrm{tr}}-I\|_{L^4}
 +\|\nabla(I^{\mathrm{tr}}-I)\|_{L^2}\leq C\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|I^{\mathrm{tr}}-I\|_{L^2}
 +\|I^{\mathrm{tr}}-I\|_{L^{2\sharp}}\leq C\sqrt\lambda.
\end{equation}

Let $\vartheta_{\rm met}$ be smooth, equal to one on $[0,1/2]$, and zero
on $[1,\infty)$.  Extend the rescaled physical metric to $\RR^4$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-induced-metric}
 h_{x,\lambda}(y)=\delta+
 \vartheta_{\rm met}(\sqrt\lambda|y|)(g_x(\lambda y)-\delta).
\end{equation}
It agrees with the rescaled physical metric wherever $\beta_I$ is supported.
The normal-coordinate bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-metric-bound}
 \|h_{x,\lambda}-\delta\|_\infty+
 \|\partial h_{x,\lambda}\|_4\leq C\lambda.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first term is $O(\lambda^2|y|^2)$ on a ball of radius
$\lambda^{-1/2}$.  The derivative is $O(\lambda^{3/2})$ on that
ball, whose volume to the power $1/4$ is $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$.

Identify forms and spinors for $h$ and $\delta$ by the positive
square root of the metric, preserving the three target summands.
Use the corresponding identification for $\kappa_0$.  On the fixed
Euclidean spaces, the resulting operator $\widehat B_h$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-operator-perturbation}
 \|\widehat B_h-\widehat B_0\|_{\dot H_I^1\oplus K_I\to L^2}
 \leq C\bigl(\|h-\delta\|_\infty+\|\partial h\|_4\bigr)
 \leq C\lambda.
\end{equation}
The principal coefficient multiplies $\nabla_Iu$, and the remaining
coefficient is bounded by $|\partial h|$ and multiplies $u\in L^4$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-induced-inverse}
 P_h=P_0\bigl(1+(\widehat B_h-\widehat B_0)P_0\bigr)^{-1}
\end{equation}
is a uniformly bounded right inverse with range in the fixed
complement used for $P_0$.  This perturbation argument does not
require $I$ to be ASD for $h$.

\subsection{Localized coefficients and matched obstruction spaces}
The remaining comparison terms are zero-order.  We repeatedly use the
following two consequences of~\eqref{eq:critical-norms}.  If $f=(v,\psi)$
is a target section supported in $B(x,r)$ and is pointwise bounded by $M$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shrinking-support}
 \|v\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}+\|\psi\|_{L^2}\leq CMr^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the $L^2$ bounds follow from volume, while the integral of
$\operatorname{dist}(z,\cdot)^{-2}$ over a four-ball is $O(r^2)$,
uniformly in $z$.

If instead $f$ is a bounded coefficient supported in the same ball, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supported-coefficient}
 \|f\|_{L^4}+\|f\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
 \leq Cr\|f\|_{L^\infty},
 \qquad
 \|fu\|_{L^2}+\|fu\|_{L^\sharp}
 \leq Cr\|f\|_{L^\infty}\|u\|_{L^2_1}.
\end{equation}
The first inequality follows by volume comparison and by splitting the
supremum in~\eqref{eq:critical-norms} according as its base point lies within
$2r$ of $x$; the second follows from~\eqref{eq:critical-products} and
$L^4\cdot L^4\subset L^2$.

The pregluing formulas have the exact support properties
\begin{align}\label{eq:pregluing-supports}
 \Phi_\lambda&=\Phi_0
 &&\text{outside }B(x,8\lambda^{1/3}),
 &\Phi_\lambda&=0
 &&\text{inside }B(x,4\lambda^{1/3}),\notag\\
 A_\lambda&=A_0
 &&\text{outside }B(x,4\sqrt\lambda).
\end{align}
Set $s_\lambda=\Phi_\lambda-\Phi_0$.  The compact background family and
\eqref{eq:supported-coefficient} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spinor-coefficient}
 \|s_\lambda\|_{L^4}+\|s_\lambda\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
 \leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
This controls the spinor coefficients in the gauge, curvature, and Dirac
rows, using $L^4$ for the ordinary rows and $L^{2\sharp}$ for the critical
curvature row.

In radial gauge, $|A_0-\Gamma|\leq C|z|$.  On
$\operatorname{supp}\beta_X$, where $|z|\geq N^{-1}\sqrt\lambda$, the
south-pole instanton decay gives
\[
 |I_\lambda(z)|\leq C\lambda^2|z|^{-3}
 \leq C N^3\sqrt\lambda.
\]
Thus $c_{x,\lambda}=\beta_X(A_\lambda-A_0)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-connection-coefficient}
 \|c_{x,\lambda}\|_{L^\infty}\leq C_N\sqrt\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|c_{x,\lambda}\|_{L^4}
 +\|c_{x,\lambda}\|_{L^{2\sharp}}\leq C_N\lambda.
\end{equation}
It contributes $O_N(\sqrt\lambda)$ to the three rows, including the Coulomb
row.  Finally, the fixed perturbing one-form in the bubble Dirac row is
supported by $\beta_I$ in a ball of radius $\tfrac12\sqrt\lambda$; ordinary
H\"older gives an $O(\sqrt\lambda)$ multiplier bound.  Consequently the
total localized coefficient error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-error}
 \|E_{\mathrm{spin}}+E^X_{\mathrm{conn}}+E^X_{\mathrm{gauge}}\|
 \leq C_N\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}

Choose the Hermitian metric on $\Xi$ so that $\iota_{s,q}$ is an
isometry in the ordinary target $L^2$ norm.  Choose a smooth compactly
supported negative-spinor profile $\kappa_0$ on the Euclidean charge-one
model with $L^2$ norm one and nonzero pairing with its Dirac cokernel.
The line of profiles varies equivariantly with the frames and represents
the same complex obstruction line $K_I$.  In oriented normal frames define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-embeddings}
 J_{s,\lambda}\xi=\beta_XT^Y_{X,\lambda}\iota_{s,q}\xi,
 \qquad
 J_{i,\lambda}c(z)=c\lambda^{-2}\kappa_0(z/\lambda),
\end{equation}
where $T^Y_{X,\lambda}$ is the background bundle identification.  For
sufficiently small scale the second support is inside the bubble core.
The metric identifications used in~\eqref{eq:euclidean-operator-perturbation}
are included in the second formula.
All formulas involving $K_I$ are interpreted equivariantly on local framed
covers, so no global trivialization of the framing line is required.

\begin{lemma}[obstruction embeddings]\label{lem:matched-obstruction}
The map $J_{s,\lambda}\oplus J_{i,\lambda}$ is injective with a uniformly
bounded inverse on its image.  Its ordinary $L^2$ projection is uniformly
bounded in the mixed target norm.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The removed background profile is smooth on a shrinking ball, and its
mixed norm tends to zero by~\eqref{eq:shrinking-support}.  Pure dilation
preserves the Euclidean $L^2$ norm of $\kappa_0$, while the physical metric
changes that norm by $o(1)$.  The two supports are disjoint for small
scale.  The Gram matrix therefore tends to the identity.  The background
profiles have bounded mixed norm; the bubble profile is entirely in the
$L^2$ Dirac row.  The projection formula and Cauchy--Schwarz prove the
second assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The complemented inverse}
Let $\cK_X(q)$ be the kernel of the literal augmented background operator
with $\iota_{s,q}$ fixed.  It has dimension $d_{\mathfrak s}+2r_N$ on a
sufficiently small disk, by openness of surjectivity.  The isomorphism $A_q$ after
\eqref{eq:background-derivative} identifies the background parameter
space with $\cK_X(q)$.

Normalize the parameter metric by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-metric}
 g_{\mathrm{par},\lambda}=g_{\overline N}+\lambda^{-2}g_X
 +(d\lambda/\lambda)^2+g_{\SO(3)}.
\end{equation}
The normalized bubble directions are $\lambda\partial_{x^j}$,
$\lambda\partial_\lambda$, and $\partial_{\theta^k}$.
For smooth corrections and auxiliary coefficients use the graph norm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graph-domain}
 \|(u,\eta_s,\eta_i)\|_{\mathrm{gr}}
 =\|u\|_{L^2_1}+\|\eta_s\|+\|\eta_i\|
 +\|\widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda(u,\eta_s,\eta_i)\|_{
 \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathbb X^{\mathrm{gr}}_{q_\lambda}$ for the completion.  This is
the domain on which the mixed-target operator is bounded; it is smaller
than the space of arbitrary $L^2_1$ corrections.

\begin{proposition}[uniform complemented inverse]\label{prop:uniform-inverse}
For $N$ sufficiently large and then $\lambda$ sufficiently small,
there are smooth equivariant synthesis and retraction maps
\begin{equation}\label{eq:synthesis}
 S_g:\cK_X(q)\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr}\longrightarrow
 \mathbb X^{\mathrm{gr}}_{q_\lambda},
 \qquad
 R_g:\mathbb X_{q_\lambda}\longrightarrow
 \cK_X(q)\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr},
\end{equation}
with uniformly bounded norms and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-matrix}
 M_g=R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}S_g=I+o(1),
 \qquad
 \Pi_g=S_gM_g^{-1}R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}.
\end{equation}
Here $S_g$ lifts the model kernel elements, while $R_g$ extracts their
parameter coordinates and ignores auxiliary obstruction coefficients.  With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complement-space}
 \mathbb H_g^{\mathrm{cmp}}
 =\ker(R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}),
\end{equation}
the augmented operator in~\eqref{eq:augmented-operator} restricts to an
isomorphism on the graph completion and on each fixed positive-scale
strong Fredholm level.  Its inverse $P_g$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-inverse-bound}
 \|P_gy\|_{\mathrm{gr}}\leq C\|y\|_{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}.
\end{equation}
The image of $S_g$ is the full augmented kernel.  Smoothness is
asserted at positive scale on strong Sobolev levels.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $T_\lambda v(z)=\lambda^{-1}v(z/\lambda)$ and
$U_\lambda f(z)=\lambda^{-2}f(z/\lambda)$, with the metric and bundle
identifications above understood.  The map $U_\lambda^{-1}\beta_I$
is uniformly bounded in ordinary $L^2$.  Domain synthesis
$v\mapsto\beta_I T_\lambda v$ is bounded from $\dot H_I^1$ to
physical $L^2_{1,A_\lambda}$.  For its differentiated cutoff term use
$\|d\beta_I\|_4\|T_\lambda v\|_4$; its undifferentiated $L^2$
term is bounded by $C\sqrt\lambda\|v\|_4$.
The truncated-connection difference is a small $L^4$ multiplier.
The critical estimate needed below also holds on the Euclidean model:
the translated Hardy inequality and Kato's inequality give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-hardy}
 \sup_z\int_{\RR^4}|z-y|^{-2}|v(y)|^2\,dy
 \leq C\|\nabla_Iv\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
It is preserved by $T_\lambda$.

Let $P_X$ be the ordinary $L^2$ inverse of the literal augmented
background operator on a smooth complement.  It is uniformly bounded
over the small compact background disk by openness of surjectivity.
For target $y$, set
\begin{align*}
 (u_X,\xi_X)&=P_X(T^Y_{X,\lambda})^{-1}(\beta_Xy),\\
 (v_I,c_I)&=P_hU_\lambda^{-1}(\beta_Iy),
\end{align*}
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-euclidean-parametrix}
 \mathcal Q_\lambda y=(\beta_XT_{X,\lambda}u_X+
                    \beta_IT_\lambda v_I,\xi_X,c_I).
\end{equation}
Restrictions are extended by zero across regions on which the cutoff
vanishes.  Applying the augmented operator cancels the obstruction
terms exactly: the background embedding contains $\beta_X$, and the
bubble profile is supported where $\beta_I=1$.  The principal terms
sum to $\beta_X^2y+\beta_I^2y=y$.  There is no principal-symbol
remainder on the bubble because $h$ agrees there with the rescaled
physical metric.

For the curvature commutator, use~\eqref{eq:euclidean-hardy} and
the $L^{2\sharp}$ bound for $d\beta_I$.  Ordinary rows use the
$L^4$ bound for $d\beta_I$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:commutator} gives the
same estimates for the background term.  The comparison coefficients
on the background are bounded by~\eqref{eq:coefficient-error}.
On the bubble support the spinor is zero.  The difference between the
full and truncated instanton is supported at physical radius
comparable to $\sqrt\lambda$ and is $O(\sqrt\lambda)$ there;
its $L^4$ and $L^{2\sharp}$ norms are $O(\lambda)$.
The fixed smooth Dirac coefficients contribute $O(\sqrt\lambda)$.
Thus all remaining errors are zero-order, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-parametrix-error}
 \widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda\mathcal Q_\lambda=1+E_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|E_\lambda\|_{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y\to\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}
 \leq C(\log N)^{-1/2}+C_N\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
Fixing $N$ large and then $\lambda$ small gives a right inverse
$P^r_\lambda=\mathcal Q_\lambda(1+E_\lambda)^{-1}$.
Its ordinary domain bound and the equation
$\widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda P^r_\lambda=1$ give the uniform graph
bound.  Smooth target data give smooth solutions by fixed-scale
elliptic regularity.  Density therefore extends the construction
to the target and graph completions specified above.

We next choose the complement using bounded integrals of configurations.
The configuration projection of $\cK_X(q)$ is injective, since
$\iota_{s,q}$ is injective.  Its configuration $L^2$ Gram matrix
defines a left inverse $r_X$; put
$R_Xu=r_X(\beta_XT_{X,\lambda}^{-1}u)$.
For $K=\ker Q_I$, choose a smooth compactly supported radial cutoff
$\chi_0$ so that
\[
 G(k,l)=\int_{\RR^4}\chi_0\langle k,l\rangle
\]
is positive definite on $K$.  Such a cutoff exists because
$K\subset L^2$ is finite dimensional.  Define $R_Iu$ by pairing
$T_\lambda^{-1}u$ with $\chi_0K$ and applying $G^{-1}$.
The compact dual profiles belong to $L^{4/3}$, so H\"older gives
$\|R_Iu\|\leq C\|u\|_4$, uniformly in scale.  Thus
$R_g=(R_X,R_I)$ is bounded on the configuration domain and ignores
all auxiliary coefficients.

Define preliminary synthesis $S_g^0$ by
$(v,\xi)\mapsto(\beta_XT_{X,\lambda}v,\xi,0)$ on $\cK_X(q)$
and $k\mapsto(\beta_IT_\lambda k,0,0)$ on $K$.
The background diagonal block of $R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}S_g^0$
tends to the identity, while the bubble block is exactly the identity
once the dual support lies where $\beta_I=1$.
The off-diagonal blocks tend to zero: a dilated bubble mode has
physical $L^2$ norm $O(\lambda)$, and the rescaled restriction of a
smooth background mode on the fixed dual support is $O(\lambda)$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminary-kernel-matrix}
 R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}S_g^0=1+o(1).
\end{equation}

Also $\widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda S_g^0=o(1)$ in the mixed target
for fixed $N$.  For background modes, the cutoff commutator is
$O_N(\sqrt\lambda)$ by smoothness and shrinking support, and the
comparison coefficients tend to zero.  For bubble modes the commutator
is supported at rescaled radii comparable to $\lambda^{-1/2}$.
Their value and derivative decay in
Proposition~\ref{prop:euclidean-fredholm} give an $O_N(\lambda)$
bound in both target norms.  The metric principal error is bounded by
$C\lambda\|\nabla_Ik\|_{L^2\cap L^\sharp}$, which is finite by that
decay.  The remaining metric coefficients have $L^4$ and
$L^{2\sharp}$ norms $O(\lambda)$.  These critical derivative estimates
are used only on the finite-dimensional smooth mode space.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-kernel-synthesis}
 S_g=(1-P^r_\lambda\widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda)S_g^0.
\end{equation}
Then $\widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda S_g=0$, and
$M_g=R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}S_g=1+o(1)$.
To see that $S_g$ spans the entire kernel, use the compact elliptic
index at any fixed positive scale.  Adding one instanton charge
changes the real ASD index by $+8$ and the real twisted Dirac index
by $-2$.  The added complex stabilization contributes $+2$.
The augmented index therefore increases by eight, and surjectivity
identifies it with the kernel dimension.  It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complemented-euclidean-inverse}
 P_g=(1-S_gM_g^{-1}R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X})P^r_\lambda
\end{equation}
is a right inverse with image in $\mathbb H_g^{\rm cmp}$.
Every kernel vector is $S_gv$ and has retraction $M_gv$;
this proves injectivity on that complement and the uniform bound.

Radial cutoffs and invariant metrics make the choices equivariant
under the compact frame and residual-circle actions.  At positive
scale the smooth elliptic family argument gives smooth dependence
on strong Sobolev levels.  This does not give bounds for derivatives
as $\lambda$ tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform parameter derivatives}
We first specify local identifications independently of the inverse.
Near $g_0=(q_0,x_0,\lambda_0,\rho_0)$ use ordinary background coordinates
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-parameter-charts}
 x=\exp_{x_0}(\lambda_0\xi),\qquad
 \lambda=\lambda_0e^s,\qquad \rho=\rho_0\exp\theta.
\end{equation}
Restrict these coordinates to a sufficiently small ball of fixed radius.
They are uniformly comparable with~\eqref{eq:parameter-metric}, with
bounded connection coefficients for second derivatives.  In the following
lemma, $\partial_p^\alpha$ denotes a mixed coordinate derivative in
these charts.  Norms on intersections are sum norms.

\begin{lemma}[physical bundle identifications]\label{lem:parameter-transport}
There are smooth unitary identifications of the gluing bundles over each
chart~\eqref{eq:normalized-parameter-charts}, covering the identity of $X$,
such that, for $1\leq|\alpha|\leq2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-coefficients}
 \|\partial_p^\alpha A_g\|_{L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}}
 +\|\partial_p^\alpha\Phi_g\|_{L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}}\leq C.
\end{equation}
In these identifications the first two derivatives of $J_{s,g}$ and
$J_{i,g}$ are uniformly bounded into the mixed target, and those of
$R_g$ are uniformly bounded from $L^2_{1,A_g}$ to
$\cK_X(q)\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr}$.  All constants are independent of
$g_0$ after fixing $N$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use a regular inner frame for the instanton and the exterior frame from
its framing pole.  The relative frame parameter is placed in their
transition function, so the regular instanton expression on the core is
independent of that parameter.  Local $\SU(2)$ lifts give the corresponding
spinor identifications.  Choose a fixed overlap annulus about $x_0$ of
radius $r_0=\sqrt{\lambda_0}$ and fixed radius ratio; its constants may
depend on $N$.  The original gluing formulas can be expressed on this
cover because their connection cutoff radii are comparable throughout
the parameter chart.

Let $t_g$ be the transition function.  On this annulus its quotient with
$t_{g_0}$ is uniformly close to the identity when the chart radius is
small.  The angular instanton transition is scale independent, normalized
center displacement divided by annulus radius is $O(\lambda_0/r_0)$,
and the remaining frames vary over compact smooth families.  Take the
Lie-group logarithm of this quotient.  Extend it to a slightly larger
annulus, multiply it by a fixed radial cutoff equal to one on the overlap,
and exponentiate.  The resulting change of inner frame makes the
transition equal to $t_{g_0}$ and is the identity toward the core.
Leave the outer frame fixed.  This identifies the bundles over $\id_X$.
The logarithm, its first two parameter derivatives, and their first
spatial derivatives are bounded respectively by $C$ and $C/r_0$.
Thus extra connection coefficients and two parameter derivatives are
$O(r_0^{-1})$ on an annulus of fixed radius ratio.  Their
$L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}$ norms are bounded by~\eqref{eq:supported-coefficient}.
These estimates also hold for the transition multipliers between
overlapping parameter charts, so the local identifications are compatible
with the configuration and target norms.

For completeness, the regular charge-one potential has components
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-instanton-coefficients}
 (I_\lambda)_\mu(z)
 =\sum_\nu C_{\mu\nu}\frac{z^\nu}{\lambda^2+|z|^2},
\end{equation}
where the fixed Lie-algebra coefficients $C_{\mu\nu}$ encode the chosen
chirality and normalization.  Its leading angular pure-gauge term is
scale independent.  Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:instanton-parameter-envelope}
 |\partial_p^\alpha I_\lambda(z)|
 \leq C\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda+|z|)^2},\qquad 1\leq|\alpha|\leq2,
\end{equation}
for center and scale derivatives in flat coordinates.  For example,
$\lambda\partial_\lambda$ replaces the numerator in
\eqref{eq:regular-instanton-coefficients} by
$-2\lambda^2C_{\mu\nu}z^\nu$ and squares the denominator;
$\lambda\partial_{x^j}=-\lambda\partial_{z^j}$.  Repeated derivatives
satisfy the same bound.  The profile on the right has bounded
$L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}$ norm: after dilation the weighted integral is
\[
 \sup_z\int_{\RR^4}|z-y|^{-2}(1+|y|)^{-4}\,dy<\infty.
\]
This follows by splitting into the regions near $0$, near $z$, and their
complement, or by radial monotonicity and evaluation at $z=0$.

Derivatives of the normal-coordinate and orthonormal-frame coefficients
gain a factor $\lambda$.  Multiplied by the regular potential
$O((\lambda+|z|)^{-1})$ on a fixed ball of radius at most one, they
are also bounded by~\eqref{eq:instanton-parameter-envelope}.
Derivatives of connection cutoffs give $O(r_0^{-1})$ terms on annuli
of fixed radius ratio.  Exterior instanton coefficients have the better
decay $O(\lambda^2|z|^{-3})$.  The background family is smooth and compact.
These observations prove the connection part
of~\eqref{eq:parameter-coefficients}, including mixed derivatives.

The spinor is zero on the connection-gluing annulus.  At its outer cutoff
radius $\lambda^{1/3}$, normalized scale derivatives of the cutoff are
bounded pointwise, while center derivatives gain
$\lambda/\lambda^{1/3}$.  The smooth background and frame coefficients
therefore give uniformly bounded spinor derivatives.  The same reasoning
applies to the smooth background obstruction profiles.  Derivatives of
the bubble embedding are $\lambda^{-2}$ times smooth compactly supported
profiles in $z/\lambda$, with bounded $L^2$ norm.  They remain entirely
in the negative-spinor row, since these bundle identifications preserve
the fixed physical metric and the target splitting.

Finally, write the compact dual profiles defining $R_I$ as $w_a$,
including the inverse of their Gram matrix.  In flat physical coordinates
its coefficients are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-dual-retraction}
 (R_Iu)_a=\int\langle u(z),
       \lambda^{-3}w_a((z-x)/\lambda)\rangle\,dz.
\end{equation}
Center and scale differentiation act on the dual profile by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-profile-derivatives}
 w\longmapsto-\partial_jw,\qquad
 w\longmapsto-3w-y\cdot\partial_yw,
\end{equation}
respectively.  After two derivatives the weights still have bounded
$L^{4/3}$ norm, so H\"older gives the asserted bound without
differentiating $u$.  The metric, volume-density, and bundle factors in
the actual formula have two bounded normalized derivatives on the fixed
rescaled support.  For $R_X$, use the compact smooth background Gram
inverse and its $L^2$ dual profiles; the normalized derivatives of
$\beta_X$ are pointwise bounded.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

Put $\widetilde S_g=S_gM_g^{-1}$; this is the unique kernel lift
whose configuration retraction is the identity.

\begin{proposition}[uniform differentiated inverse]\label{prop:uniform-derivatives}
In the physical bundle identifications of
Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport}, the graph completions form locally
a common Banach space with uniformly equivalent norms.  The operators
$P_g$, $R_g$, $\widetilde S_g$, and $\Pi_g$ have uniformly bounded first
and second derivatives with respect to~\eqref{eq:parameter-metric}.
For maps with values in the augmented configuration space these are
graph-norm bounds.  The operators remain equivariant and are smooth on
strong Sobolev levels at positive scale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Abbreviate $\widehat{\mathscr D}_{q_g}$ by $B_g$ and write
$W=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y$ and
$\cK=\cK_X(q)\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr}$.  Use smooth local orthonormal
frames for the finite-dimensional bundles.  Because the metric and
principal symbols are fixed, every parameter derivative of $B_g$ is
zero-order in the configuration variable.  Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport}
and the critical product estimates give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator-parameter-bound}
 \|(\partial_p^\alpha B_g)(u,\eta)\|_W
 \leq C(\|u\|_{L^2_{1,A_g}}+|\eta|),\qquad 1\leq|\alpha|\leq2.
\end{equation}
Use $L^4\cdot L^4\subset L^2$ in the ordinary rows and
$L^{2\sharp}\cdot L^{2\sharp}\subset L^\sharp$ in the curvature row.
No $L^\sharp$ bound for an arbitrary $\nabla u$ is required.

Integrating~\eqref{eq:parameter-coefficients} along a parameter segment
bounds $\|A_g-A_{g_0}\|_4$ by $C|p-p_0|$.  Kato's inequality and
Sobolev embedding then compare $L^2_{1,A_g}$ and $L^2_{1,A_{g_0}}$
uniformly in both directions.  Integrating
\eqref{eq:operator-parameter-bound} similarly bounds $B_g-B_{g_0}$
from the first-order augmented space to $W$.
The graph norms are therefore uniformly equivalent on their common
smooth domain.  Taking smooth completions identifies them with one
Banach space $D$; this identification was obtained without using an inverse.

Consider the square operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-augmented-family}
 F_g:D\longrightarrow W\oplus\cK,\qquad
 F_gh=(B_gh,R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}h).
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse} gives its uniformly bounded inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-family-inverse}
 L_g(y,k)=P_gy+\widetilde S_gk.
\end{equation}
The coefficient and dual-profile formulas show that $F_g$ is $C^2$ in
operator norm on these fixed Banach spaces, with two uniform derivatives.
The inverse identity gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:inverse-parameter-derivatives}
 \partial_iL&=-L(\partial_iF)L,\notag\\
 \partial_j\partial_iL
 &=L(\partial_jF)L(\partial_iF)L
  +L(\partial_iF)L(\partial_jF)L
  -L(\partial_j\partial_iF)L.
\end{align}
All factors are uniformly bounded between the specified spaces.
This proves the bounds for $P_g$ and $\widetilde S_g$, and hence for
$\Pi_g=\widetilde S_gR_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}$.
The bounds for $R_g$ were proved in the lemma.  Conversion to covariant
second derivatives uses the uniformly bounded connection coefficients of
\eqref{eq:normalized-parameter-charts}.  Equivariance follows from the
uniqueness characterizations and the equivariant undifferentiated
construction.  Positive-scale strong smoothness is the ordinary elliptic
family assertion already established above.
\end{proof}

The normalized synthesis has the same image as $S_g$ and changes its
finite-dimensional basis by $M_g^{-1}=I+o(1)$, of positive determinant.
The original synthesis remains useful for comparison with the ordered
model modes.  No derivative bound for its particular raw-parametrix
formula is needed.  The estimates above concern the first-order graph
norms; they do not give uniform bounds in unrescaled higher Sobolev norms.

\subsection{Comparison with orthogonal projection}
The orientation argument will use orthogonal projection of cutoff kernel
fields.  Bubble fields have physical $L^2$ norm of order $\lambda$;
comparison in the unweighted configuration norm would not suffice.
Write $T_g=S_g^0$ for the preliminary synthesis above, and put
\begin{align}\label{eq:weak-physical-norms}
 \|(u,\xi_s,\xi_i)\|_{\mathcal H_\lambda}^2
 &=\|u\|_2^2+|\xi_s|^2+\lambda^2|\xi_i|^2,\notag\\
 |(v,k)|_\lambda^2&=|v|^2+\lambda^2|k|^2
 \quad(v\in\cK_X(q),\ k\in\cK_I^{\rm fr}).
\end{align}
Here $|v|$ includes the auxiliary coordinates of the background kernel.

\begin{proposition}[weak inverse and projected kernel]
\label{prop:projected-kernel}
With $N$ fixed as in Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse}, the inverse
$P_g$ extends to $L^{4/3}$ in all three target rows and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-glued-inverse}
 \|P_gf\|_{\mathcal H_\lambda}
 +\|\nabla_{A_g}\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}P_gf\|_{4/3}
 \leq C_N\|f\|_{4/3}.
\end{equation}
Let $\pi_g$ be orthogonal projection onto $\ker B_g$ in
$\mathcal H_\lambda$, and set $\mathcal L_g=(1-P_gB_g)T_g$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-kernel-comparison}
 \mathcal L_g^{-1}\pi_gT_g=1+O_N(\sqrt\lambda)
\end{equation}
in the weighted parameter norm.  In particular $\pi_gT_g$, $\mathcal L_g$, and
$S_g$ are kernel isomorphisms with the same orientation.  The conclusion
also holds for $\pi_gT'_g$ if
$\|T'_g-T_g\|_{|\cdot|_\lambda\to\mathcal H_\lambda}=o(1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First consider the Euclidean completion
\[
 \dot W_I^{1,4/3}
 =\overline{C_c^\infty}^{\,\|\nabla_I(\cdot)\|_{4/3}}
 \hookrightarrow L^2.
\]
The embedding follows from Kato's inequality and scalar Sobolev.
The exterior argument in Proposition~\ref{prop:euclidean-fredholm}
applies at this exponent: the flat Calder\'on--Zygmund estimate bounds
$\|\partial v\|_{4/3}$ by $C\|D_0v\|_{4/3}$, and the term
$\|I\|_4\|v\|_2$ is absorbed outside a fixed large ball.  Interior
elliptic estimates give a compact remainder
$\|v\|_{L^{4/3}(B_{2R})}$.  Thus the kernel is finite dimensional and
the range is closed.  The kernel consists of $L^2$ harmonic fields,
and the annihilator of the range consists of harmonic fields in the
dual space $L^4$.  Both spaces compactify and extend across the pole
by the conformal and cutoff calculation in that proposition.
The same compact kernel computation therefore applies.  In particular
$\widehat B_0$ is surjective at exponent $4/3$, with the same real
eight-dimensional kernel.

One can use the same complement at both exponents.  For each kernel
field $k$ its homogeneous orthogonality functional is
\[
 \int\langle\nabla_Iv,\nabla_Ik\rangle
 =\int\langle v,\nabla_I^*\nabla_Ik\rangle.
\]
The coefficient on the right is smooth and in $L^2$: the Dirac-square
identity makes it curvature times $k$, of order $r^{-7}$ at infinity.
The functionals extend to $L^2$ and their matrix on the kernel is its
positive homogeneous Gram matrix.  For compactly supported smooth
target data, either inverse is harmonic off a compact set.
Conformal removability gives $r^{-3}$ decay there, so each solution
belongs to both homogeneous domains.  Complemented uniqueness makes
the inverses equal.  Density in $L^2\cap L^{4/3}$ extends this equality
to common data.  The metric perturbation in
\eqref{eq:euclidean-operator-perturbation} has a principal coefficient
of size $O(\lambda)$ in $L^\infty$ and a remaining coefficient of
size $O(\lambda)$ in $L^4$.  They act on $\nabla v\in L^{4/3}$
and $v\in L^2$, respectively.  Its Neumann inverse consequently
has the same agreement and bound at exponent $4/3$.

The physical scaling is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-dilations}
 \|T_\lambda v\|_2=\lambda\|v\|_2,\quad
 \|\nabla T_\lambda v\|_{4/3}=\lambda\|\nabla v\|_{4/3},\quad
 \|U_\lambda f\|_{4/3}=\lambda\|f\|_{4/3}.
\end{equation}
Smooth finite-dimensional complements also give a background inverse
from $L^{4/3}$ to $W^{1,4/3}$ plus its auxiliary space.  Repeat
\eqref{eq:raw-euclidean-parametrix} in $L^{4/3}$ in every target row.
The principal symbols still match, and the finite columns cancel
exactly.  Cutoff derivatives and connection errors are $L^4$
multipliers on $L^2$ configurations, so the error is bounded by
\[
 C\|d\beta_X\|_4+C\|d\beta_I\|_4+C_N\lambda^{1/3}<\tfrac12.
\]
This gives a raw inverse $P_g^r$ with the bound in
\eqref{eq:weak-glued-inverse}.  The weight on the bubble auxiliary
coordinate follows from~\eqref{eq:weak-dilations}.

The preliminary modes satisfy the sharper error estimates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-preliminary-errors}
 \|B_gT_g(v,0)\|_{4/3}\leq C_N\lambda|v|,
 \qquad
 \|B_gT_g(0,k)\|_{4/3}\leq C_N\lambda^{3/2}|k|.
\end{equation}
For the first estimate, a smooth background mode times a cutoff
derivative on radius $r\asymp\sqrt\lambda$ has norm $O(r^2)$.
The removed bounded spinor coefficients have support radius
$\lambda^{1/3}$ and contribute $O((\lambda^{1/3})^3)=O(\lambda)$.
The finite column cancels the cut-off background equation.
For the second estimate, a bubble mode is
$O(\lambda^2r^{-3})$ on the cutoff annulus.  Its product with
$d\beta_I$ is bounded on volume $O_N(\lambda^2)$, giving
$O_N(\lambda^{3/2})$.  The principal metric error contributes
$O(\lambda)O(\lambda)$ by~\eqref{eq:weak-dilations}; the remaining
connection and metric coefficients have $L^4$ norm $O(\lambda)$
and multiply a field of $L^2$ norm $O(\lambda)$.
The bubble modes have no spinor component, and the preglued spinor
vanishes on their support.

The map $T_g$ has uniform upper and lower bounds from
$|\cdot|_\lambda$ to $\mathcal H_\lambda$.  Its background Gram
matrix tends to the positive augmented background Gram matrix.
After dividing bubble fields by $\lambda$, their Gram matrix tends
to the positive $L^2$ Gram matrix of the Euclidean kernel.  The
cross terms tend to zero: the $L^1$ norm of
$\lambda^{-1}T_\lambda k$ on the support is $O_N(\lambda^{3/2})$,
apart from the smaller $O(\lambda^2)$ core contribution.

The retraction $R_g$ is bounded from configuration $L^2$ to the
weighted parameter norm.  Its bubble dual has $L^2$ norm
$O(\lambda^{-1})$, canceled by the weight on that row.
Moreover $M_g^0=R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}T_g=1+o(1)$
also in this norm.  The background diagonal error is $O_N(\lambda^2)$;
the bubble diagonal is the identity; the bubble row on a background
column vanishes because $\beta_X=0$ on the compact dual support.
The remaining cross term is $O_N(\lambda^{5/2})$, by integrating
$\lambda^2r^{-3}$ on the overlap.

Put $S_g^r=(1-P_g^rB_g)T_g$.  The weak raw inverse and
\eqref{eq:weak-preliminary-errors} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-kernel-lift-error}
 \|(S_g^r-T_g)(v,k)\|_{\mathcal H_\lambda}
 \leq C_N(\lambda|v|+\lambda^{3/2}|k|).
\end{equation}
Its retraction matrix is invertible.  At fixed positive scale, weak
kernel elements are smooth by elliptic regularity; surjectivity and
index therefore imply that $S_g^r$ spans the full kernel.
Subtract its kernel projection from $P_g^r$ to obtain a right inverse
in $\ker(R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X})$.  It retains the weak
bound, including the derivative bound since the cutoff kernel modes
have uniformly bounded $W^{1,4/3}$ norm relative to
$|\cdot|_\lambda$.  On smooth data this is $P_g$ by uniqueness
in the stated complement.  This proves~\eqref{eq:weak-glued-inverse}.

The same estimate~\eqref{eq:weak-kernel-lift-error} holds for $\mathcal L_g$,
and $R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}\mathcal L_g=M_g^0$ exactly.  Its
orientation agrees with $S_g$, since their retraction matrices both
have positive determinant.  Finally $\pi_g\mathcal L_g=\mathcal L_g$ and
$\|\pi_g\|=1$, whence
\[
 \|(\pi_gT_g-\mathcal L_g)(v,k)\|_{\mathcal H_\lambda}
 \leq C_N\sqrt\lambda\,|(v,k)|_\lambda.
\]
The lower bound for $T_g$ and the lift error give a uniform lower
bound for $\mathcal L_g$.  This proves~\eqref{eq:projected-kernel-comparison}
and its assertion for $T'_g$.
\end{proof}

In the remaining sections write
$\mathbb H^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{\lambda,N}=\mathbb H_g^{\mathrm{cmp}}$ and
$P_{\lambda,N}=P_g$ after fixing the cutoff parameter $N$.

In particular, by Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse}, a unit vector in the
complement cannot have image tending to zero in the mixed target.  This
conclusion follows from~\eqref{eq:uniform-inverse-bound} without a
separate assumption excluding neck-supported approximate kernels.

\section{Projected residual and nonlinear correction}\label{sec:correction}

\subsection{The projected splicing error}
Put $r=\sqrt\lambda$ and $R=\lambda^{1/3}$.  For small $\lambda$ the
pregluing formula divides $X$ into the core $B(x,r/4)$; the inner, flat, and
middle annuli with radii $(r/4,r/2)$, $(r/2,2r)$, and $(2r,4r)$; the
zero-spinor plateau $(4r,4R)$; the outer spinor-cutoff annulus $(4R,8R)$; and
the exterior of $B(x,8R)$.  The logarithmic parametrix cutoffs lie inside the
$r$-scale annuli but play no role in this residual decomposition.

\begin{proposition}[projected residual]\label{prop:residual}
Uniformly in the compact background family, the center, and the frames,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 \|\mathfrak S(q_\lambda)-J_{s,\lambda}b(q)\|_{L^{\sharp,2;2}}
 \leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
Here $L^{\sharp,2;2}$ means $L^{\sharp,2}$ in the curvature component and
$L^2$ in the negative-spinor component.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the exterior, the preglued pair equals the background pair and
$J_{s,\lambda}$ equals the background obstruction embedding; the residual
therefore vanishes identically.

Write $\mathfrak S(q)=(v_0,\psi_0)$ and let $\chi_{\mathrm{out}}$ be the outer
spinor cutoff.  On $4R<d(x,\cdot)<8R$ the pair is
$(A_0,\chi_{\mathrm{out}}\Phi_0)$ and
$J_{s,\lambda}b(q)=\mathfrak S(q)$.  If $\mathcal Q(\Phi)$ denotes the
quadratic spinor term in the curvature equation, the residual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outer-residual}
 \bigl(\mathcal Q(\Phi_0)-\mathcal Q(\chi_{\mathrm{out}}\Phi_0),
 (\chi_{\mathrm{out}}-1)\psi_0
 +\rho(d\chi_{\mathrm{out}})\Phi_0\bigr).
\end{equation}
The first component and the first term in the second component are uniformly
bounded on a ball of radius $8R$, and hence have mixed-target norm $O(R^2)$
by~\eqref{eq:shrinking-support}.  Direct radial integration gives
$\|d\chi_{\mathrm{out}}\|_{L^2}\leq CR$; consequently the last term in
\eqref{eq:outer-residual} is $O(R)=O(\lambda^{1/3})$.

On the plateau $4r<d(x,\cdot)<4R$ the pair is $(A_0,0)$, so its Dirac
component is zero.  Subtracting the matched background residual gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plateau-residual}
 \mathfrak S(A_0,0)-\mathfrak S(A_0,\Phi_0)
 =\bigl(\mathcal Q(\Phi_0),-\psi_0\bigr),
\end{equation}
with the sign in $\mathcal Q$ fixed by the convention in
\eqref{eq:outer-residual}.  Both entries are uniformly bounded and supported
in $B(x,4R)$, so their mixed-target norm is $O(R^2)=O(\lambda^{2/3})$.

On the middle annulus $2r<d(x,\cdot)<4r$, write the connection in radial
gauge as $\Gamma+\chi_{\mathrm{mid}}(A_0-\Gamma)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:middle-curvature}
 F=\chi_{\mathrm{mid}}F_{A_0}
   +d\chi_{\mathrm{mid}}\wedge(A_0-\Gamma)
   +(\chi_{\mathrm{mid}}^2-\chi_{\mathrm{mid}})
    (A_0-\Gamma)\wedge(A_0-\Gamma).
\end{equation}
The bounds $|A_0-\Gamma|\leq Cr$ and
$|d\chi_{\mathrm{mid}}|\leq C/r$ show that
\eqref{eq:middle-curvature} is uniformly bounded.  The spinor is zero and the
matched background target is uniformly bounded, so
\eqref{eq:shrinking-support} gives an $O(r^2)=O(\lambda)$ contribution.  On
the flat annulus $r/2<d(x,\cdot)<2r$ the preglued pair is $(\Gamma,0)$; the
only term after subtraction is again a uniformly bounded matched background
target, and its contribution is $O(\lambda)$.

On the inner annulus $r/4<d(x,\cdot)<r/2$, the instanton decay gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inner-decay}
 |F_I|\leq C\lambda^2d(x,\cdot)^{-4}\leq C,
 \qquad
 |A_I|\leq C\lambda^2d(x,\cdot)^{-3}\leq C\sqrt\lambda.
\end{equation}
Since the cutoff derivative is $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$, every curvature term in
the truncated connection and every subtracted background term is uniformly
bounded.  This annulus therefore also contributes $O(\lambda)$.

Finally, on $B(x,r/4)$ the model instanton is anti-self-dual for the Euclidean
metric $\delta$, but not identically for the physical metric $g$.  Its
self-dual curvature is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-metric-error}
 F_I^{+,g}=\tfrac12(*_g-*_\delta)F_I.
\end{equation}
The normal-coordinate estimate $|g-\delta|\leq Cd(x,\cdot)$, conformal
invariance of the four-dimensional curvature norm, and radial integration of
the standard instanton give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-metric-bound}
 \|F_I^{+,g}\|_{L^{\sharp,2}(B(x,r/4))}\leq C\sqrt\lambda.
\end{equation}
The matched background target on the core contributes $O(r^2)=O(\lambda)$.
Adding the seven disjoint regional estimates gives
\[
 C\bigl(\lambda^{1/3}+\lambda^{2/3}
       +\lambda+\lambda^{1/2}\bigr)
 \leq C\lambda^{1/3},
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:residual}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Solving the augmented equation}
Write $h=(u,\eta_s,\eta_i)$ and let $P_\lambda$ be the inverse in
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse}.  Expanding~\eqref{eq:augmented-map} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlinear-expansion}
 \widehat\cF_\lambda(h)=r_\lambda+
 \widehat{\mathscr D}_\lambda h+Q_\lambda(u,u),
\end{equation}
where $r_\lambda$ is the residual in Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}.
Sobolev multiplication gives the scale-independent estimates
\begin{align}\label{eq:quadratic-estimates}
 \|Q_\lambda(u,u)\|_{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}
 &\leq C\|u\|_{L^2_1}^2,\notag\\
 \|Q_\lambda(u,u)-Q_\lambda(v,v)\|_{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}
 &\leq C(\|u\|_{L^2_1}+\|v\|_{L^2_1})\|u-v\|_{L^2_1}.
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[small correction]\label{prop:correction}
For sufficiently small $\lambda$, there is a unique
$h_\lambda=(u_\lambda,\eta_{s,\lambda},\eta_{i,\lambda})$ in a fixed small
ball in $\mathbb H^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{\lambda,N}$ satisfying
$\widehat\cF_\lambda(h_\lambda)=0$.  It obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correction}
 \|u_\lambda\|_{L^2_1}+\|\eta_{s,\lambda}\|
 +\|\eta_{i,\lambda}\|\leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
It is smooth in all positive-scale gluing parameters.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the ball of radius $2C\|r_\lambda\|$, use the map
\[
 h\longmapsto-P_\lambda\bigl(r_\lambda+Q_\lambda(u,u)\bigr).
\]
Propositions~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse} and~\ref{prop:residual}, together with
\eqref{eq:quadratic-estimates} show that it preserves this ball and has
Lipschitz constant less than $1/2$ after decreasing the scale.  Banach's
fixed-point theorem gives existence, uniqueness, and~\eqref{eq:correction}.
Any solution in a fixed sufficiently small graph-norm ball satisfies
$\|h\|_{\mathrm{gr}}\leq C\|r_\lambda\|+
C\|h\|_{\mathrm{gr}}^2$ and therefore lies in the contraction ball.
This proves the stated uniqueness.
At a fixed positive scale, local elliptic regularity makes the solution
smooth.  For the initial critical step, take small coordinate balls on
which the $L^4$ norm of $u$ is small, absorb its multiplication operator
in the local first-order $W^{1,p}$ estimate for some $p>2$, and then
bootstrap the quadratic equation.  All other coefficients and the
finite-dimensional source profiles are smooth at this fixed scale.
The linearized equation is invertible on the complement by the same
small-operator estimate as in the contraction; elliptic regularity
gives its inverse on the strong levels.  The implicit-function theorem
now gives smooth dependence for $\lambda>0$.
\end{proof}

Define the corrected gluing map and obstruction section by
\begin{align}\label{eq:corrected-map-section}
 \gamma(g)&=[q_\lambda+u_\lambda],\notag\\
 \chi(g)&=(b(q)+\eta_{s,\lambda},\eta_{i,\lambda}).
\end{align}
Then $\gamma(g)$ is a genuine monopole exactly when $\chi(g)=0$.

\subsection{Continuity at zero scale}
The constants above are independent of a moving center.  If
$g_n\to g_0$ with $\lambda_n\to0$, then~\eqref{eq:correction} gives
$u_{\lambda_n}\to0$ in the global $L^2_1$ norm.  On compact subsets away from
the limiting center, the preglued pairs converge in every Sobolev norm to
the background.  After rescaling at the centers, their connections converge
to the framed instanton while their spinors converge to zero.  For the
corrected pairs, local elliptic estimates upgrade the $L^2_1$ convergence
away from the center and on fixed rescaled balls: in these regions
the coefficients are uniformly smooth, and the difference of the
augmented source from the limiting source tends to zero smoothly.
Moreover, writing $u_\lambda=(a_\lambda,\phi_\lambda)$, we have
\[
 \|F_{A_\lambda+a_\lambda}-F_{A_\lambda}\|_2
 \leq C\bigl(\|a_\lambda\|_{L^2_{1,A_\lambda}}+
             \|a_\lambda\|_{L^2_{1,A_\lambda}}^2\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the correction does not change the limiting curvature measure.
These properties are precisely Uhlenbeck
convergence to $(q,x)$.  Thus $\gamma$ extends continuously by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-scale-map}
 \gamma(q,x,0)=(q,x).
\end{equation}
The same estimates and the definitions in~\eqref{eq:matched-embeddings}
show that the norm of $\chi$ is continuous when the instanton line is
collapsed at the cone vertex.

\subsection{Derivatives of the residual and correction}
\begin{proposition}[differentiated correction]
\label{prop:correction-derivatives}
Use the physical bundle identifications from
Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport}.  For $1\leq|\alpha|\leq2$, the
residual and correction satisfy
\begin{align}\label{eq:residual-parameter-derivatives}
 \|\partial_p^\alpha r_g\|_{\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y}
 &\leq C\lambda^{1/3},\\
 \|\partial_p^\alpha h_g\|_{\mathrm{gr},g}
 +\|\partial_p^\alpha u_g\|_{\mathrm{crit},g}
 &\leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
 \label{eq:correction-parameter-derivatives}
\end{align}
Here the graph norm is~\eqref{eq:graph-domain}, and the critical
configuration norm is defined in~\eqref{eq:critical-configuration-norm}.
The constants are uniform over the compact parameter family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We differentiate the regional formulas in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}.  For a radial cutoff at radius
$s=\lambda^\nu$, where $\nu=1/2$ or $1/3$, its first two normalized
parameter derivatives have bounded amplitude, and their first spatial
derivatives have amplitude at most $C/s$.  Logarithmic scale derivatives
differentiate a fixed profile.  A normalized center derivative gains
$\lambda/s\leq1$.  Use slightly enlarged annuli for these bounds;
no characteristic function of a moving region is differentiated.
All background coefficients and their two parameter derivatives are
bounded on the compact smooth family.

In~\eqref{eq:outer-residual}, the differentiated Dirac cutoff term has
amplitude $C/R$ on a set of volume $O(R^4)$, where $R=\lambda^{1/3}$.
Its $L^2$ norm is therefore $O(R)$.  Every other outer or plateau term,
including its two derivatives, is bounded on $B(x,8R)$ and contributes
$O(R^2)$ to the mixed target.  The exterior contribution remains zero:
differentiate the exact identity $\mathfrak S(q)=\iota_qb(q)$, retaining
all derivatives of the embedding and of $b$.

Put $r_0=\sqrt\lambda$.  On the middle annulus, the radial background
potential and its normalized derivatives are $O(r_0)$; center
derivatives may add an $O(\lambda)$ term, which is smaller.
The cutoff differentials are $O(r_0^{-1})$.
Thus~\eqref{eq:middle-curvature} and its two derivatives have bounded
coefficients.  On the inner annulus, normalized derivatives of the
exterior instanton potential have the same
$O(\lambda^2r_0^{-3})=O(r_0)$ bound, and the curvature and cutoff
curvature terms have bound $O(\lambda^2r_0^{-4})=O(1)$.
The matched background target and its derivatives are also bounded,
including derivatives of its cutoff.  These annuli and the flat annulus
therefore contribute $O(r_0^2)$.

On the core write~\eqref{eq:core-metric-error} as
$\mathfrak m_gF_{I_\lambda}$, where
$\mathfrak m_g=\tfrac12(*_g-*_\delta)$ in the chosen identifications.
The normal-coordinate Taylor formulas, expressed in the fixed physical
target, give
\[
 |\partial_p^\alpha\mathfrak m_g|\leq Cr_0
 \quad\text{on }B(x,Cr_0),\qquad 0\leq|\alpha|\leq2.
\]
Indeed the undifferentiated metric discrepancy vanishes at the center;
normalized center derivatives add a factor $\lambda$, and changes of
normal frames have bounded coefficients.  The regular-gauge rational
curvature formula gives
\[
 |\partial_p^\alpha F_{I_\lambda}(z)|
 \leq C\frac{\lambda^2}{(\lambda+|z|)^4},\qquad |\alpha|\leq2.
\]
This envelope has uniformly bounded $L^2$ and $L^\sharp$ norms.
Leibniz's rule thus gives $O(r_0)$ for the differentiated core error.
On the bundle-transition annulus, first use gauge covariance: the
residual transforms as a target section by a unitary multiplier with
bounded first two parameter derivatives.  Its potentially large spatial
derivatives cancel before any estimate.  The spinor is zero there.
Summing $O(R)+O(R^2)+O(r_0^2)+O(r_0)$ proves
\eqref{eq:residual-parameter-derivatives}.

Use the common graph domain and square operator $F_g$ from
\eqref{eq:square-augmented-family}.  The correction satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-nonlinear-correction}
 F_gh_g+(Q_g(u_g,u_g),0)+(r_g,0)=0.
\end{equation}
The quadratic maps $Q_g$ have uniformly bounded bilinear norms
$L^2_1\times L^2_1\to\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y$, including two parameter
derivatives: their coefficients are smooth metric and Clifford
contractions in the fixed physical bundle charts.  The derivative of
\eqref{eq:square-nonlinear-correction} in $h$ is a small perturbation
of $F_g$, with uniformly bounded inverse into the graph domain.
Differentiate once.  The residual derivative is $O(\lambda^{1/3})$,
and the other inhomogeneous terms contain $h_g$ or two factors of $u_g$.
The inverse gives the same bound for $\partial_p h_g$.
Differentiating a second time uses the two bounded operator derivatives
and the first correction derivative just obtained, so all inhomogeneous
terms are again $O(\lambda^{1/3})$.

For the critical upgrade, differentiate the exact zero Coulomb row:
\begin{align}\label{eq:correction-critical-divergences}
 d_g^{0,*}\partial_i u_g
 &=-(\partial_i d_g^{0,*})u_g,\notag\\
 d_g^{0,*}\partial_{ij}u_g
 &=-(\partial_{ij}d_g^{0,*})u_g
  -(\partial_i d_g^{0,*})\partial_j u_g
  -(\partial_j d_g^{0,*})\partial_i u_g.
\end{align}
The differentiated gauge rows are zero-order critical multipliers by
\eqref{eq:parameter-coefficients}.  The right sides therefore have
$L^{\sharp,2}$ norm $O(\lambda^{1/3})$.
Difference quotients converge in the first-order space and their
divergences converge in $L^{\sharp,2}$, which proves the assertion in
the smooth critical completion.
\end{proof}

\section{Parameter recovery and the compactified embedding}
\label{sec:embedding}

\subsection{Critical gauge estimates}
Use the full determinant-one gauge group of pairs.  At a preglued pair
$q_g=(A_g,\Phi_g)$ impose the Coulomb condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:based-slice}
 d_g^{0,*}\bigl((A,\Phi)-q_g\bigr)=0.
\end{equation}
No value at a point is prescribed for this ambient gauge transformation.
The relative instanton frame remains a separate gluing parameter.
Put $\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}\zeta=\zeta\Phi_g$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-laplacian}
 \Delta_g^0=d_g^{0,*}d_g^0
 =d_{A_g}^*d_{A_g}+\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}^*\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}.
\end{equation}
For configurations and infinitesimal gauges, respectively, use
\begin{align}\label{eq:critical-configuration-norm}
 \|c\|_{\mathrm{crit},g}
 &=\|c\|_{L^2_1}+\|d_g^{0,*}c\|_{L^{\sharp,2}},\\
 \|\zeta\|_{2,\sharp,g}
 &=\|\Delta_g^0\zeta\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}+\|\zeta\|_{L^2}.
 \label{eq:critical-gauge-norm}
\end{align}
Denote these smooth completions by $\mathbb E_g^1$ and $\mathbb E_g^0$,
respectively.  On the horizontal slice the
configuration norm is exactly its $L^2_1$ norm.  For arbitrary corrections,
$L^2_1$ control only puts the divergence in $L^2$, which does not suffice
for the critical Green-operator argument.  The critical configuration
norm is the pair analogue of the norm in
\cite[Theorem~8.2]{FeehanCritical}.

\begin{lemma}[gauge and scalar spectral gaps]\label{lem:pair-spectral-gap}
For the compact preglued family with sufficiently small background disk and
scale bound, there is a uniform $C$ such that
\[
 \|\zeta\|_{L^2}^2\leq C\bigl(
\|d_{A_g}\zeta\|_{L^2}^2+\|\zeta\Phi_g\|_{L^2}^2\bigr).
\]
The same uniform estimate holds for real functions $f$, in the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-pair-gap}
 \|f\|_2^2\leq C\bigl(\|df\|_2^2+
                         \||\Phi_g|f\|_2^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The compact background family has a positive lower bound for its spinor
$L^2$ norm and a finite upper bound for its $C^0$ norm.
The spinor cutoff removes a term of $L^2$ norm $O(\lambda^{2/3})$,
so, for small scale,
\[
 \inf_g\|\Phi_g\|_2=m_\Phi>0,\qquad
 \sup_g\|\Phi_g\|_\infty=M_\Phi<\infty.
\]
For a real function $f$, let $\bar f$ be its average.
The scalar Poincar\'e inequality gives
\[
 m_\Phi|\bar f|
 \leq\||\Phi_g|f\|_2+M_\Phi\|f-\bar f\|_2
 \leq\||\Phi_g|f\|_2+C\|df\|_2.
\]
Together with $\|f\|_2\leq\|f-\bar f\|_2+
\operatorname{vol}(X)^{1/2}|\bar f|$, this proves
\eqref{eq:scalar-pair-gap}.
For a skew-Hermitian trace-free matrix in the rank-two factor,
$\zeta^*\zeta=\tfrac12|\zeta|_{\rm HS}^2\,1$.
Thus $|\zeta\Phi_g|^2=\tfrac12|\zeta|_{\rm HS}^2|\Phi_g|^2$.
Apply the scalar estimate to $|\zeta|_{\rm HS}$ and use Kato's
inequality to obtain the gauge estimate.  All bundle norm conventions
differ from this one by fixed constants.
\end{proof}

Let $G_g=(\Delta_g^0)^{-1}$, initially on smooth sections, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-projection}
 H_g=1-d_g^0G_gd_g^{0,*},\qquad
 \cC_g^{\mathrm{sl}}=\ker R_g\cap\ker d_g^{0,*}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[critical pair Green estimates]\label{prop:pair-green}
The operator $G_g$ extends from $L^{\sharp,2}$ to $\mathbb E_g^0$ and
satisfies the uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-pair-green}
 \|G_gf\|_{\mathbb E_g^0}+\|G_gf\|_{C^0}
 +\|G_gf\|_{L^2_{2,A_g}}
 +\|\nabla_{A_g}G_gf\|_{L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}}
 \leq C\|f\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}.
\end{equation}
The map $H_g$ is a uniformly bounded projection on $\mathbb E_g^1$
with range $\ker d_g^{0,*}$.  In the physical bundle identifications
of Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport}, both graph completions have
uniformly equivalent local norms, and $G_g$ and $H_g$ have uniformly
bounded first and second normalized parameter derivatives between these
spaces.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first record the coefficient estimates used in this proof.  The fixed
instanton energy, compact background family, and cutoff formulas give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-family-data}
 \|F_{A_g}\|_2+\|\Phi_g\|_\infty+
 \|\nabla_{A_g}\Phi_g\|_2\leq C.
\end{equation}
For $a_\alpha=\partial_p^\alpha A_g$ and
$\phi_\alpha=\partial_p^\alpha\Phi_g$ in the charts
of~\eqref{eq:normalized-parameter-charts}, one also has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatial-parameter-coefficients}
 \|\nabla_{A_g}a_\alpha\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}
 +\|\phi_\alpha\|_\infty+\|\nabla_{A_g}\phi_\alpha\|_2
 \leq C,\qquad 1\leq|\alpha|\leq2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, one spatial derivative of the rational formula
\eqref{eq:regular-instanton-coefficients} bounds
$\nabla_{A_g}a_\alpha$ by $C\lambda/(\lambda+|z|)^3$.
This profile has bounded $L^2$ and $L^\sharp$ norms; the corresponding
radial integrals are
$\int_0^\infty t^3(1+t)^{-6}\,dt=1/20$ and
$\int_0^\infty t(1+t)^{-3}\,dt=1/2$.
On the fixed-ratio transition annulus, the logarithms used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport} have two spatial derivatives
bounded by $C/r_0^2$, also after two parameter derivatives.
The resulting connection terms are $O(r_0^{-2})$ on that annulus.
Their $L^{\sharp,2}$ norms are bounded
by~\eqref{eq:shrinking-support}.  The additional commutators use the
critical product estimates.  Finally, the spinor vanishes on the
connection-gluing region, and its outer cutoff and two normalized
derivatives have spatial derivative $O(\lambda^{-1/3})$ on a ball of
radius $C\lambda^{1/3}$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:gauge-family-data}--\eqref{eq:spatial-parameter-coefficients}.

Let $\zeta=G_gf$ for smooth $f$.  The pointwise identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-green-identity}
 \tfrac12d^*d|\zeta|^2+|\nabla_{A_g}\zeta|^2+
 |\zeta\Phi_g|^2=\langle f,\zeta\rangle.
\end{equation}
The positive Green kernel of $d^*d+1$ is uniformly comparable with
$\operatorname{dist}(x,y)^{-2}$
\cite[Lemma~5.1]{FeehanCritical}.  Apply it
to~\eqref{eq:pair-green-identity} and discard the nonnegative spinor
term.  The argument of \cite[Lemma~5.3]{FeehanCritical} gives
\[
 \|\nabla_{A_g}\zeta\|_{L^{2\sharp}}^2+\|\zeta\|_\infty^2
 \leq C\|\zeta\|_\infty
       \bigl(\|f\|_{L^\sharp}+\|\zeta\|_{L^\sharp}\bigr).
\]
Using
$\|\zeta\|_{L^\sharp}\leq C\|\zeta\|_4
 \leq C\|\zeta\|_2^{1/2}\|\zeta\|_\infty^{1/2}$
and Young's inequality, then Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-spectral-gap}, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-green-continuous}
 \|\zeta\|_\infty+\|\nabla_{A_g}\zeta\|_{L^{2\sharp}}
 +\|\zeta\|_2\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}.
\end{equation}
Put $V_g=\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}^*\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}$.
Since $V_g$ is uniformly bounded, the equation
$\nabla_{A_g}^*\nabla_{A_g}\zeta=f-V_g\zeta$ has right side bounded
in $L^{\sharp,2}$ by~\eqref{eq:pair-green-continuous}.
The estimates of \cite[Lemmas~5.4--5.5]{FeehanCritical}, whose constants
depend on the curvature only through its $L^2$ norm, give the
$L^2_{2,A_g}$ and $L^4$ derivative bounds.
The gauge graph bound follows from the equation and the spectral gap.
Smooth approximation proves~\eqref{eq:critical-pair-green} on the stated
completions, and also gives these embeddings for every
$\zeta\in\mathbb E_g^0$.

In the fixed physical bundle charts, a parameter derivative of
$\Delta_g^0$ is a sum of contractions of
$a_\alpha\nabla_{A_g}\zeta$, $(\nabla_{A_g}a_\alpha)\zeta$,
and $(\partial_p^\alpha V_g)\zeta$.
Second derivatives also contain products $a_\alpha a_\beta\zeta$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:parameter-coefficients},
\eqref{eq:spatial-parameter-coefficients}, and
\eqref{eq:critical-pair-green} therefore imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-laplacian-derivatives}
 \|(\partial_p^\alpha\Delta_g^0)\zeta\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}
 \leq C\|\zeta\|_{\mathbb E_g^0},\qquad 1\leq|\alpha|\leq2.
\end{equation}
Expand $\Delta_g^0-\Delta_{g_0}^0$ using $\nabla_{A_{g_0}}$.
Integrating the coefficient bounds controls this difference by
$C|p-p_0|$ from $\mathbb E_{g_0}^0$ to $L^{\sharp,2}$.
For a sufficiently small fixed chart radius the smooth gauge graph
norms are thus equivalent in both directions.  The configuration graph
norms are likewise equivalent, since $d_g^{0,*}-d_{g_0}^{0,*}$
is zero-order with bounded critical multiplier coefficients.
Taking smooth completions gives common local Banach spaces.

We may now apply the inverse differentiation identities
\eqref{eq:inverse-parameter-derivatives} to $\Delta_g^0$ and $G_g$.
They give the two uniform derivatives of $G_g$.
Moreover,
\[
 d_g^0:\mathbb E_g^0\longrightarrow\mathbb E_g^1,\qquad
 d_g^{0,*}:\mathbb E_g^1\longrightarrow L^{\sharp,2}
\]
are uniformly bounded with two parameter derivatives.
For the first map use $\nabla_{A_g}^2\zeta$ in $L^2$,
$\zeta$ in $C^0$, and $\nabla_{A_g}\Phi_g$ in $L^2$;
its divergence is $\Delta_g^0\zeta$.
Derivatives of this map use
\eqref{eq:spatial-parameter-coefficients} and the same products,
including in the critical divergence.
Derivatives of the second map are zero-order.
The formula~\eqref{eq:horizontal-projection} now proves the claims for
$H_g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[stability of the horizontal projection]
\label{lem:horizontal-stability}
Let $c=(a,\phi)$ have sufficiently small critical norm
$\epsilon=\|c\|_{\mathrm{crit},g}$ at $q_g$.  The pair Laplacian of
$q_g+c$ is invertible on the same gauge graph completion, with uniform
bound.  Its horizontal projection $H_{q_g+c}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-stability}
 \|H_{q_g+c}-H_g\|_{\mathbb E_g^1\to\mathbb E_g^1}\leq C\epsilon.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The difference of the pair Laplacians is a sum of contractions of
\[
 a\nabla_{A_g}\zeta,\quad (d_{A_g}^*a)\zeta,\quad
 a^2\zeta,\quad \Phi_g\phi\zeta,\quad\phi^2\zeta.
\]
Here $d_{A_g}^*a=d_g^{0,*}c+\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}^*\phi$ has
$L^{\sharp,2}$ norm at most $C\epsilon$.
Use $\zeta$ in $C^0$, $\nabla_{A_g}\zeta$ in
$L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}$, and the critical product bounds.  This gives
\[
 \|\Delta_{q_g+c}^0-\Delta_g^0\|_{
       \mathbb E_g^0\to L^{\sharp,2}}
 \leq C(\epsilon+\epsilon^2).
\]
A Neumann series therefore gives $G_{q_g+c}$ on the same smooth
graph completion, with $G_{q_g+c}-G_g=O(\epsilon)$.
Also $d_{q_g+c}^{0,*}-d_g^{0,*}$ is a zero-order operator of norm
$O(\epsilon)$ from $\mathbb E_g^1$ to $L^{\sharp,2}$.
For $d_{q_g+c}^0-d_g^0$, the first-order configuration norm uses
$\nabla_{A_g}a,\nabla_{A_g}\phi$ in $L^2$ and $\zeta$ in $C^0$.
Its critical divergence uses $d_{A_g}^*a$, $a\nabla_{A_g}\zeta$, and
$\Phi_g\phi\zeta$, so this difference has norm $O(\epsilon)$ from
$\mathbb E_g^0$ to $\mathbb E_g^1$.
Insert these bounds in
$H_{q_g+c}=1-d_{q_g+c}^0G_{q_g+c}d_{q_g+c}^{0,*}$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:horizontal-stability}.  All estimates pass to the specified
smooth completions.  In particular no pointwise bound for the perturbed
spinor is required.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A uniform slice for the full gauge group}

\begin{proposition}[uniform critical pair slice]\label{prop:pair-slice}
There are positive radii $\epsilon_{\rm sl},r_{\rm sl}$, independent
of small scale and datum, such that every
$q_g+c$ with $\|c\|_{\mathbb E_g^1}<\epsilon_{\rm sl}$
is gauge equivalent to a unique
$q_g+h$ with
\[
 d_g^{0,*}h=0,\qquad \|h\|_{\mathbb E_g^1}<r_{\rm sl}.
\]
The gauge transformation is unique for the full determinant-one gauge
group.  Its logarithm and the slice representative depend smoothly on
$c$, with uniformly bounded first two derivatives; this includes two
normalized parameter derivatives in the physical bundle charts.
The slice is a local chart for the critical configuration quotient.
At positive scale the maps are smooth on the corresponding strong
Sobolev levels.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For endomorphisms of the rank-two factor, use the auxiliary graph norm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endomorphism-gauge-norm}
 \|v\|_{{\rm end},g}
 =\|\nabla_{A_g}^*\nabla_{A_g}v\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}+\|v\|_2.
\end{equation}
Its smooth completion embeds in $C^0\cap L^2_{2,A_g}$ and has
$\nabla_{A_g}v\in L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}$, with uniform bounds,
by~\eqref{eq:gauge-family-data} and
\cite[Lemmas~5.3--5.5]{FeehanCritical}.
The Laplacian product identity and the critical product inequalities
make this a unital Banach algebra with a uniform multiplication constant.
Its determinant-one unitary elements form the critical gauge group used
below.
On skew-Hermitian trace-free sections its norm is uniformly equivalent to
$\mathbb E_g^0$, because $V_g$ is bounded.
The exponential and local logarithm are therefore smooth, with uniform
derivative bounds on fixed small balls; compare
\cite[Lemmas~7.2--7.3]{FeehanCritical}.

Use the action convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-gauge-action}
 u\cdot(A+a,\Phi+\phi)
 =\bigl(A+u^{-1}au+u^{-1}d_Au,\ u^{-1}(\Phi+\phi)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $h\in\ker d_g^{0,*}$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-slice-map}
 \Psi_g(\zeta,h)=e^\zeta\cdot(q_g+h)-q_g.
\end{equation}
Its derivative at the origin is $(\zeta,h)\mapsto d_g^0\zeta+h$,
with uniformly bounded inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-slice-linear-inverse}
 c\longmapsto(G_gd_g^{0,*}c,H_gc).
\end{equation}
We verify the nonlinear estimates in these same norms.
Spatial differentiation of the connection terms in
\eqref{eq:pair-gauge-action} uses the $L^2_2$ bounds on $u=e^\zeta$,
the $L^4$ bounds on $d_{A_g}u$ and $a$, and the $L^2_1$ bound on $a$.
Their divergence consists of conjugates of $d_{A_g}^*a$ and
$\nabla_{A_g}^*\nabla_{A_g}u$, and contractions
$d_{A_g}u\cdot a$ and $d_{A_g}u\cdot d_{A_g}u$.
Here
$d_{A_g}^*a=d_g^{0,*}h+\mathfrak r_{\Phi_g}^*\phi$.
Every term is bounded in $L^{\sharp,2}$ by the critical products.
The spinor terms use the bounded background spinor and its $L^2$
derivative; their contribution to the divergence is zero-order.
Differentiating these multiplication maps gives a uniform $C^2$ bound
on a fixed small ball and
\[
 \|D\Psi_g(\zeta,h)-D\Psi_g(0,0)\|
 \leq C\bigl(\|\zeta\|_{\mathbb E_g^0}+\|h\|_{\mathbb E_g^1}\bigr).
\]
Parameter derivatives differentiate the coefficients $d_{A_g}$,
$\Phi_g$, and $d_g^{0,*}$ and are controlled by
\eqref{eq:spatial-parameter-coefficients}.
The horizontal domains have compatible local identifications:
$H_gH_{g_0}+(1-H_g)(1-H_{g_0})$ is invertible on a small fixed
parameter chart and maps $\operatorname{ran}H_{g_0}$ to
$\operatorname{ran}H_g$.
The quantitative inverse-function theorem applied to
\eqref{eq:pair-slice-linear-inverse} gives a uniform local
diffeomorphism, whose image contains a fixed ball in $\mathbb E_g^1$.
The inverse and its first two derivatives, including normalized
parameter derivatives, are uniformly bounded.

It remains to exclude identifications by gauges outside this exponential
chart.  Suppose $q_g+c_i$, $c_i=(a_i,\phi_i)$, have
$\|c_i\|_{\mathbb E_g^1}<r$ and are related by any determinant-one
gauge transformation $u$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-gauge-action} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-gauge-comparison}
 d_{A_g}u=u a_2-a_1u,\qquad
 (u-1)\Phi_g=\phi_1-u\phi_2.
\end{equation}
Unitarity gives
\[
 \|d_{A_g}u\|_{L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}}+\|d_{A_g}u\|_2
 +\|(u-1)\Phi_g\|_2\leq Cr.
\]
For a determinant-one unitary matrix of rank two,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:su2-displacement}
 (u-1)^*(u-1)=\tfrac12|u-1|_{\rm HS}^2\,1.
\end{equation}
Apply the scalar gap~\eqref{eq:scalar-pair-gap} to $|u-1|_{\rm HS}$
and use Kato's inequality.  The spinor term is a fixed multiple of
$\|(u-1)\Phi_g\|_2$, so $\|u-1\|_2\leq Cr$.
Differentiating the first equation in~\eqref{eq:full-gauge-comparison}
expresses the covariant Laplacian of $u$ as
$u\,d_{A_g}^*a_2-(d_{A_g}^*a_1)u$ and contractions of $d_{A_g}u$
with $a_i$.  The critical configuration norms control $d_{A_g}^*a_i$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-gauge-smallness}
 \|u-1\|_{{\rm end},g}\leq C(r+r^2).
\end{equation}
Thus $u-1$ is small in $C^0$ and in the Banach algebra norm.
Its logarithm satisfies $\|\log u\|_{\mathbb E_g^0}\leq Cr$.
The argument holds first for strong gauges and then, by the same weak
product identities, for the critical gauge group.

If both $c_i$ are horizontal, choose $r_{\rm sl}$ so small that
$(\log u,c_1)$ and $(0,c_2)$ lie in the injectivity domain of
$\Psi_g$.  Their images agree; hence $u=1$ and $c_1=c_2$.
Choose $\epsilon_{\rm sl}$ smaller so that the local inverse sends
its ball into this slice ball.  This proves full-group uniqueness and
freeness there.  The group translates of the local product chart give
the asserted quotient chart.  For strong inputs at positive scale,
regularity follows by the gauge-equation bootstrap of
\cite[Lemma~8.1]{FeehanCritical}: the additional spinor terms are
smooth zeroth-order terms, and the small $L^4$ norm of $d_{A_g}u$
permits the same elliptic absorption.  The strong smoothness assertion
then follows from the inverse-function theorem at those levels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Framed Coulomb modes and pregluing derivatives}
We first make the Euclidean parameter modes explicit.  Identify
$\RR^4$ with $\mathbb H$, put $r=|y|$ and $a(y)=y/r$, and use
imaginary quaternion units with $[\tau_i,\tau_j]=2\epsilon_{ijk}\tau_k$.
The regular and exterior expressions for the unit ASD instanton are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quaternion-instanton}
 I_{\rm reg}=\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\bar y\,dy)}{1+r^2},\qquad
 I_{\rm ext}=aI_{\rm reg}a^{-1}-da\,a^{-1}
 =-\frac{\operatorname{Im}(dy\,\bar y)}{r^2(1+r^2)}.
\end{equation}
Both expressions are in radial gauge.  Choose the relative-frame
convention in which $\rho$ acts on the exterior expression by
$\rho^{-1}I_{\rm ext}\rho$.

\begin{lemma}[explicit framed Coulomb modes]\label{lem:framed-coulomb-modes}
For $\theta\in\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H$, define the adjoint section
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic-frame-gauge}
 h_\theta^{\rm ext}=\frac{r^2}{1+r^2}\theta,\qquad
 h_\theta^{\rm reg}=\frac{\bar y\theta y}{1+r^2}.
\end{equation}
It is smooth, covariantly harmonic, and tends to $\theta$ in the
exterior frame.  The normalized translation, dilation, and relative-frame
directions have Coulomb representatives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-coulomb-modes}
 k_j=-\iota_{e_j}F_I,\qquad k_s=-\iota_yF_I,\qquad
 k_\theta=d_Ih_\theta.
\end{equation}
These modes form a basis of $\cK_I^{\rm fr}$.
Their values are $O(r^{-3})$ and their covariant derivatives are
$O(r^{-4})$ in the exterior frame.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The regular expression in~\eqref{eq:harmonic-frame-gauge} is smooth
at zero.  The exterior connection satisfies
$\sum y^\mu I_\mu=0$ and $\sum\partial_\mu I_\mu=0$.
The imaginary component rows of $dy\,\bar y$ are orthogonal with
squared length $r^2$.  Since
$\sum_i[\tau_i,[\tau_i,\theta]]=-8\theta$, it follows that
\[
 \sum_\mu[I_\mu,[I_\mu,\theta]]
 =-\frac{8}{r^2(1+r^2)^2}\theta.
\]
For a radial scalar function $f$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-adjoint-laplacian}
 \nabla_I^*\nabla_I(f\theta)
 =-\left(f''+\frac3r f'-\frac{8f}{r^2(1+r^2)^2}\right)\theta.
\end{equation}
Taking $f=r^2/(1+r^2)$, both terms in parentheses are
$8/(1+r^2)^3$, so the section is harmonic.  Its exterior difference
from $\theta$ is $O(r^{-2})$, giving the stated derivative decay.

Cartan's covariant formula and radial gauge give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-gauge-decomposition}
 -\partial_jI=k_j+d_I(-I_j),\qquad
 -I-y\cdot\partial I=k_s.
\end{equation}
The Yang--Mills equation and antisymmetry of $F_I$ imply
$d_I^*k_j=d_I^*k_s=0$.  Their self-dual derivatives vanish because
translation and dilation preserve the ASD equation.  For the frame modes,
$d_I^*k_\theta=0$ follows from harmonicity, and
$d_I^+k_\theta=[F_I^+,h_\theta]=0$.
To identify the frame sign, use a cutoff $\chi$ equal to zero toward
the origin and one on an inner--outer overlap.  On a fixed bundle the
derivative of the framed family is $d_I\sigma$, where
$\sigma_{\rm reg}=\chi a^{-1}\theta a$ and
$\sigma_{\rm ext}=\theta$.  Then
$d_I\sigma-k_\theta=d_I(\sigma-h_\theta)$.
The section $\sigma-h_\theta$ is smooth, is $O(r^{-2})$ at infinity,
and has derivative $O(r^{-3})$.  It belongs to the based homogeneous
scalar space in~\eqref{eq:euclidean-based-coulomb}, so this is exactly
the framed Coulomb representative.

The curvature density is $C(1+r^2)^{-4}$, with $C>0$.
Its derivative under translation $v$ and logarithmic dilation $s$ is
\[
 \frac{8C\langle v,y\rangle+4Cs(r^2-1)}{(1+r^2)^5}.
\]
Gauge variations do not change it, so a relation among the eight modes
first forces $v=s=0$.  If $d_Ih_\theta=0$, the section commutes with
all curvature values, which span $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, and hence
$\theta=0$.  Independence and Proposition~\ref{prop:euclidean-fredholm}
give the basis assertion.  The decay also follows directly from
\eqref{eq:quaternion-instanton}; translations have the better orders
$r^{-4}$ and $r^{-5}$.
\end{proof}

For background tangents use the pair Coulomb connection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-frame-connection}
 \Omega_q(v)=(d_q^{0,*}d_q^0)^{-1}d_q^{0,*}dq(v)
\end{equation}
and its induced connection on the gluing frame bundle, given by
evaluation at $x$.  These are smooth over compact background charts.
Split parameter tangents using this connection and the fixed geometric
connections on the center and orthonormal-frame bundles.  Thus at any
one parameter a background direction can be represented by a horizontal
variation of $q$ and a parallel relative frame.  This is a pointwise
choice of tangent representatives, not an assertion that the horizontal
distribution is integrable.
Let $\jmath_g:T_g\cG^+\to\cK_X(q)\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr}$ use
$A_q$ on these background directions and
\eqref{eq:explicit-coulomb-modes} on the normalized bubble directions.
This uses the same $A_q$ as before: the background kernel configurations
are horizontal, so their $L^2$ Gram retraction annihilates gauge
derivatives.  In particular the term in~\eqref{eq:background-derivative}
is still retained.  The map $\jmath_g$ has a uniformly bounded inverse.

\begin{proposition}[pregluing derivatives]\label{prop:pregluing-modulation}
The normalized splicing derivatives satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-retraction}
 R_gH_gdq_g=\jmath_g+o(1),
\end{equation}
with uniformly bounded inverse for $\jmath_g$.  In compatible local
trivializations the tubular map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tubular-map}
 \mathcal T(g,h)=[q_g+h],\qquad h\in\cC_g^{\mathrm{sl}},
\end{equation}
has uniform second-derivative bounds.  The tubular maps are
continuous in the critical configuration norm, smooth on positive-scale
strong Sobolev charts, and equivariant under the frame actions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $N$.  In Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport} take the transition
annulus inside the pure-instanton region, with outer radius less than
$r_0/(16N)$, where $r_0=\sqrt{\lambda_0}$.  Its radius ratios are
fixed.  After reducing the parameter chart it stays in that region;
the spinor is zero there.  All previous transport estimates remain valid.
For each unit normalized bubble direction $v$, we claim that a smooth
global infinitesimal gauge $\zeta_v$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pregluing-mode-comparison}
 dq_g(v)=d_g^0\zeta_v+\beta_IT_\lambda k_v+e_v,
 \qquad \|e_v\|_{\mathbb E_g^1}\leq C_N\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
We verify the critical divergence as well as the first-order norm.

For translation, take $\zeta_j=-\lambda(I_\lambda)_j$ in the
regular core and cut it off to zero at radius comparable to $r_0$,
before the outer boundary of the inner chart.
Equation~\eqref{eq:parameter-gauge-decomposition} gives the core identity.
On the cutoff annulus the gauge has size $O(\lambda/r_0)$; its first
two spatial derivatives have sizes $O(\lambda/r_0^2)$ and
$O(\lambda/r_0^3)$.  The normalized center derivative of the transition
logarithm is $O(\lambda/r_0)$ and gives the same connection bounds.
The remaining annular errors therefore have value
$O(\lambda/r_0^2)$ and covariant derivative
$O(\lambda/r_0^3)$.  Their first-order norm and critical divergence
are $O(\lambda/r_0)=O(\sqrt\lambda)$ by shrinking support.

For dilation, the angular transition is scale independent and the core
variation is already $T_\lambda k_s$.  At the connection cutoff the
exterior instanton and its normalized scale derivative have size
$O(\lambda^2/r_0^3)$, with spatial derivative
$O(\lambda^2/r_0^4)$.  Their annular errors have critical configuration
norm $O(\lambda^2/r_0^2)=O(\lambda)$.

For a frame direction, the transition is $a^{-1}\rho$.
The inner frame change identifying it with $a^{-1}$ differentiates to
$\sigma_{\rm reg}=\chi a^{-1}\theta a$, while
$\sigma_{\rm ext}=\theta$.  In the uncut instanton region put
$\zeta_\theta=\sigma-h_\theta(z/\lambda)$.
The potentially nonvanishing annular term cancels exactly:
\[
 d_I\sigma-d_I\zeta_\theta=T_\lambda k_\theta.
\]
The inner expression is smooth at the origin.  In the outer frame
$\zeta_\theta=\lambda^2(\lambda^2+r^2)^{-1}\theta$.
Cut off this expression to zero at radius comparable to $r_0$.
Across the connection cutoff, the unmatched terms have value
$O(\lambda^2/r_0^3)$ and spatial derivative
$O(\lambda^2/r_0^4)$, hence critical configuration norm $O(\lambda)$.
Background connection terms are smaller: in radial gauge they are
$O(r_0)$ and multiply $\zeta_\theta=O(\lambda^2/r_0^2)$.
The spinor vanishes on the support of this gauge.

These comparisons include the physical metric identifications.
On the core the metric error is $O(r^2)$ with derivative $O(r)$.
Using $r^2\leq C_N\lambda$, the mode decay, and
$\nabla_Ik\in L^2\cap L^\sharp$ bounds their first-order and
critical-divergence errors by $O_N(\lambda)$.
Normalized derivatives of the smooth coordinate and frame coefficients
give additional values bounded by $C\lambda/(\lambda+r)$, with
spatial derivative $C\lambda/(\lambda+r)^2$ on a fixed physical ball.
The latter profile has $L^2$ norm
$O(\lambda\sqrt{1+|\log\lambda|})$ and $L^\sharp$ norm
$O(\lambda(1+|\log\lambda|))$, both $o(\lambda^{1/3})$.
Radial monotonicity bounds the weighted supremum.
At the spinor cutoff radius $R=\lambda^{1/3}$, scale differentiation
gives value $O(1)$ and spatial derivative $O(R^{-1})$;
center differentiation gives $O(\lambda/R)$ and
$O(\lambda/R^2)$, respectively.  These give first-order errors
$O(R)$ and $O(\lambda)$; the spinor contribution to the gauge row
is zero-order.  This proves~\eqref{eq:pregluing-mode-comparison}.

For a background direction represented horizontally as above, put
$w=dq(v)$.  Its pregluing derivative satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-mode-comparison}
 dq_g(v)=\beta_XT_{X,g}w+e_v
       \pmod{\operatorname{im}d_g^0},\qquad
 \|e_v\|_{\mathbb E_g^1}\leq C_N\lambda^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the inner model is independent of the background.  With the
frame parallel for~\eqref{eq:background-frame-connection}, the derivative
of a background radial-gauge frame is zero at $x$ and $O(r)$ nearby.
Its cutoff extension gives a connection error $O(1)$ with spatial
derivative $O(r_0^{-1})$ on the $r_0$-annulus.
The removed smooth background modes have the same bounds, giving
$O(r_0)$ in the first-order norm and critical divergence.
The removed spinor gives $O(R)$ in the first-order norm and
$O(R^2)$ in the critical gauge row.  Outside these balls the variation
is exactly $w$.
Equivariance of pregluing, with the induced action on relative frames,
shows that changing the background representative adds a global gauge
derivative.  Without the induced frame connection a finite frame term
would remain, so the tangent convention is needed here.

The same smooth-mode and escaping-tail estimates used in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mode-divergences}
 \|d_g^{0,*}(\beta_IT_\lambda k)\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}=o(1),\qquad
 \|d_g^{0,*}(\beta_XT_{X,g}w)\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}=o(1).
\end{equation}
For bubble modes the cutoff gauge row is $O_N(\lambda)$ by the
$r^{-3}$ decay, and the metric terms have the bounds above.
For background modes the cutoff row is $O_N(\sqrt\lambda)$,
the comparison coefficients tend to zero, and $d_q^{0,*}w=0$.
Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-green} shows that $H_g$ changes these
cut-off modes by $o(1)$ in $\mathbb E_g^1$ and removes the exact
gauge terms.  The Gram retractions give
\[
 R_g(\beta_IT_\lambda k)=(0,k)+o(1),\qquad
 R_g(\beta_XT_{X,g}w)=(r_Xw,0)+o(1)=(A_qv,0)+o(1).
\]
The diagonal and off-diagonal estimates are those in
\eqref{eq:preliminary-kernel-matrix}.  This proves
\eqref{eq:parameter-retraction} uniformly over the compact data.

It remains to bound the tubular map in compatible quotient charts.
Put $\ell_g=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}\widetilde S_g$.
The identities $R_g\ell_g=1$ and $d_g^{0,*}\ell_g=0$ imply
\begin{align}\label{eq:horizontal-kernel-derivatives}
 d_g^{0,*}\partial_i\ell_g
 &=-(\partial_i d_g^{0,*})\ell_g,\notag\\
 d_g^{0,*}\partial_j\partial_i\ell_g
 &=-(\partial_j\partial_i d_g^{0,*})\ell_g
   -(\partial_i d_g^{0,*})\partial_j\ell_g
   -(\partial_j d_g^{0,*})\partial_i\ell_g.
\end{align}
The differentiated gauge rows are bounded critical multipliers on
$L^2_1$.  Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-derivatives},
these equations upgrade the two derivatives of $\ell_g$ to
$\mathbb E_g^1$ bounds.  Difference quotients converge there because
they converge in $L^2_1$ and their divergences converge in
$L^{\sharp,2}$ by these identities.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizontal-complement-projection}
 Q_g^{\rm sl}=H_g-\ell_gR_gH_g
\end{equation}
is a projection onto $\cC_g^{\rm sl}$ with two uniform derivatives.
The map
$U_g=Q_g^{\rm sl}Q_{g_0}^{\rm sl}+(1-Q_g^{\rm sl})(1-Q_{g_0}^{\rm sl})$ is invertible on a fixed small
chart and identifies the corresponding complements.

Equation~\eqref{eq:spatial-parameter-coefficients} also bounds the first
two derivatives of $q_g-q_{g_0}$ in $\mathbb E_{g_0}^1$:
the connection divergence is a contraction of $\nabla_{A_g}a_\alpha$,
and its spinor term is zero-order.  If $s_0$ is the slice-representative
map at $q_{g_0}$, the tubular map in that fixed slice is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-slice-tubular-map}
 (g,v)\longmapsto s_0(q_g-q_{g_0}+U_gv),\qquad
 v\in\cC_{g_0}^{\rm sl}.
\end{equation}
Its argument stays in a fixed slice ball for small fixed parameter
and complement radii.  The chain rule and
Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-slice} prove the uniform second-derivative
bound.  Positive-scale strong smoothness follows from the same
constructions and elliptic regularity.  The definitions give continuity
and equivariance.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform parameter recovery}
Local parameter charts are taken in a slightly larger background disk
and scale interval; their artificial outer boundaries are fixed only
after the following local construction.

\begin{lemma}[uniform modulation]\label{lem:modulation}
There are constants
$c_{\mathrm{mod}},r_{\mathrm{mod}}>0$ independent of small scale such that
\eqref{eq:tubular-map} is one-to-one for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulation-tube}
 d_{\mathrm{par}}(g,g')<c_{\mathrm{mod}},\qquad
 \|h\|_{L^2_1}+\|h'\|_{L^2_1}<r_{\mathrm{mod}}.
\end{equation}
Its image contains a uniform neighborhood in the critical topology.  The
recovered parameter is characterized, in the Coulomb representative, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulation-characterization}
 R_g((A,\Phi)-q_g)=0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The differential along the zero section is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulation-differential}
 D\mathcal T_{(g,0)}(\dot g,\dot h)=H_gdq_g(\dot g)+\dot h.
\end{equation}
Apply $R_g$ and use~\eqref{eq:parameter-retraction} to recover $\dot g$;
then recover $\dot h$ by subtraction.  This gives a uniformly bounded
inverse.  Proposition~\ref{prop:pregluing-modulation} and the slice estimates allow the
quantitative inverse-function theorem with a uniform radius.  On the
horizontal complement the critical and $L^2_1$ norms agree, which gives
\eqref{eq:modulation-tube}.
\end{proof}

The correction in Proposition~\ref{prop:correction} is horizontal and lies
in $\ker R_g$.  Hence modulation recovers its input parameter exactly.

\subsection{Global recovery of center, scale, and frame}
For a framed charge-one ASD connection on $\RR^4$, the model center
and scale are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-center-scale}
 z[A]=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_{\RR^4}y\,|F_A|^2,
 \qquad
 \sigma[A]^2=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}
 \int_{\RR^4}|y-z[A]|^2|F_A|^2.
\end{equation}
They identify translations and dilations of the charge-one instanton.
The analytic scale $\lambda$ is the dilation parameter in
$I_\lambda(z)=\lambda^{-1}I(z/\lambda)$, so
$\sigma[I_\lambda]=\lambda\sigma[I]$.
For
the corrected configurations we use only bounded local observables: a fixed
fractional-energy radius, a first moment truncated in normalized
coordinates, and convergence of the rescaled framed connection on compact
sets.  Proposition~\ref{prop:correction} implies that these observables tend
to those of the input instanton.  We do not apply the unbounded global
moments in~\eqref{eq:model-center-scale} directly to the corrected
connection.

\begin{lemma}[relative-frame recovery]
\label{lem:outer-frame}
Let $q_{g_n}+t_nu_{g_n}$ and $q_{g'_n}+t'_nu_{g'_n}$ be gauge-equivalent
pairs from Proposition~\ref{prop:correction}, where $t_n,t'_n\in[0,1]$
and both scales tend to zero.  Suppose their background distance,
center displacement divided by $\lambda_n$, and logarithmic scale ratio
tend to zero.  Use the same exterior background normalization and smooth
local frame conventions.  Then their relative frames tend to the same
element of $\SO(3)$.  This assertion is uniform in $t_n,t'_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The local elliptic estimates following Proposition~\ref{prop:correction}
give smooth convergence to the background on exterior compact sets and
to the regular-gauge instanton on fixed rescaled compact sets.  These
conclusions remain true after multiplying the correction by any
$t\in[0,1]$; no equation for the interpolated pair is needed.

First align the second background, center, and scale with the first,
leaving its relative frame unchanged in the chosen local conventions.
Lemma~\ref{lem:parameter-transport} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:pregluing-modulation} change the preglued pair by
$o(1)$ in the critical configuration norm.  Differences of the covariant
derivatives and pair divergences are bounded zero-order critical
multipliers.  Thus the transported corrections also remain $o(1)$ in
that norm.  These identifications preserve smooth endpoint convergence:
they fix the exterior frame, are the identity toward the regular core,
and the normalized center and scale changes tend to zero.
We may therefore compare pairs with common $q_n,x_n,\lambda_n$, frames
$\rho_{1,n},\rho_{2,n}$, and errors
$c_{i,n}=(a_{i,n},\phi_{i,n})$ satisfying
\[
 \epsilon_n:=\sum_{i=1}^2\|c_{i,n}\|_{\mathrm{crit},g_{i,n}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
These errors need not remain exactly horizontal after alignment.

Pass to a subsequence with convergent frames and choose local $\SU(2)$
lifts.  A smooth determinant-one gauge $v_n$ relating the pairs has
subsequential smooth limits on all endpoint compact sets.  Indeed its
first-order connection equation and unitarity bound all its derivatives
there.  The exterior limit stabilizes the background pair and is the
identity because its spinor is nonzero.  The inner regular-gauge limit
stabilizes $I$, and hence is $s\in\{1,-1\}$ by irreducibility.  With
$a(y)=y/|y|$, the two transitions are $a^{-1}\rho_i$, so the exterior
matrix of the inner limiting gauge is the constant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame-endpoint-constant}
 c=\rho_1^{-1}a s a^{-1}\rho_2=s\rho_1^{-1}\rho_2.
\end{equation}
We show that $c=1$.

Fix large $R$ and small $d$, and set
$\Omega_n=B(x_n,d)\setminus B(x_n,R\lambda_n)$.  In its common exterior
trivialization write the total connection forms as
$b_i=A_i^{\mathrm{pre}}+a_i$.  The smooth background is in radial gauge.
The preglued forms are radial-cutoff combinations of this background
and $\rho_{i,n}^{-1}I^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\lambda_n}\rho_{i,n}$.  Formula
\eqref{eq:quaternion-instanton} gives, for $r\geq R\lambda_n$,
\[
 |I^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\lambda_n}|\leq C\lambda_n^2r^{-3},\qquad
 |\partial I^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\lambda_n}|\leq C\lambda_n^2r^{-4}.
\]
The flat divergence and radial contraction vanish.  Gauss's lemma makes
the contraction with the gradient of a radial cutoff vanish also for the
physical metric.  Since its normal-coordinate coefficients satisfy
$g^{ij}-\delta^{ij}=O(r^2)$ and $\partial g=O(r)$, radial integration yields
\begin{align}\label{eq:frame-annular-coefficients}
 \|A_i^{\mathrm{pre}}\|_{L^4(\Omega_n)\cap L^{2\sharp}(\Omega_n)}
 &\leq C(d^2+R^{-2}),\notag\\
 \|d^*A_i^{\mathrm{pre}}\|_{L^\sharp(\Omega_n)}
 &\leq Cd^2+C\lambda_n^2(1+|\log\lambda_n|).
\end{align}
Here $d^*$ is the physical scalar codifferential on matrix-valued forms.
Restricted critical norms use zero extension of the absolute coefficient;
the divergence itself is taken before restriction.  The bounds hold with
the supremum over all points of $X$: dominate the exterior profiles by
radially decreasing functions constant on $r<R\lambda_n$.  Their weighted
integrals are largest at the center, up to uniform coordinate constants.
The weighted square integral of the potential is $O(R^{-4})$, while
the metric divergence error $O(\lambda_n^2/r^2)$ contributes
$O(\lambda_n^2(1+|\log\lambda_n|))$.  The background contributes $O(d^2)$.

Kato's inequality and Hardy's inequality bound
$\|a_i\|_{L^4\cap L^{2\sharp}}$ by $C\epsilon_n$.  Expressing the
ordinary divergence through the pair divergence adds contractions of
$A_i^{\mathrm{pre}}a_i$ and $\Phi_i^{\mathrm{pre}}\phi_i$.
The preglued spinor is uniformly bounded, with restricted
$L^{2\sharp}$ norm at most $Cd$.  The critical product inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame-error-divergence}
 \|d^*a_i\|_{L^\sharp(\Omega_n)}
 \leq C\epsilon_n(1+d+d^2+R^{-2}).
\end{equation}
This is where control of the critical divergence, in addition to the
first-order norm, enters the comparison.

The gauge equation is $dv_n=v_nb_2-b_1v_n$.  A fixed determinant
connection contributes a scalar matrix and cancels from this equation.
Unitarity gives
$\|dv_n\|_{L^{2\sharp}(\Omega_n)}\leq C\mu_n$, where
$\mu_n=d^2+R^{-2}+\epsilon_n$.
Applying $d^*$ to the equation expresses $\Delta v_n$ as
$v_nd^*b_2-(d^*b_1)v_n$ and contractions of $dv_n$ with $b_1,b_2$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame-gauge-laplacian}
 \|\Delta v_n\|_{L^\sharp(\Omega_n)}
 \leq C\left(d^2+\lambda_n^2(1+|\log\lambda_n|)
 +\epsilon_n(1+d+d^2+R^{-2})+\mu_n^2\right).
\end{equation}

Extend $v_n$ as a matrix-valued function by setting it equal to $c$
on the inner ball and to $1$ outside the outer ball, using linear
cutoffs on $R\lambda_n<r<2R\lambda_n$ and $d/2<r<d$.
This extension need not take values in $\SU(2)$.  For fixed $R,d$,
smooth endpoint convergence makes its additional Laplacian terms
$o(1)$ in $L^\sharp$: an inner cutoff error has size
$o(1)/(R\lambda_n)^2$ on an annulus of radius $R\lambda_n$, and the
outer error has the analogous fixed-scale bound.  Subtract $1$ and
extend by zero to obtain a global function $w_n$ with values in a
trivial matrix bundle.  It satisfies $\|w_n\|_2\leq Cd^2$.
The scalar case of \cite[Lemma~5.3(2)]{FeehanCritical} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame-scalar-extension}
 \|w_n\|_{C^0}\leq
 C\bigl(\|\Delta w_n\|_{L^\sharp}+\|w_n\|_2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The metric in this estimate is the fixed physical metric throughout.
Taking $n\to\infty$ in~\eqref{eq:frame-gauge-laplacian} and
\eqref{eq:frame-scalar-extension}, with $R,d$ fixed, gives
\[
 |c-1|\leq C\bigl(d^2+(d^2+R^{-2})^2\bigr).
\]
Let $d\downarrow0$ and then $R\to\infty$.  Thus $c=1$, and
\eqref{eq:frame-endpoint-constant} identifies the two $\SO(3)$ frames.
Every subsequence of frames has the same conclusion, which proves the
lemma.  All estimates are uniform in the interpolation parameters.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[separation of parameters]\label{prop:separation}
Suppose $g_n$ and $g'_n$ have scales tending to zero and
$\gamma(g_n)=\gamma(g'_n)$.  Then their background points and
ideal centers agree in the limit.  For positive scales, the normalized
center displacement tends to zero, the scale ratio tends to one, and
the relative-frame distance in $\SO(3)$ tends to zero.  Consequently the map is
one-to-one after decreasing the maximum scale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
To prove injectivity after decreasing the maximum scale, argue by
contradiction with a sequence of failed pairs whose maximum scales tend to
zero.  Thus $\lambda_n,\lambda'_n\to0$.  The assertion needed here is
this small-scale separation; a local inverse at fixed positive scale would
not by itself prove global injectivity.  Uhlenbeck convergence first identifies the two
background limits and ideal points.  The fixed background slices then give
$d_N(q_n,q'_n)\to0$.

Rescale the common corrected configuration about $x_n$ by $\lambda_n$.
The first representation converges on compact sets to the centered
unit-scale instanton $I$.  In these coordinates, the second bubble has
relative translation and scale
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-center-scale}
 z_n=\lambda_n^{-1}\exp_{x_n}^{-1}(x'_n),
 \qquad t_n=\lambda'_n/\lambda_n.
\end{equation}
The curvature difference from either preglued connection tends to zero
in $L^2$, so their curvature measures have the same limit in these
coordinates.  Each preglued curvature measure differs by $o(1)$ in total
variation from the sum of its background measure and its localized
instanton measure.  Indeed the connection-cutoff errors and the omitted
instanton tail have $L^2$ norm $O_N(\lambda)$, the removed background
curvature has vanishing norm, and the metric comparison on the shrinking
ball tends to the Euclidean one.  The same bounds hold with $\lambda'_n$
for the second description.  A translated and dilated instanton has density
$Ct_n^4/(t_n^2+|y-z_n|^2)^4$.  If $|z_n|\to\infty$, its energy on
any fixed compact set tends to zero, uniformly in $t_n$: the density
there is at most $C/(|z_n|-K)^4$ on $|y|\leq K$.  If $t_n\to\infty$
with bounded $z_n$, the density also tends to zero.  If $t_n\to0$
with bounded $z_n$, a subsequence of its curvature measures tends to a
point mass.  Each alternative contradicts the smooth unit-scale limit.
Thus, after a subsequence,
$z_n\to z\in\RR^4$ and $t_n\to t\in(0,\infty)$.

The two rescaled limits are gauge equivalent, and the second is the
translate and dilate of $I$ by $(z,t)$.  Applying
\eqref{eq:model-center-scale} to these exact limiting instantons gives
$z=0$ and $t=1$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:outer-frame}, with the same exterior
background normalization, then identifies their relative $\SO(3)$ frames modulo
exactly the central and diagonal actions already divided out.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-separation}
 d_{\mathrm{par}}(g_n,g'_n)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
For large $n$, the two parameters lie within the uniform modulation radius.
Put the common configuration in the Coulomb slice and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:modulation}; uniqueness gives literal equality.  At zero
scale the cone quotient has already identified all relative frames.
\end{proof}

Because the compactified parameter space over the compact background is
compact after the outer boundary is fixed, sequential separation also rules
out coincidences between parameters that are not initially close.  We have
therefore proved the following statement.

\begin{corollary}[compactified embedding]\label{cor:embedding}
After decreasing $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$, $\gamma$ is an equivariant
topological embedding of the compactified virtual gluing space.  On the
positive-scale stratum it is a smooth embedding.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Separation and uniform modulation give injectivity for a uniform small
scale bound.  Continuity at zero scale and compactness of the domain
then give a topological embedding into the Hausdorff Uhlenbeck space.
At positive scale the tubular coordinates identify the corrected map
with the smooth graph $g\mapsto(g,u_g)$, so it is a smooth embedding.
\end{proof}

Finally write $\gamma(g)=q_g+u_g$ in the chosen Coulomb slice.  For
$0\leq t\leq1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isotopy}
 \gamma_t(g)=q_g+t\,u_g.
\end{equation}
The correction remains horizontal and in $\ker R_g$ after multiplication
by $t$, and $\|t u_g\|_{\mathrm{crit},g}\leq C\lambda^{1/3}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:outer-frame} supplies frame recovery uniformly in $t$;
Lemma~\ref{lem:modulation} supplies the uniform local uniqueness.
The parameter-separation argument therefore applies uniformly in $t$.  Hence every $\gamma_t$ is a compactified
embedding.  The construction is equivariant, $\gamma_0$ is the splicing
map, and $\gamma_1=\gamma$.

\section{Reverse gluing and neighborhood surjectivity}
\label{sec:reverse-gluing}

Continuity and injectivity do not imply that every nearby monopole is
obtained by gluing.  We prove the converse by first putting an arbitrary
one-bubble sequence close to a preglued pair and then applying the same
complement and inverse used in the construction.

\begin{proposition}[strong splicing approximation]
\label{prop:strong-splicing}
Suppose that irreducible monopoles $[A_n,\Phi_n]$ converge in the Uhlenbeck
topology to
\[
 ([B,\Psi],x)\in M_{\mathfrak s}\times X
\]
with exactly one unit of instanton charge lost at $x$.  There are coarse
gluing data
\[
 g_n^0=([B,\Psi],x_n,\lambda_n,\rho_n),\qquad
 x_n\to x,\quad\lambda_n\downarrow0,
\]
standard gluing bundle identifications, and determinant-one gauges for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-splicing}
 \|(A_n,\Phi_n)-q_{\lambda_n}(g_n^0)\|_{\mathrm{crit},g_n^0}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The relative frames $\rho_n$ use the quotient convention in
\eqref{eq:gluing-quotient}; no auxiliary tangent or bundle frame remains in
the datum.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Extraction of the bubble}
The monopole Weitzenb\"ock formula gives a uniform $L^\infty$ bound for
$\Phi_n$ on the fixed compact perturbation family.  Choose $x_n$ and
$\lambda_n$ by a fixed fractional-energy centering and radius convention in
a normal-coordinate ball about $x$.  Pull back by
$y\mapsto\exp_{x_n}(\lambda_n y)$ and choose Uhlenbeck gauges.  The rescaled
curvature equation has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rescaled-curvature}
 F_{A_n^{\mathrm{res}}}^+
 =\lambda_n^2\mathcal Q(\Phi_n^{\mathrm{res}})
  +\lambda_n^2\mathcal P_n,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P_n$ is bounded on each fixed normalized ball.  The right
side tends to zero.  Uhlenbeck compactness and removal of the singularity at
infinity therefore give a finite-energy ASD connection $I$ on $S^4$.  The
lost charge is one, so $I$ has charge one.  The centering and radius
conventions make it centered and of unit scale; the framed charge-one
classification identifies the remaining parameter with a framed rotation
$\rho_n\in\SO(3)$.  We choose this rotation by comparing the rescaled bubble
gauge with the outer background gauge through the annular patching
normalization below; this is equivalent to the framed classification and
prevents a second, unrecorded frame from entering the gluing datum.

After a subsequence,
\begin{align}\label{eq:two-sided-strong-convergence}
 A_n^{\mathrm{res}}&\longrightarrow I
 &&\text{strongly in $L^2_1$ on compact subsets of $\RR^4$,}\notag\\
 (A_n,\Phi_n)&\longrightarrow(B,\Psi)
 &&\text{strongly in $L^2_1$ on compact subsets of $X\setminus\{x\}$.}
\end{align}
The conformally rescaled spinor tends to zero on the bubble side.

\begin{lemma}[one-bubble no neck]\label{lem:no-neck}
For the centers and scales above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-neck-energy}
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{d\downarrow0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \int_{B(x_n,d)\setminus B(x_n,R\lambda_n)}|F_{A_n}|^2=0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The curvature measures converge to
$|F_B|^2dV+8\pi^2\delta_x$.  For fixed $d$, the exterior accounts
for the background energy.  The rescaled ball $B(0,R)$ accounts for
the corresponding part of the charge-one instanton's energy.
The remaining background energy tends to zero as $d\downarrow0$,
and the instanton tail tends to zero as $R\to\infty$.  Subtracting
these contributions proves the assertion.  The same conclusion holds
on any fixed-ratio enlargement of the annulus.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local regularity and annular gauges}
We first obtain gauges on the complete neck with small local norms.
Summability will follow from the curvature equation in a further gauge.
All annuli and overlaps below have fixed shapes after dilation, and
local estimates are applied on slightly enlarged sets.

\begin{lemma}[local neck regularity]\label{lem:neck-local-regularity}
For the genuine monopoles under consideration, let $r$ be a sufficiently
small coordinate-annulus radius and normalize by $z=ry$.
On a fixed normalized ball with sufficiently small curvature energy,
there is a determinant-one gauge in which the trace-free connection
form $a$ and the spinor $\psi=r\Phi$ satisfy, on a smaller ball,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-local-regularity}
 \|a\|_{C^k}\le C_k(\|F_A\|_{L^2}+r^2),\qquad
 \|\psi\|_{C^k}\le C_k r.
\end{equation}
Here $k$ is any fixed finite integer.  The constants are uniform in
the center, radius, and fixed compact perturbation family.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the Euclidean coordinate metric in the ball gauge theorem
\cite[Theorem~2.1]{Uhlenbeck82}.  Curvature norms for it and the
normalized geometric metric are uniformly comparable.  The resulting
gauge has $d_\delta^*a=0$ and
$\|a\|_{W^{1,2}}\le CE$, where $E=\|F_A\|_{L^2}$.
The geometric metric differs from $\delta$ by $O(r^2)$ with any fixed
number of normalized derivatives.  The fixed determinant and spin
connections are smooth coefficients.  Constant dilation of the
monopole equations, with $\psi=r\Phi$, gives $|\psi|\le Cr$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-bootstrap-equation}
 Q_\delta a+K_r\nabla a+B_a a=s_r,
 \quad Q_\delta=d_\delta^*\oplus d_\delta^+,
 \quad \|K_r\|_{C^k}\le C_k r^2,
 \quad |B_a|\le C|a|,
 \quad \|s_r\|_{L^\infty}\le Cr^2.
\end{equation}
The term $B_a a$ is the quadratic curvature term, viewed as linear
in one factor.  The spinor and smooth determinant terms give $s_r$.

Choose a finite chain of nested balls, with the number depending only
on $k$.  For an interior cutoff $\chi$, extend $v=\chi a$ and the
cutoff coefficients to $\RR^4$.  The right side of its equation is in
$L^2\cap L^3$ with norm at most $C(E+r^2)$: the additional terms
are cutoff derivatives times $a\in L^4$.  The extended $B_a$ has
$L^4$ norm $O(E)$.  The constant-coefficient inverse $Q_\delta^{-1}$
maps $L^s$ into $\dot W^{1,s}$ for $s=2,3$.  Sobolev multiplication
therefore bounds
$Q_\delta^{-1}(K_r\nabla+B_a)$ on either space by $C_s(r^2+E)$.
Choose the thresholds to make both norms less than one half.
The same Neumann series converges in both spaces.  Uniqueness in
$\dot W^{1,2}$ identifies it with $\chi a$, proving
\[
 \|a\|_{W^{1,3}}+\|a\|_{L^{12}}\le C(E+r^2)
\]
on the first smaller ball.

The interior Dirac estimate now gives
$\|\psi\|_{W^{1,12}}\le Cr$, because the connection term is
$a\psi$ and $|\psi|\le Cr$.  The curvature equation has its
quadratic term in $L^6$, so the interior estimate for
$(d_\delta^*,d_g^+)$ gives
$\|a\|_{W^{1,6}}\le C(E+r^2)$.  These operators are uniformly
elliptic in the normalized metrics.  Since $W^{1,6}$ is an algebra
in dimension four, differentiation of the two equations and interior
estimates on the remaining nested balls prove
\eqref{eq:neck-local-regularity}.  The spinor estimates retain the
factor $r$, and their quadratic curvature sources retain $r^2$.
This argument uses constant dilation on each ball; it does not
identify that spinor normalization with a variable conformal weight.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[patching annular gauges]\label{lem:annular-patching}
Suppose a chain of overlapping normalized annuli has local connection
forms with $W^{1,p}$ norms $\epsilon_j$, where $2<p<4$ and
$\sup_j\epsilon_j$ is sufficiently small.  There is one gauge in
which the connection form on the $j$th slab has norm bounded by
$C(\epsilon_{j-1}+\epsilon_j+\epsilon_{j+1})$, allowing a fixed
number of adjacent terms for enlarged slabs.  Thus both the
$\ell^1$ and $\ell^\infty$ bounds are independent of chain length.
The outer frame can be fixed, with one constant rotation retained
at the inner end.  The same assertion holds in any fixed higher
regularity supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:neck-local-regularity}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For adjacent forms $a,b$, a transition $v$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-transition-equation}
 dv=vb-av.
\end{equation}
Put $q=4p/(4-p)>4$.  Unitarity and Sobolev embedding give
$\|dv\|_{L^q}\le C(\|a\|_{W^{1,p}}+\|b\|_{W^{1,p}})$.
Hence $v$ is continuous.  Differentiating the equation gives
$\nabla^2v$ as a sum of $v\nabla b$, $(\nabla a)v$,
$(dv)b$, and $a(dv)$.  Since $q/2\ge p$, these terms are bounded
in $L^p$.  Poincar\'e and Morrey estimates on the fixed connected
overlap give a constant $c\in\SU(2)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-transition-bound}
 \|v-c\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|v-c\|_{C^0}
 \le C(\|a\|_{W^{1,p}}+\|b\|_{W^{1,p}}).
\end{equation}
The right side is small enough to take the logarithm of $vc^{-1}$.
Its $W^{2,p}$ norm has the same bound.  No embedding
$W^{2,2}\hookrightarrow C^0$ is used.

If $e_{j+1}=e_jv_j$ are the local frames, put $s_1=1$ and
$s_{j+1}=c_j^{-1}s_j$.  The frames $e_j'=e_js_j$ have transitions
\[
 v_j'=s_j^{-1}(v_jc_j^{-1})s_j
\]
close to $1$ with the same bounds.  Products of constant unitary
matrices cause no norm growth.  On disjoint interpolation regions
use the frame $e_j'\exp(\chi_j\log v_j')$, where $\chi_j$ runs
from zero to one with fixed normalized derivative bounds.  It agrees
with the adjacent frames near its ends.  Differentiating the gauge
action gives the asserted local estimate.  Each nonconstant correction
is applied only on its own overlap.  The only accumulated transformation
is the constant $s_j$.

To obtain one small frame on each normalized annulus from the ball
gauges of Lemma~\ref{lem:neck-local-regularity}, use a fixed finite
good cover of $S^3\times(1,2)$.  The transition equation gives
$C^{k+1}$ bounds modulo constants on its overlaps.  Normalize those
constants on a spanning tree of the nerve.  The nerve is simply
connected; fix fillings by triangles for the finitely many remaining
edge loops.  The cocycle identity on a triple overlap bounds each
constant triangle product by $1+O(\epsilon)$.  The fixed fillings
therefore make all normalized transitions $1+O(\epsilon)$.
Using a determinant trivialization, view the local frames as unit
sections of a quaternionic line.  Their partition-of-unity average
is nonzero, since in any local frame it is $1+O(\epsilon)$.
Normalize this average to obtain a global unit section and hence an
$\SU(2)$ frame.  The finite construction preserves the derivative
bounds.  Apply the preceding chain argument to these annular frames.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Coulomb gauge on spheres and summable decay}
Write $t=\log r$ on the neck.  Apply the preceding lemmas on a
fixed-ratio enlargement, such as $R\lambda_n/4<r<4d$.
The no-neck energy gives, on $R\lambda_n<r<d$, one smooth gauge with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-small-gauge}
 \mathcal A=\alpha_0\,dt+a_0,\qquad
 \sup_j\|\mathcal A\|_{C^k(D_j^+)}\le C\epsilon,
\end{equation}
for a fixed sufficiently large $k$, say $k=8$.  Here $D_j^+$ are
fixed enlarged cylinder slabs, and $\epsilon$ tends to zero in the
iterated limit of~\eqref{eq:no-neck-energy}, with an additional
$O(d^2)$ term.  We now improve the supremum bound to a sum bound.

\begin{lemma}[sphere Coulomb gauge]\label{lem:sphere-coulomb}
Under~\eqref{eq:neck-small-gauge}, a further determinant-one gauge
gives $\mathcal A=\alpha\,dt+a$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sphere-coulomb}
 d_{S^3}^*a=0,\qquad \int_{S^3}\alpha=0.
\end{equation}
The resulting connection has uniformly small $C^2$ slab norms.
The gauge consists of a uniformly small transformation and a path
of spatially constant rotations, normalized at the outer end.
Its derivative bounds are independent of cylinder length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At each $t$, solve
\[
 d_{S^3}^*(u^{-1}a_0u+u^{-1}d_{S^3}u)=0,
 \qquad u=e^\xi,\quad\int_{S^3}\xi=0.
\]
The derivative at zero is the scalar Laplacian on mean-zero adjoint
functions on the fixed round sphere.  Apply the implicit-function
theorem in high Sobolev spaces.  Its inverse and differentiated
equations give the required spatial and time derivative bounds;
the initial $C^8$ control permits these derivatives and traces.
All estimates are on the same sphere and have no length parameter.
Let $\beta$ be the temporal part after this gauge and let
$\bar\beta$ denote its spherical average.  Solve
\[
 h'=-\bar\beta h,\qquad h(t_+)=1.
\]
Gauge by the spatially constant $h$.  The temporal part becomes
$h^{-1}(\beta-\bar\beta)h$, while the tangential Coulomb condition
is preserved.  Unitarity bounds $h$ and the equation bounds its
derivatives locally from those of $\bar\beta$.  The endpoint
$h(t_-)$ is retained as a relative rotation.
\end{proof}

In this gauge, the curvature equation, expressed using the round
product metric and the orientation $dt\wedge\operatorname{vol}_{S^3}$,
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-curvature-equation}
 \partial_ta-d_{S^3}\alpha+*_{S^3}d_{S^3}a
 =f-[\alpha,a]-*_{S^3}(a\wedge a).
\end{equation}
The forcing satisfies, for any fixed $2<p<4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-forcing}
 \|f\|_{W^{1,p}(D_j^+)}\le Cr_j^2,
 \qquad\sum_j\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(D_j^+)}\le Cd^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the normalized monopole source and its first derivative are
$O(r_j^2)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:neck-local-regularity}.  In normal
polar coordinates $r^{-2}g$ differs from the round product metric
by $O(r^2)$, with the same derivative bounds.  The difference of
the self-duality projections applied to curvature therefore contributes
$O(\epsilon r_j^2)$ with one derivative, using the already established
local $C^2$ connection bounds.  Smooth determinant and perturbation
coefficients obey the same estimates.  The gauges in
Lemma~\ref{lem:sphere-coulomb} preserve these bounds under conjugation.
Finally sum the geometric series in $r_j^2$.

\begin{lemma}[summable neck estimate]\label{lem:two-sided-cylinder}
Let $e_-,e_+$ be the $W^{1,p}$ norms of the connection on fixed
enlarged endpoint slabs.  In the gauge~\eqref{eq:sphere-coulomb},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinder-l1}
 \sum_j\|\alpha\,dt+a\|_{W^{1,p}(D_j)}
 \le C(e_-+e_++d^2),
\end{equation}
with $C$ independent of the neck length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $N=f-[\alpha,a]-*_{S^3}(a\wedge a)$.  Applying $d_{S^3}^*$
to~\eqref{eq:neck-curvature-equation} gives
$-\Delta_{S^3}\alpha=d_{S^3}^*N$.
The mean-zero inverse composed with $d_{S^3}^*$ maps $L^s$ to
$W^{1,s}$ on the sphere.  Absorbing the term with coefficient $a$
gives, for $s=2,p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-temporal-bound}
 \|\alpha(t)\|_{W^{1,s}(S^3)}
 \le C\|f(t)\|_{L^s(S^3)}
       +C\epsilon\|a(t)\|_{L^s(S^3)}.
\end{equation}
Let $P$ be projection onto coexact one-forms.  There are no harmonic
one-forms on $S^3$, so $Pa=a$.  Projection of the curvature equation
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-coexact-equation}
 (\partial_t+L)a=PN,
 \qquad L=*_{S^3}d_{S^3}|_{\ker d_{S^3}^*}.
\end{equation}
The spectrum of $L$ avoids $(-2,2)$.  Let $P_+$ and $P_-$ be its
positive and negative spectral projections, and let $t_-,t_+$ be
the two ends of the cylinder.  Variation of constants gives
\begin{align*}
 a(t)={}&e^{-(t-t_-)L}P_+a(t_-)
       +e^{(t_+-t)L}P_-a(t_+)\\
 &+\int_{t_-}^{t}e^{-(t-s)L}P_+PN(s)\,ds
       -\int_t^{t_+}e^{-(t-s)L}P_-PN(s)\,ds.
\end{align*}
Each propagator decays in $L^2$ at least as $e^{-2|t-s|}$ in
its indicated time direction.  The endpoint traces satisfy
$b_\pm:=\|a(t_\pm)\|_2\leq Ce_\pm$ by the trace estimate on
the fixed enlarged endpoint slabs.  If $b_j$ and $F_j$ are the
slab $L^2$ norms of $a$ and $f$, respectively, this formula and
\eqref{eq:neck-temporal-bound} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-annulus}
 b_j\le C\left(\theta^{j-j_-}b_-+
 \theta^{j_+-j}b_++\sum_k\theta^{|j-k|}(F_k+\epsilon b_k)\right)
\end{equation}
for a fixed $0<\theta<1$.
The convolution is bounded on $\ell^1$ independently of length.
Summing and absorbing gives a summed $L^2$ bound.  Fixed-slab
interior estimates for the elliptic system
$((\partial_t+*d)a,d^*a)$ then give the corresponding summed
$W^{1,2}$ bound.

For $2<p<4$, the slab embedding $W^{1,2}\hookrightarrow L^4$
controls the summed $L^p$ norm of $a$.  The Hodge projection $P$
is bounded on $L^p$, and~\eqref{eq:neck-temporal-bound} bounds
$PN$ by $C\|f\|_{L^p}+C\epsilon\|a\|_{L^p}$.
Apply the fixed-slab interior estimate at exponent $p$.  Enlarged
endpoint slabs account for the boundary terms, yielding
\[
 \sum_j\|a\|_{W^{1,p}(D_j)}
 \le C\left(e_-+e_++\sum_j\|f\|_{L^p(D_j^+)}\right).
\]
Finally differentiate the scalar equation for $\alpha$ in $t$.
The mean remains zero.  Absorb the term $(\partial_t\alpha)a$;
the other nonlinear terms have coefficients $\alpha$ or $a$,
already small in $C^0$.  Thus
\[
 \|\partial_t\alpha(t)\|_{W^{1,p}(S^3)}
 \le C\|\partial_tf(t)\|_{L^p(S^3)}
       +C\epsilon\|\partial_ta(t)\|_{L^p(S^3)}.
\]
Integration,~\eqref{eq:neck-temporal-bound}, and
\eqref{eq:neck-forcing} prove~\eqref{eq:cylinder-l1}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Critical comparison and the endpoint gauges}
On a physical annulus $U_j$ of radius $r_j$, let
\[
 \delta_j=r_j^{1-4/p}\|c\|_{L^p(U_j)}
          +r_j^{2-4/p}\|\nabla c\|_{L^p(U_j)}.
\]
This is equivalent to the $W^{1,p}$ norm of its pulled-back one-form
on the normalized annulus.  For $p>2$, H\"older's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-weighted-shell}
 \sup_x\int_{U_j}\frac{|v(z)|}{|x-z|^2}\,dz
 \le Cr_j^{2-4/p}\|v\|_{L^p(U_j)}.
\end{equation}
For $x$ within distance $O(r_j)$ of $U_j$, integrate the kernel
to the power $p'<2$; for more distant $x$, bound it by $Cr_j^{-2}$.
Thus the supremum includes every center.  Applying the analogous
estimate to $|c|^2$, and using the normalized embedding
$W^{1,p}\hookrightarrow L^{4p/(4-p)}$, shows
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-critical-sum}
 \|c\|_{L^4}+\|\nabla c\|_{L^2}
 +\|c\|_{L^{2\sharp}}+\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}
 \le C\sum_j\delta_j.
\end{equation}
These are norms of the indicated functions on the annulus, extended
by zero as measurable functions for the weighted estimates.  No
derivative of a zero extension is taken.  Smooth metric comparability
and its lower-order connection terms give the same physical estimates.

The spliced background--instanton connection has normalized annular
norms bounded by $C(r_j^2+(\lambda_n/r_j)^2)$ in its exterior
trivialization.  Their sum on $R\lambda_n<r<d$ is at most
$C(d^2+R^{-2})$, including the fixed-ratio cutoff annuli.
Together with~\eqref{eq:cylinder-l1}, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-neck-strong}
 \|A_n-A_n^{\rm spl}\|_{L^4}
 +\|\nabla_{A_n^{\rm spl}}(A_n-A_n^{\rm spl})\|_{L^2}
 +\|d_{A_n^{\rm spl}}^*(A_n-A_n^{\rm spl})\|_{L^{\sharp,2}}
 \le C(e_-+e_++d^2+R^{-2})+o_n(1).
\end{equation}
Here the gauges at the endpoints are chosen as follows.

At the outer end, the sequence in its background convergence gauge
has normalized connection norm $O(d^2)+o_n(1)$ in a radial frame.
At the inner end, its extracted instanton in exterior gauge has norm
$O(R^{-2})+o_n(1)$.  Compare these gauges with the cylinder gauge
using~\eqref{eq:annular-transition-equation}.  The nonconstant
transition parts are small in $W^{2,p}$ and $C^0$, bounded by the
endpoint errors and the corresponding model norms.  Fix the outer
constant and retain the inner one as $\rho_n$.  Interpolate only
the small nonconstant parts by their logarithms on fixed-ratio
endpoint annuli.  The regular-to-exterior instanton transition is
kept intact: it is the charge-one clutching map.  The interpolated
gauge agrees with the original background convergence gauge outside
and with the extracted bubble gauge, up to $\rho_n$, inside.
It therefore defines one determinant-one bundle identification of
the prescribed splicing type.  Its additional connection errors
obey~\eqref{eq:neck-critical-sum}, proving
\eqref{eq:no-neck-strong}.  Changing ordinary to model-covariant
derivatives uses $L^4\cdot L^4\to L^2$ and
$L^{2\sharp}\cdot L^{2\sharp}\to L^\sharp$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:strong-splicing}]
The exterior and rescaled convergence in
\eqref{eq:two-sided-strong-convergence} improves, after gauge, to smooth
convergence on fixed compact sets.  Cover each such set by finitely
many small-energy balls.  The interior bootstrap in
Lemma~\ref{lem:neck-local-regularity} gives uniform higher bounds
in local Coulomb gauges; the transition equation patches their limits,
which the original convergence identifies with the background or
instanton.  On the neck use
the gauges and estimate~\eqref{eq:no-neck-strong}.  The endpoint
terms tend to zero in the iterated limit by
\eqref{eq:neck-small-gauge}.  The fixed-domain exterior and rescaled
core differences tend to zero in the critical norms by smooth
convergence and~\eqref{eq:neck-weighted-shell}.

For the spinor, the uniform pointwise bound gives
\[
 \|\Phi_n\|_{L^4(B_d)}+\|\Phi_n\|_{L^{2\sharp}(B_d)}\le Cd.
\]
Choose $\eta$ supported in $B_{2d}$ and equal to one on $B_d$.
Since $D_{A_n}\Phi_n=-\rho(\vartheta)\Phi_n$, the
Weitzenb\"ock identity for $\eta\Phi_n$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neck-spinor-energy}
 \|\nabla_{A_n}\Phi_n\|_{L^2(B_d)}^2
 \le C\int_{B_{2d}}(|d\eta|^2+1)|\Phi_n|^2\le Cd^2.
\end{equation}
Only self-dual curvature acts on the positive spinor.  Its trace-free
part is bounded by the monopole equation and its determinant part
is fixed, so no bound on the full curvature is needed here.
The preglued spinor is cut off at radius $\lambda_n^{1/3}$,
outside the modified connection region.  Its derivative is the
cutoff times the background derivative plus the cutoff derivative
times the background spinor; its $L^2(B_d)$ norm is bounded by
$C(d^2+\lambda_n^{1/3})$.  Changing the derivative of $\Phi_n$
to the spliced connection adds at most
$Cd\|A_n-A_n^{\rm spl}\|_{L^4}$.
The spinor contribution to the pair divergence has
$L^{\sharp,2}(B_d)$ norm $O(d^2)$, since both spinors are bounded.
These estimates and~\eqref{eq:no-neck-strong} give the full critical
pair comparison.  On the exterior the spinors converge smoothly.

Choose a slow diagonal $R_n\to\infty$, $d_n\downarrow0$ after
the fixed-$R,d$ estimates, retaining
$R_n\lambda_n\ll\sqrt{\lambda_n}\ll\lambda_n^{1/3}\ll d_n$.
All pregluing cutoffs then lie inside the comparison annulus.
The resulting global determinant-one identifications and relative
frames give~\eqref{eq:strong-splicing}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Exact modulation and complemented uniqueness}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:modulation} to the configurations in
Proposition~\ref{prop:strong-splicing}.  After determinant-one gauge,
there are unique exact gluing data
$g_n=(q_n,\widehat x_n,\widehat\lambda_n,\widehat\rho_n)$ and differences
$u_n$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-modulation}
 (A_n,\Phi_n)=q_{\widehat\lambda_n}(g_n)+u_n,\qquad
 d^{0,*}_{q_{\widehat\lambda_n}}u_n=0,\qquad
 R_{g_n}u_n=0,\qquad
 \|u_n\|_{L^2_1}\to0.
\end{equation}
The exact parameters are close to the coarse ones in
\eqref{eq:parameter-metric}; in particular, $q_n$ tends to the reducible
background and $b(q_n)\to0$.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-augmented-vector}
 H_n=(u_n,-b(q_n),0).
\end{equation}
The complement condition depends only on $u_n$, so $H_n$ lies in
$\mathbb H^{\mathrm{cmp}}_{\widehat\lambda_n,N}$.  Since $(A_n,\Phi_n)$ is
a monopole and is in the Coulomb slice,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-augmented-equation}
 \widehat\cF_{g_n}(H_n)=0.
\end{equation}
Let $e_n=\widehat\cF_{g_n}(0)$.  The inverse and quadratic estimates give
\[
 \|H_n\|
 \leq C\|e_n\|+C\|H_n\|^2
 \leq C\widehat\lambda_n^{1/3}+C\|H_n\|^2.
\]
The qualitative convergence in~\eqref{eq:exact-modulation} absorbs the
quadratic term, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-rate}
 \|H_n\|\leq 2C\widehat\lambda_n^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
Thus $H_n$ lies in the same contraction ball as the correction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:correction}.  Uniqueness of that fixed point gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse-uniqueness}
 (u_n,-b(q_n),0)
 =(u_{g_n},\eta_{s,g_n},\eta_{i,g_n}).
\end{equation}
It follows that $[A_n,\Phi_n]=\gamma(g_n)$ and $\chi(g_n)=0$.

\begin{proposition}[neighborhood surjectivity]\label{prop:neighborhood}
There is an Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood $\cU$ of
$M_{\mathfrak s}\times X$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neighborhood-image}
 \cU\cap\overline\cM_{\mathfrak t'}^{*,0}
 =\cU\cap\gamma(\chi^{-1}(0)).
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\gamma$ restricts to a homeomorphism from $\chi^{-1}(0)$
onto an Uhlenbeck-open neighborhood of the ideal stratum, after restricting
the virtual neighborhood over its outer boundary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At positive scale, the augmented equation and injectivity of $J_g$
identify the obstruction zero set with the monopoles in the image.
At zero scale, $\chi^{-1}(0)=b^{-1}(0)\times X$ is the nearby
lower-level monopole space.  We first check that its middle-stratum
points belong to the Uhlenbeck closure of the positive-scale moduli
space.  We work in the interior of the background disk, which suffices
near $M_{\mathfrak s}\times X$.

Fix $q_0\in b^{-1}(0)-M_{\mathfrak s}$ and a center $x$.
Transversality of $b$ gives a local disk $D$ through $q_0$ on which
$b$ is a diffeomorphism onto a neighborhood of zero in $\Xi_{q_0}$,
after trivializing $\Xi$.  Trivialize the relative-frame bundle over
this disk.  Proposition~\ref{prop:correction-derivatives} gives
$\chi_s-b=O(\lambda^{1/3})$ in $C^1$ in the background variable,
uniformly for $\rho\in\SO(3)$.  The implicit-function theorem
therefore gives a smooth choice $q(\lambda,\rho)\in D$ with
$\chi_s(q(\lambda,\rho),x,\lambda,\rho)=0$ and
$q(\lambda,\rho)\to q_0$ uniformly in $\rho$.
For each small positive $\lambda$, the remaining section over this
graph takes values in
\[
 \SU(2)\times_{\{\pm1\}}\CC\longrightarrow\SO(3).
\]
This complex line is nontrivial: its first Chern class generates
$H^2(\SO(3);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2$.
It consequently admits no nowhere-zero section.  Choose a zero
$\rho_\lambda$ of the restricted $\chi_i$.  The corresponding
positive-scale monopoles converge to $(q_0,x)$, proving the required
closure assertion.  If $r_\Xi=0$, take $D=\{q_0\}$ in this argument.
The lowest stratum already belongs to the compactification by
\eqref{eq:intro-stratum}.  Thus the inclusion from right to left
holds at every scale.

Conversely, nearby zero-scale ideal monopoles are in the image by
the background finite-dimensional reduction.  If the reverse
inclusion failed in every neighborhood, there would therefore be genuine
monopoles outside $\gamma(\chi^{-1}(0))$ converging to the compact ideal
stratum.  A subsequence converges to one point of that stratum, and the
argument above places every sufficiently late member in
$\gamma(\chi^{-1}(0))$, a contradiction.  The inverse is continuous because
$\gamma$ is a compactified embedding by Corollary~\ref{cor:embedding}.
\end{proof}

\section{The obstruction cone and orientations}\label{sec:orientations}

\subsection{The compactified section}
The background summand $\overline\Upsilon^s$ is the pullback of the fixed
complex bundle $\Xi\to M_{\mathfrak s}$ to
$N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}$.  The charge-one Dirac
operator has the kernel and cokernel in~\eqref{eq:bubble-cokernel}.
At positive scale its obstruction line is
\[
 (0,\delta]\times\SU(2)\times_{\{\pm1\}}\CC
 \longrightarrow(0,\delta]\times\SO(3).
\]
Its compactification is the cone map~\eqref{eq:obstruction-cone}; the whole
fiber, not merely its norm, is identified with the vertex at zero scale.
There are no partition-overlap maps at level one.

At positive scale, define $\chi$ by~\eqref{eq:corrected-map-section}; at zero
scale, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-scale-obstruction}
 \chi(q,x,0)=(b(q),0).
\end{equation}
The contraction is equivariant, because the nonlinear map, cutoffs,
transports, and inverse are equivariant and its fixed point is unique.  We use
the diagonal presentation before taking the effective residual circle; it has
weight two on the normal and background-obstruction factors.  Write
$\nu$ for minus the first Chern class of the line associated to the
effective circle character of the background obstruction on the virtual
link quotient; equivalently the weight-one associated line has class
$-\nu$ in this convention.  Since $\Xi$ was chosen globally trivial,
after passage to the effective action its Euler class is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:background-euler}
 e(\overline\Upsilon^s/S^1)=(-\nu)^{r_\Xi}.
\end{equation}
The instanton line retains the nontrivial
$\SU(2)/\{\pm1\}$ framing twist, so its Euler class is computed from its
tensor square in Section~\ref{sec:pairing} rather than copied from
\eqref{eq:background-euler}.

\begin{proposition}[compactified obstruction section]
\label{prop:obstruction-section}
The two components of $\chi$ are smooth at positive scale.  The background
component extends continuously over the compactification and smoothly on
each zero-scale stratum.  The instanton component is a continuous section of
the cone pseudo-bundle.  With the metric induced by
$J_{s,g}\oplus J_{i,g}$, the norm of $\chi$ is continuous along the section.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Positive-scale smoothness follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:correction}.
At zero scale, its estimate gives
\[
 \|\eta_{s,g}\|+\|\eta_{i,g}\|\leq C\lambda^{1/3}.
\]
Thus $\chi_s$ tends to $b(q)$ and $\chi_i$ tends to the cone vertex.  For
$g_n\to(q,x,0)$, the Gram estimate in
Lemma~\ref{lem:matched-obstruction} gives
\[
 \|J_{s,g_n}\chi_s(g_n)+J_{i,g_n}\chi_i(g_n)\|_{L^2}^2
 =\|b(q)\|^2+o(1),
\]
which is the asserted norm continuity.  This does not extend the ambient
instanton line as a constant-rank bundle; only the section tends to its
collapsed fiber.
\end{proof}

We next compare the global reduction with the local small-eigenvalue
reduction of~\cite[Section~8]{FeehanLenessIII}.  There are three distinct
finite-dimensional reductions.  On a relatively compact positive-scale
chart $U^+$ on which a
cutoff $\mu$ avoids the spectrum, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-obstruction-models}
 E_{\mathrm{glob},g}=\Xi_q\oplus K_I,\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{loc},g}=\ker d_q^{1,*}\oplus K_I,\qquad
 E_{\mathrm{spec},g}=\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{q_g,\mu}.
\end{equation}
The published cut-and-project and synthesis estimates
\cite[Propositions~8.19 and~8.26]{FeehanLenessIII} identify
$E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with $E_{\mathrm{spec}}$ on a constant-rank chart:
their two composites are $I+o(1)$.  They also transport the ordered
orientation local system of
$\ker d_q^{1,*}\oplus K_I$ to $E_{\mathrm{spec}}$, compatibly on overlaps.
There is generally no isomorphism between $E_{\mathrm{glob}}$ and
$E_{\mathrm{loc}}$, since the global augmentation may contain extra
stabilizing directions.

\begin{proposition}[stable comparison of reductions]
\label{prop:stable-reduction}
On every positive-scale constant-rank germ of the monopole zero set, the
global and spectral reductions admit a common equivariant finite-dimensional
enlargement.  Their zero loci agree, the comparisons satisfy the cocycle
condition up to equivariant homotopy on a common germ, and the induced determinant
orientation and relative Euler--Thom pairing agree.  No equality of the two
ranks and no spectral bundle at zero scale is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a smooth Fredholm section $F$ with two surjective augmentations
$i_j:E_j\to Y$, form
\[
 \cV_{01}=\{(x,e_0,e_1):F(x)-i_0e_0-i_1e_1=0\}.
\]
The two reductions embed by setting the other coefficient equal to zero.
At a point of the first reduction, projection to $e_1$ identifies its normal
bundle in $\cV_{01}$ with $E_1$: surjectivity of
$DF\oplus(-i_0)$ supplies a lift of every prescribed $e_1$.  The analogous
statement holds with the indices reversed.  Thus both obstruction zero loci
are canonically $F^{-1}(0)$.

Apply this construction with $E_0=E_{\mathrm{glob}}$ and
$E_1=E_{\mathrm{spec}}$.  The first augmented derivative is surjective
at the pregluing by Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse}, and remains
so at the small correction by the quadratic estimate and openness.
On $E_{\mathrm{spec}}^\perp$, the second
has right inverse
\[
 d_{q_g}^{1,*}(d_{q_g}^1d_{q_g}^{1,*})^{-1},
\]
bounded by the spectral gap at $\mu$.  Positive-scale smoothness of the
inverse and the Riesz formula for $\Pi_{q_g,\mu}$ give smooth tubular
maps on a common germ.  On overlaps,
order all spectral factors by chart label and use literal zero-coordinate
embeddings; associativity of direct sum gives the cocycle before tubular
choices, and nearby invariant tubular choices are homotopic through their
graph maps.

Along the first embedding,
\[
 \det T\cV_{01}\cong\det T\cV_0\otimes\det E_1.
\]
The same $E_1$ occurs in the common obstruction bundle, so its determinant
line cancels its dual.  Along the other embedding the fixed order contributes
the Koszul factor
$(-1)^{\rank_{\RR}E_0\rank_{\RR}E_1}$; it is $+1$ because
$E_0=\Xi\oplus K_I$ has even real rank.  If $E_1$ is not globally oriented,
its orientation local system is carried by both factors and cancels
canonically.  The identical cancellation holds in relative equivariant
cohomology: the Thom class of the added normal $E_1$ is the Euler factor of
the added obstruction $E_1$.  Hence stabilization changes neither the
oriented zero locus nor the relative Euler--Thom pairing.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact transversality statement}
At a positive-scale zero, the extended equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extended-identity}
 \mathfrak S(\gamma(g))=J_g\chi(g).
\end{equation}
Let $Q_g$ be projection onto the obstruction image.  The contraction
parametrizes the local virtual manifold $(1-Q_g)\mathfrak S=0$, and the
complemented inverse identifies its tangent space with
$D\gamma_g(T_g\cG^+)$.  Differentiating~\eqref{eq:extended-identity} at a
zero gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:differentiated-obstruction}
 D\mathfrak S_{\gamma(g)}D\gamma_g=J_gD\chi_g.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[stratumwise transversality]\label{prop:transversality}
For a generic perturbation at which the irreducible monopole section is
transverse, the total section $\chi_s\oplus\chi_i$ is transverse on
$\cG^+$.  On
$(N_{\mathfrak t,\mathfrak s}-M_{\mathfrak s})\times X$, the surviving
background component $\chi_s=b$ is transverse.  No transversality holds or
is asserted on $M_{\mathfrak s}\times X$ when $r_\Xi>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given $e\in\Upsilon_g$ at a positive-scale zero, surjectivity of
$D\mathfrak S_{\gamma(g)}$ supplies $v$ with
$D\mathfrak S_{\gamma(g)}v=J_ge$.  Its projected equation component
vanishes, so $v$ is tangent to the virtual manifold and equals
$D\gamma_g\dot g$ for some $\dot g$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:differentiated-obstruction} and injectivity of $J_g$ give
$D\chi_g\dot g=e$.

On the middle stratum, the instanton fiber has collapsed and the background
finite-dimensional reduction makes $b$ transverse; the $X$ factor is
passive.  On the lowest stratum $b$ is identically zero.  An identically zero
section of a positive-rank bundle is not transverse.  This is why
\cite[Theorem~3.8 and Section~5.1]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} asserts precisely
the first two conclusions and disclaims the third.
\end{proof}

Accordingly, the all-strata formulation in
\cite[Hypothesis~7.8.1(2)]{FeehanLenessCobordism} must be read using the
coarser stratification there.  For the fine decomposition
\eqref{eq:three-strata}, the relative Euler construction uses Proposition
\ref{prop:transversality} and does not require transversality on the lowest
stratum.

\subsection{Orientation under instanton addition}
For a real Fredholm operator $F$, use
\[
 \det F=\Lambda^{\max}\ker F\otimes
 (\Lambda^{\max}\coker F)^*.
\]
Direct-sum factors are ordered as written, and complex vector spaces carry
their complex orientations.  For $q\in N(\varepsilon)$, write
$p=\pi_N(q)\in M_{\mathfrak s}$.  Orient $T_pM_{\mathfrak s}$ using the homology
orientation $\Omega$.  Orient the centered framed instanton parameters
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quaternionic-orientation}
 (\partial_\lambda,i,j,k)
 \quad\text{on}\quad \RR\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)=\mathbb H.
\end{equation}

Write $O^{\mathrm{asd}}(\Omega,c_1(L))$ for the monopole orientation
obtained from the ASD orientation by turning off the spinor and using
the complex Dirac orientation, as in
\cite[Lemma~2.2 and Definition~2.3]{FeehanLenessII}.
We compare the specific kernel map $S_g$ with the instanton-addition
map of~\cite[Proposition~(3.20)]{DonaldsonOrientation}.  The latter is
constructed by projecting cutoff curvature-contraction modes and
background kernel fields.  The following argument includes finite
stabilizations along the homotopy that removes the spinor couplings.

\begin{proposition}[instanton-addition orientation]
\label{prop:addition-orientation}
The synthesis $S_g$ induces an orientation-preserving isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:augmented-excision}
 \det\widehat{\mathscr D}^{X}_q\otimes\det\cK_I^{\rm fr}
 \longrightarrow\det\widehat{\mathscr D}_{q_g},
\end{equation}
where the monopole determinant lines have orientation
$O^{\mathrm{asd}}(\Omega,c_1(L))$, the finite obstruction spaces have
their complex orientations, and the bubble has
\eqref{eq:quaternionic-orientation} followed by its four translations.
This comparison is compatible with the frame and residual-circle actions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first fix the rotation sign.  In the regular quaternionic gauge put
$v_\theta(y)=\theta y$ for $\theta\in\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H$.
Left rotations preserve
$I=\operatorname{Im}(\bar y\,dy)/(1+|y|^2)$, and
$\iota_{v_\theta}I=h_\theta=\bar y\theta y/(1+|y|^2)$.
Covariant Cartan's formula gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotation-contraction-orientation}
 0=\mathcal L_{v_\theta}I
   =\iota_{v_\theta}F_I+d_Ih_\theta,
 \qquad -\iota_{\theta y}F_I=k_\theta.
\end{equation}
In the exterior framing, pullback by $y\mapsto e^{-t\theta}y$
acts as $I\mapsto e^{-t\theta}Ie^{t\theta}$.  This agrees with the
relative-frame convention used for the modes above.  The negative
dilation and translation flows give $k_s=-\iota_yF_I$ and
$k_j=-\iota_{e_j}F_I$, respectively, with the based gauge corrections
already included in their Coulomb representatives.

For $y=x^0+x^1i+x^2j+x^3k$, the positive self-dual basis is
\begin{align*}
 \omega_1&=dx^0\wedge dx^1+dx^2\wedge dx^3,\quad
 \omega_2=dx^0\wedge dx^2-dx^1\wedge dx^3,\\
 \omega_3&=dx^0\wedge dx^3+dx^1\wedge dx^2.
\end{align*}
Indeed $-\operatorname{Im}(dy\wedge d\bar y)
=2(i\omega_1+j\omega_2+k\omega_3)$.  With complex coordinates
$z_1=x^0+ix^1$, $z_2=x^2+ix^3$, the real $(0,2)$ plane has complex
orientation $(\omega_2,\omega_3)$ after the K\"ahler form $\omega_1$.
These are the conventions of~\cite[Section~3(c), p.~413]{DonaldsonOrientation}.
Thus the oriented curvature modes for
$\RR\oplus\Lambda^+\oplus\RR^4$ are precisely
$(k_s,k_i,k_j,k_k,k_0,\ldots,k_3)$, where the subscripts $i,j,k$
denote frame directions and $0,\ldots,3$ denote translations.
All changes from normalized scale and center coordinates multiply
basis vectors by positive numbers.  Interchanging the two blocks of
four directions also has positive determinant.

We next establish compatibility with arbitrary finite stabilizations.
Let $D_b$ be a compact smooth background family obtained by varying
the zeroth-order monopole coefficients, and choose smooth target
columns $J_b:W\to C^\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-orientation-augmentation}
 B_b(u,w)=D_bu-J_bw
\end{equation}
is surjective.  The columns need not be independent.  Localize them
as $\beta_XT_X^YJ_b$ and retain the complex bubble Dirac column.
The parametrix and weak estimates of
Propositions~\ref{prop:uniform-inverse} and~\ref{prop:projected-kernel}
still apply, with constants uniform on this family.  A smooth target
column in the real gauge row has the same cutoff estimates as one
in the other rows.

For dependent columns, change the background retraction to the
positive Gram inverse of the full augmented kernel, using
$\|u\|_2^2+|w|^2$ and applying its duals to
$(\beta_XT_X^{-1}u,w)$.  Keep the compact bubble duals.  This
retains pure auxiliary kernel vectors, which configuration integrals
alone would lose.  The preliminary synthesis is
$(v,w)\mapsto(\beta_XT_Xv,w,0)$ on the background kernel and
$k\mapsto(\beta_IT_\lambda k,0,0)$ on the bubble.  The weighted
norm is now $\|u\|_2^2+|w|^2+\lambda^2|c_I|^2$.
The Gram lower bound and both mode errors in
\eqref{eq:weak-preliminary-errors} are unchanged.  The index increase
is eight: the ASD block contributes eight, the real Dirac index
decreases by two, and its complex bubble column adds two.
The corrected lift and orthogonal projection consequently give the
same orientation isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-kernel-addition}
 \cG_{J,g}:\ker B_b\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr}
 \longrightarrow\ker B_{J,g}.
\end{equation}
Local frames of these kernels suffice, and the maps vary continuously
at positive scale.

Suppose that $B_b$ is already surjective and add columns $J'_b$ on
$W'$.  The two exact sequences, on the background and glued sides, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orientation-enlargement-sequences}
 \begin{gathered}
 0\longrightarrow\ker B_b\longrightarrow\ker(B_b-J'_b)
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_{W'}}W'\longrightarrow0,\\
 0\longrightarrow\ker B_{J,g}\longrightarrow\ker B_{J\oplus J',g}
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_{W'}}W'\longrightarrow0.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For a background right inverse $P_b$, the first sequence splits by
$(k,w')\mapsto(k+P_bJ'_bw',w')$.  For the enlarged glued problem
let $T_g^+$ be its preliminary synthesis and use the old right
inverse with zero new coordinate:
$\widehat P_gf=(P_gf,0)$.  Its corrected preliminary lift
\[
 (1-\widehat P_gB_{J\oplus J',g})T_g^+
\]
restricts to the old lift and induces the identity on $W'$.
It is an isomorphism by the two exact sequences.  The weak error
bound shows that it has the same orientation as orthogonal
projection of $T_g^+$, just as in
\eqref{eq:projected-kernel-comparison}.  Moving $W'$ past the
eight-dimensional bubble kernel has sign $(-1)^{8\dim W'}=1$.
This proves compatibility with the determinant stabilization
sequences and independence of the columns by common enlargement.
In this comparison the actual negative maps $-J_b$ and $-J'_b$ are
retained in both sequences.  Common real auxiliary factors cancel
between the background and glued problems; no parity assumption on
$\dim W$ or $\dim W'$ is needed.

Apply this construction to the spinor homotopy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orientation-spinor-homotopy}
 D_t=\mathscr D_{A,t\Phi},\qquad
 D_{g,t}=\mathscr D_{A_g,t\Phi_g},\qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\end{equation}
At each $t$ choose smooth representatives spanning the background
cokernel.  They give surjectivity on a neighborhood of $t$.
A finite cover of $[0,1]$ and the union of these columns give one
finite stabilization for the whole path.  Include the original
$\Xi$ columns at $t=1$, and separate real ASD and complex Dirac
columns at $t=0$.  Redundancy is allowed by the preceding argument.
The construction works on neighborhoods in the background and
center parameters; a finite cover makes the bounds uniform over
the compact family.  Thus the localized glued family is surjective
for sufficiently small scale throughout the path.
The intermediate pairs need not solve the equations.  In particular,
real gauge cokernels at zero spinor are included in the stabilization.

The determinant comparison varies continuously along this homotopy.
At $t=0$, common enlargement permits replacement by the separated
ASD and Dirac columns; at $t=1$ it permits cancellation back to the
original augmentation.  At zero spinor choose Hermitian complements
in the Dirac blocks.  Their columns, localizations, inverses, and
projections are complex linear, so their determinant comparison is
positive.  Only the real ASD comparison remains.

For that block, the map in
\cite[Section~3(d), Proposition~(3.20)]{DonaldsonOrientation} is the
orthogonal projection of background cutoff fields and contractions
of the anti-self-dual curvature with cutoff conformal-affine fields.
For our normalized parameters $(s,\theta,\xi)$, the latter field
on the core is $sz+\theta z+\lambda\xi$ in physical coordinates.
Its curvature contraction is exactly $T_\lambda k$ there.
On a transition annulus $r\asymp\sqrt\lambda$ the curvature is
$O_N(1)$, while the dilation and rotation fields are
$O_N(\sqrt\lambda)$ and translations are $O(\lambda)$.
The resulting $L^2$ error is $O_N(\lambda^{3/2})$.
Discarded mode tails have the same bound by $r^{-3}$ decay;
metric projection errors are smaller by the $O(r^2)$ metric
discrepancy.  Different background cutoffs contribute $O_N(\lambda)$
in $L^2$.  These errors tend to zero in the weighted operator norm
of Proposition~\ref{prop:projected-kernel}.

Flattening cutoffs and column cutoffs may also be matched to those
in the source.  Alternatively, the difference of flattened connections
is $O_N(\sqrt\lambda)$ on radius $O_N(\sqrt\lambda)$, hence
$O_N(\lambda)$ in $L^4$; a difference of smooth cutoff columns is
$O_N(\lambda^{3/2})$ in $L^{4/3}$.  Their linear interpolation
is therefore a small perturbation of the uniformly surjective
augmented family, with the same mode estimates.  It preserves the
orientation comparison.  On the ASD block there is no bubble
auxiliary coordinate, so the Hilbert projection used above is the
ordinary configuration $L^2$ projection with the background
stabilization coordinates retained.

The source uses gauge row $-d_A^*$.  Apply the fixed target
isomorphism $C(f,\omega)=(-f,\omega)$ to both its background and
glued operators and to their finite columns.  Kernels and the
projected modes are unchanged, while cokernels and determinant
sequences are transported by $C$.  The induced square of determinant
addition maps commutes, since on the stabilized kernels both
horizontal maps are the same projected synthesis.  Both standard
orientations are propagated from a fixed split reference; any sign
between their reference normalizations is consequently carried
equally to the background and glued sides and cancels.  In particular,
one does not discard a real constant-gauge factor separately on one
side.  This gives the $+d_A^*$ convention used in
\cite[Section~2.2]{FeehanLenessII}.
The mode identification and Proposition~\ref{prop:projected-kernel}
now identify~\eqref{eq:general-kernel-addition}, in orientation,
with the instanton-addition map that defines the standard ASD
orientation from a split reference bundle and $\Omega$.

Finally, the orientation $O^{\mathrm{asd}}$ is defined by the same
spinor homotopy and the complex Dirac orientation
\cite[Lemma~2.2, Equation~(2.5), and Definition~2.3]{FeehanLenessII}.
Transport back to $t=1$ and cancel the common auxiliary columns.
At a reducible background,
\cite[Lemma~2.6]{FeehanLenessII} identifies
$O^{\mathrm{asd}}(\Omega,c_1(L))$ with the Seiberg--Witten tangent
orientation and complex normal orientation.  The original complex
augmentation gives the stated background kernel orientation.
Continuity and the background derivative comparison extend this
identification over the small disk $N(\varepsilon)$.
All original maps are equivariant; independence of the extra local
columns makes these local orientation comparisons agree on overlaps
and under the frame and circle actions.  This proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

In particular the orientation of the kernel identification
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-excision}
 \ker\widehat{\mathscr D}_{q_g}
 \cong\ker\widehat{\mathscr D}^{X}_q\oplus\cK_I^{\rm fr}
\end{equation}
is represented by the synthesis in~\eqref{eq:synthesis}.

The ordered model kernels and finite-dimensional stabilizations give
\begin{align}\label{eq:model-determinants}
 \det\widehat{\mathscr D}^{X}_q
 &=\det N_p\otimes\det T_pM_{\mathfrak s},\notag\\
 \det\cK_I^{\rm fr}
 &=\det T_IM_1^s(S^4,\delta),\\
 \det\widehat{\mathscr D}_{q_g}
 &=\det\mathscr D_{q_g}\otimes\det\Xi_p\otimes\det K_I.
 \notag
\end{align}
The last identity is the determinant exact sequence for adding the
finite-dimensional domain $\Xi_p\oplus K_I$ with the ordered map
$-(J_{s,\lambda}\oplus J_{i,\lambda})$.  Its minus sign has positive
determinant because the real stabilization dimension is $2r_\Xi+2$.
Combining~\eqref{eq:augmented-excision}--\eqref{eq:model-determinants} and
putting the obstruction determinants on the dual side yields
\begin{align}\label{eq:determinant-splitting}
 \det\mathscr D_{q_g}\cong{}&
 \det N_p\otimes\det T_pM_{\mathfrak s}
 \otimes\det T_I M_1^{s,\natural}(S^4,\delta)
 \otimes\det T_xX\notag\\
 &\otimes(\det\Xi_p)^*\otimes(\det K_I)^*.
\end{align}
Here the centered bubble factor contains scale and relative rotation; its
four translations are identified orientation-preservingly with $T_xX$ by
the chosen oriented tangent frame.  Moving the four-dimensional translation
factor past the four-dimensional scale--rotation factor contributes no sign.

The splitting~\eqref{eq:determinant-splitting} has no orientation monodromy
around a loop in $M_{\mathfrak s}$.  The tangent bundle is oriented by
$\Omega$, the bundles $N$ and $\Xi$ have their complex orientations, the
instanton modes have the fixed quaternionic orientation, $TX$ is oriented,
and $K_I$ is a complex line.  Possible spectral flow of an associated
self-adjoint family changes neither this real orientation nor the global
stabilization.

The tangent- and adjoint-frame determinants introduced before taking the
diagonal $\SO(4)\times\SO(3)$ quotient cancel between the ordered tangent
exact sequences for the framed parameter space and for the orbit directions.
Both group dimensions are moved past identical factors, so the two Koszul
signs cancel.  The remaining relative-frame factor is the $\SO(3)$ already
contained in $M_1^{s,\natural}$.

There is also no orientation monodromy around this relative-frame factor.
The generator of $\pi_1(\SO(3))$ lifts to a path in $\SU(2)$ from $1$ to
$-1$.  Equivariance of Proposition~\ref{prop:addition-orientation} transports the determinant
along this lift.  At the endpoint, $-1$ acts trivially on adjoint connection
directions, by complex multiplication by $-1$ on each complex spinor or
obstruction factor, and by $-1$ on the four-dimensional quaternionic
parameter space.  Each real determinant is $+1$.  Hence the
orientation descends around the nontrivial loop in $\SO(3)$.

The path $q_g+t u_g$ transports the determinant to the corrected
operator.  The following proposition identifies this transport with
the horizontal tangent of the corrected chart.  Comparison with the
published obstruction reduction uses the common enlargement in
Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-reduction}; the additional complex factors
cancel with their duals.  The normalization discrepancy in the
spinor-bilinear term is removed by a zero-order homotopy through positive
coefficients.  The ordered factors in~\eqref{eq:determinant-splitting}
are those of \cite[Section~3.9]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.

\begin{proposition}[orientation of the corrected chart]
\label{prop:chart-orientation}
At a positive-scale zero of $\chi$, there is a canonical orientation
isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orientation-isomorphism}
 \det\mathscr D_{\gamma(g)}
 \cong\det T_g\cG^+\otimes(\det\Upsilon_g)^*.
\end{equation}
Under this isomorphism, $D\gamma$ identifies the transverse-zero-locus
orientation of $\chi^{-1}(0)$ with the orientation
$O^{\mathrm{asd}}(\Omega,c_1(L))$ of the actual monopole moduli space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $q_t=q_g+t u_g$ and keep $J_g$ and $R_g$ fixed.  On augmented
variables define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-kernel-transport}
 B_t(v,\eta)=
 (d_{q_t}^{0,*}v,D\mathfrak S_{q_t}v-J_g\eta),\qquad
 \mathcal F_t=(B_t,R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}).
\end{equation}
The quadratic estimates give $B_t-B_0=O(\lambda^{1/3})$ from the
first-order augmented space to $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Y$, uniformly
for $t\in[0,1]$.  The graph completions therefore coincide, and
\eqref{eq:square-family-inverse} and a Neumann series make every
$\mathcal F_t$ invertible.  The maps
\[
 \widetilde S_t k=\mathcal F_t^{-1}(0,k)
\]
identify the full kernels continuously, with
$R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}\widetilde S_t=1$.  They give the
ordered determinant transport along this path; intermediate pairs
need not solve the equations.

At a zero of $\chi$, differentiating~\eqref{eq:extended-identity}
removes the derivative of $J_g$.  Gauge equivariance gives
$D\mathfrak S_{\gamma(g)}d_{\gamma(g)}^0=0$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-horizontal-tangent}
 \Theta_g\dot g=
 (H_{\gamma(g)}D\gamma_g\dot g,D\chi_g\dot g)
 \in\ker B_1.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:horizontal-stability},
Proposition~\ref{prop:correction-derivatives}, and the uniformly bounded
pregluing derivative give
\begin{align}\label{eq:corrected-tangent-retraction}
 R_g\operatorname{pr}_{\mathbb X}\Theta_g
 &=R_gH_gdq_g+O(\lambda^{1/3})\notag\\
 &=\jmath_g+o(1).
\end{align}
After applying $\jmath_g^{-1}$ this map has positive determinant.
Thus $\Theta_g$ is an isomorphism and identifies the parameter orientation
with the augmented kernel orientation transported from $t=0$.
The horizontal projection in~\eqref{eq:corrected-horizontal-tangent}
is essential; $R_g$ need not vanish on gauge directions.

Proposition~\ref{prop:addition-orientation} identifies the orientation
at $t=0$ with the first four factors of
\eqref{eq:determinant-splitting}.  These are the determinant line of
the tangent bundle of the virtual domain, while
$\Upsilon_g=\Xi_p\oplus K_I$.  The stabilization exact sequence
therefore gives~\eqref{eq:orientation-isomorphism} with its stated
orientation.  The ordering $K_I\oplus\Xi_p$ used in one source formula may be
interchanged with $\Xi_p\oplus K_I$ without sign because both have even real
dimension.  Similarly, the analytic stabilization is $-J_g$, but
$\dim_\RR\Upsilon_g=2r_\Xi+2$ is even.

Proposition~\ref{prop:transversality} gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow T_g\chi^{-1}(0)\longrightarrow T_g\cG^+
 \xrightarrow{D\chi_g}\Upsilon_g\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its determinant is the right side of~\eqref{eq:orientation-isomorphism}
with the same ordered factors.  The claim follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The residual circle and the link boundary}
Let $\cZ^+=\chi^{-1}(0)\cap\cG^+$.  On the free circle locus define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-quotient-orientation}
 \lambda_{\cZ}=v_{S^1}\wedge\widetilde\lambda_{\cZ/S^1}.
\end{equation}
The weight-two presentation changes the positive speed of an orbit but not
its orientation.  Let $\vec r$ point outward from the deleted neighborhood
of the ideal stratum.  The boundary of its complement has
\[
 \lambda_{\cZ/S^1}=-\vec r\wedge\lambda_L.
\]
Together with~\eqref{eq:circle-quotient-orientation}, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:link-orientation}
 \lambda_{\cM}=-v_{S^1}\wedge\vec r
 \wedge\widetilde\lambda_L,
\end{equation}
which is the convention in
\cite[Equation~(3.81)]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  The sign can also be read in
one complex fiber: moving the positive orbit vector $i\vec r$ past the
outward radial vector contributes the displayed minus sign.

The compactified virtual link has a background-normal face and an
instanton-scale face.  Their common edge receives opposite orientations,
because exchanging the two outward normals contributes a minus sign.  Thus
the faces form an oriented relative cycle.  This statement concerns the
smooth positive-scale zero set and the relative Euler cycle; it does not
reinterpret the lowest stratum as a transverse zero set.

Finally, if $w$ is an integral lift of $w_2(\mathfrak t')$, the orientation
comparison of~\cite{FeehanLenessII} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lift-orientation}
 O^{\mathrm{asd}}(\Omega,w)
 =(-1)^{(w-c_1(L))^2/4}
 O^{\mathrm{asd}}(\Omega,c_1(L)).
\end{equation}
Write $o_{\mathfrak t'}(w,\mathfrak s)
=\tfrac14(w+c_1(\mathfrak s)-c_1(\mathfrak t'))^2$ for this integral
exponent.  The circle and boundary constructions are linear
in the ambient orientation, so the same factor relates the corresponding
link orientations.  This completes the orientation assertion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:neighborhood}.

\section{The level-one pairing}\label{sec:pairing}
In this section write $\delta_{\rm gl}>0$ for the fixed small upper bound
on the gluing scale; $\delta$ will denote the insertion degree.

We now apply the family neighborhood theorem to the published level-one
pairing.  It identifies the actual and virtual links, allows geometric
representatives to be pulled back, and carries the universal bundle through
an equivariant homotopy to the splicing map.  The obstruction section
represents the smooth zero locus by a relative Euler class, and the
orientation comparison preserves signed intersections.  Corollary~\ref{cor:embedding}, Proposition
\ref{prop:neighborhood}, Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruction-section}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:chart-orientation} supply these inputs.  The dimension
argument of~\cite[Lemmas~3.9 and~5.4]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} puts the counted
intersection in the positive-scale smooth locus and away from the common
edge.  The universal-bundle calculation, Thom and Segre reduction,
charge-one fiber evaluations, and Jacobi identity are retained as published
topological inputs.  The proved orientation comparison permits their
application to signed intersections on the actual link.

We retain the notation of that paper for the
pairing.  Let $\mathfrak t'$ be a
$\operatorname{spin}^u$ structure with $w_2(\mathfrak t')$ good in the sense
of~\cite[Definition~2.3]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}, and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-level-relation}
 \bigl(c_1(\mathfrak t')-c_1(\mathfrak s)\bigr)^2
 =p_1(\mathfrak t')+4.
\end{equation}
Let $h\in H_2(X;\RR)$ and let $x\in H_0(X;\mathbb Z)$ be the positive
generator.  Write $Q_X$ for the intersection form on $H_2(X;\RR)$.
For a monomial $z$ in $h$ and $x$, let $\overline V(z)$ denote
the closure of a transverse geometric representative of the Donaldson class
$\mu_p(z)$; let $\overline W$ represent the circle class $\mu_c$; and let
$\overline L_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}$ be the standard oriented link of
$M_{\mathfrak s}\times X$ in
$\overline\cM_{\mathfrak t'}^{*,0}/S^1$.  The symbol $\#$ below denotes the
signed count of a transverse zero-dimensional intersection.  Let
$\mu_{\mathfrak s}\in H^2(M_{\mathfrak s};\mathbb Z)$ be the first Chern
class of the Seiberg--Witten base-point fibration.  With the orientation
determined by the chosen homology orientation, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sw-definition}
 \SW_X(\mathfrak s)
 =\left\langle\mu_{\mathfrak s}^{d},[M_{\mathfrak s}]\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Also write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asd-dimension}
 d_a(\mathfrak t')=-2p_1(\mathfrak t')
 -\tfrac32\bigl(\chi(X)+\sigma(X)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-pairing-notation}
 \beta=c_1(\mathfrak s)-c_1(\mathfrak t'),\qquad
 B_h=\langle\beta,h\rangle.
\end{equation}
Write $c_1^2(X)=2\chi(X)+3\sigma(X)$.  For the Jacobi polynomials we
use the normalization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-jacobi-normalization}
 P_d^{A,B}(\zeta)=2^{-d}\sum_{v=0}^d
 \binom{d+A}{v}\binom{d+B}{d-v}
 (\zeta-1)^{d-v}(\zeta+1)^v.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pullbacks and Euler classes}
Let $\cG^{\mathrm{vir},*}$ be the locus in the virtual gluing space on
which the effective circle acts freely.  With the associated-line
convention fixed before~\eqref{eq:background-euler},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nu-definition}
 \nu=c_1(\cG^{\mathrm{vir},*}\longrightarrow
             \cG^{\mathrm{vir},*}/S^1).
\end{equation}
The characteristic classes below are first taken on this locus and then
extended in the relative cohomology used for the virtual link.
The line defining $\mu_c$ on the monopole
configuration quotient has weight $-2$, while the action on the gluing
domain before effectivization has twice the angular parameter.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-pullback}
 \gamma^*\mu_c=-\nu.
\end{equation}

Away from the incidence diagonal, the pulled-back universal $SO(3)$ bundle
splits as $\RR\oplus\mathbb L^{\mathrm{vir}}$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:associated-line-c1}
 c_1(\mathbb L^{\mathrm{vir}})=
 2\pi_{\mathfrak s}^*\mu_{\mathfrak s}-\nu
 +\pi_{X,2}^*c_1(L),\qquad c_1(L)=-\beta.
\end{equation}
The equivariant isotopy~\eqref{eq:isotopy} identifies the corrected and
splicing pullbacks.  Insertion of one instanton changes the Pontrjagin class
by the incidence diagonal, so
\begin{align}\label{eq:universal-p1}
 (\gamma\times\id_X)^*p_1(\mathbb F_{\mathfrak t'})
 ={}&\bigl(2\pi_{\mathfrak s}^*\mu_{\mathfrak s}-\nu
 +\pi_{X,2}^*c_1(L)\bigr)^2\notag\\
 &-4(\pi_X\times\id_X)^*\operatorname{PD}[\Delta].
\end{align}
The coefficient $-4$ is fixed by a chain meeting the diagonal once.  Since
$\mu_p(\alpha)=-\tfrac14p_1(\mathbb F_{\mathfrak t'})/\alpha$, slant product
with a point and a surface gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:mu-pullbacks}
 \gamma^*\mu_p(x)
 &=-\tfrac14(2\mu_{\mathfrak s}-\nu)^2
   +\pi_X^*\operatorname{PD}[x],\notag\\
 \gamma^*\mu_p(h)
 &=\tfrac12\langle\beta,h\rangle
   (2\mu_{\mathfrak s}-\nu)+\pi_X^*\operatorname{PD}[h].
\end{align}
These are the formulas of
\cite[Lemma~4.10]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}; the second summand in each line is
the diagonal correction.

The background Euler class is~\eqref{eq:background-euler}.  For the
instanton factor, the square of the framing line loses the central twist:
\[
 \bigl(\SU(2)\times_{\{\pm1\}}\CC\bigr)^{\otimes2}
 \cong\SO(3)\times\CC.
\]
Accounting for the central and residual circle weights gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:instanton-euler-square}
 c_1\bigl((\Upsilon^i/S^1)^{\otimes2}\bigr)
 =\pi_X^*c_1(\mathfrak t')-\nu.
\end{equation}
Thus, in rational cohomology,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:instanton-euler}
 e(\Upsilon^i/S^1)
 =\tfrac12\bigl(\pi_X^*c_1(\mathfrak t')-\nu\bigr).
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ comes from taking the tensor square of the framed
associated line.

\subsection{Relative Euler reduction}
Let $\delta,m,\eta$ be nonnegative integers with $2m\leq\delta$.
Put $n=\delta-2m$ and $z=h^nx^m$; the exponent $\eta$ records the
circle-class insertion.  Here $\delta$ is the degree parameter for the
Donaldson insertions.
On the positive-scale virtual link, the section determines a relative Euler
class.  The geometric-representative cocycle is supported away from the
lower-level zero set.  The homotopy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-homotopy}
\chi_s\oplus\chi_i\longmapsto\chi_s\oplus t\chi_i,
\qquad 0\leq t\leq1,
\end{equation}
does not vanish off $\chi_s^{-1}(0)$.  At $t=0$ the class factors as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-factorization}
 e(\Upsilon/S^1,\chi_s)
 =e(\overline\Upsilon^s/S^1,\chi_s)
  \smile e(\Upsilon^i/S^1).
\end{equation}
This is precisely where one needs continuity of the background section and
the cone topology, but not transversality of the lowest stratum.  The signed
actual-link count is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-pairing}
 \left\langle
 \overline\mu_p(z)\smile\overline\mu_c^{\eta}
 \smile\overline e_s\smile\overline e_i,
 [\overline L^{\mathrm{vir}}]\right\rangle.
\end{equation}

Write
\[
 H=\pi_X^*\operatorname{PD}[h],\qquad
 X_0=\pi_X^*\operatorname{PD}[x],\qquad
 c=\pi_X^*c_1(\mathfrak t').
\]
Substitution of~\eqref{eq:circle-pullback},
\eqref{eq:mu-pullbacks},~\eqref{eq:background-euler}, and
\eqref{eq:instanton-euler} into~\eqref{eq:relative-pairing} gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:pairing-integrand}
 \mathcal P=\Bigl\langle&
 \left[\tfrac12B_h(2\mu_{\mathfrak s}-\nu)+H\right]^n\notag\\
 &\smile\left[-\tfrac14(2\mu_{\mathfrak s}-\nu)^2+X_0\right]^m
 (-\nu)^{\eta+r_\Xi}\notag\\
 &\smile\tfrac12(c-\nu),[\overline L^{\mathrm{vir}}]
 \Bigr\rangle.
\end{align}
The dimension relation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairing-dimension}
 \delta+\eta=d+r_N-r_\Xi+2.
\end{equation}

The equivariant Thom division of
\cite[Propositions~5.20--5.21]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} expresses this pairing
over
\[
 M_{\mathfrak s}\times
 \partial\overline{\Gl}_{\mathfrak t}(\delta_{\rm gl})/S^1
\]
as a finite sum involving the Segre classes of $N$.  The normal-bundle
calculation~\cite[Lemma~2.4]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-segre}
 s_j(N)=S_j\mu_{\mathfrak s}^{j},
\end{equation}
where $S_j$ is the binomial coefficient in
\cite[Equation~(6.14)]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  On this product base,
\cite[Lemma~5.23]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-splitting}
 \nu=\nu_{\mathfrak t}+2\mu_{\mathfrak s}.
\end{equation}
Every nonzero term then has base factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sw-base-pairing}
 \left\langle\mu_{\mathfrak s}^{d},[M_{\mathfrak s}]\right\rangle
 =\SW_X(\mathfrak s).
\end{equation}
In particular, positive-dimensional base classes have not been discarded;
the Segre calculation combines all of them into the single pairing in
\eqref{eq:sw-base-pairing}.

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobi-parameters}
 A_J=\eta-d+1,\qquad
 B_J=\tfrac12(2\delta-d_a(\mathfrak t')-d_{\mathfrak s})
      -\tfrac14\bigl(\chi(X)+\sigma(X)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobi-values}
 J_d=P_d^{A_J,B_J}(0),\qquad
 J_d'=P_d^{A_J-1,B_J+1}(0),
\end{equation}
using~\eqref{eq:intro-jacobi-normalization}.  The finite Segre sum is
$2^dJ_d$, except in the term containing $Hc$, where it is $2^dJ_d'$;
this is the Jacobi identity of
\cite[Lemma~6.2]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.

\begin{lemma}[published charge-one link integrals]
\label{lem:instanton-link-integrals}
Write $\nu_t=\nu_{\mathfrak t}$ for the effective circle class on the
charge-one instanton link in~\eqref{eq:circle-splitting}.
With the orientation in~\eqref{eq:link-orientation},
\begin{align}\label{eq:instanton-link-integrals}
 \langle\nu_tX_0,[\partial\overline\Gl/S^1]\rangle&=2,
 &\langle\nu_t^2H,[\partial\overline\Gl/S^1]\rangle&=-4B_h,
 \notag\\
 \langle\nu_tHc,[\partial\overline\Gl/S^1]\rangle
 &=2\langle c_1(\mathfrak t'),h\rangle,
 &\langle\nu_t^3,[\partial\overline\Gl/S^1]\rangle
 &=6\beta^2+2c_1^2(X),\notag\\
 \langle\nu_t^2c,[\partial\overline\Gl/S^1]\rangle
 &=-4\beta\cdot c_1(\mathfrak t').&&
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
These numerical evaluations are cohomological inputs from
\cite[Lemma~5.24 and Equation~(5.77)]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}; they are not
reproved here.  We recall enough of that calculation to fix their
normalization.  On a finite cover of $X$, the framed charge-one gluing bundle
is covered with degree $-2$ by the boundary of a rank-two Hermitian bundle
$F$.  The induced map of projectivizations has degree $-1$.
For the usual hyperplane class $\xi=c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(F)}(1))$,
the circle class used in that source is $-\xi$, so $\nu_t$ pulls
back to $-2\xi$.  The projective-bundle pushforward
\[
 \pi_*(\xi^{1+j})=s_j(F)
\]
therefore gives
$\langle\nu_tX_0\rangle=(-1)(-2)=2$ and, from the published Segre classes of $F$,
the second and fourth values in~\eqref{eq:instanton-link-integrals}.  Taking
$h=\operatorname{PD}(c)$ in the second value gives the fifth.  Finally,
Poincare duality in Equation~(5.77) gives
\[
 \langle\nu_tHc\rangle
 =\langle\nu_tX_0\rangle\langle c_1(\mathfrak t'),h\rangle,
\]
which is the third value.  Since $c_1(L)=-\beta$, these conventions agree
with~\eqref{eq:circle-pullback} and~\eqref{eq:link-orientation}.
\end{proof}

Combining the base reduction with
Lemma~\ref{lem:instanton-link-integrals} gives the following polynomial form,
which does not divide by a Jacobi value and therefore remains defined when
$J_d=0$.

\begin{corollary}[family level-one pairing]\label{cor:pairing}\label{cor:intro-pairing}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:neighborhood}.
Suppose in addition that
$w_2(\mathfrak t')$ is good in the sense of
\cite[Definition~2.3]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  Let $\delta,m,\eta$ be
nonnegative integers satisfying
\[
 0\leq m\leq\lfloor\delta/2\rfloor,
 \qquad
 2(\delta+\eta)=\dim(\cM_{\mathfrak t'}/S^1)-1,
\]
put $n=\delta-2m$ and use the notation in
\eqref{eq:intro-pairing-notation} and
\eqref{eq:jacobi-parameters}--\eqref{eq:jacobi-values}.  Then
\begin{align*}
&\#\bigl(\overline V(h^{\delta-2m}x^m)\cap\overline W^\eta
\cap\overline L_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}\bigr)\\
&\quad=(-1)^{m+1+d}2^{d-\delta}
\left\langle\mu_{\mathfrak s}^{d},[M_{\mathfrak s}]\right\rangle\\
&\qquad\quad\times
\left[J_d\left(a_0B_h^n+a_1B_h^{n-2}Q_X(h,h)\right)
+2nJ_d'B_h^{n-1}\langle c_1(\mathfrak t'),h\rangle\right],
\end{align*}
where terms with negative powers of $B_h$ are omitted and
\begin{align*}
 a_0&=3\beta^2+c_1^2(X)+2\beta\cdot c_1(\mathfrak t')
 +4n-4m,\qquad
 a_1=4\binom n2.
\end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:pairing-integrand}, equivariant Thom division,
\eqref{eq:normal-segre}, and~\eqref{eq:circle-splitting} reduce the base
degree to~\eqref{eq:sw-base-pairing}.  The Jacobi identity gives $2^dJ_d$
and $2^dJ_d'$ in the two indicated groups of terms.
The $\nu_t^3$ and $\nu_t^2c$ evaluations in
Lemma~\ref{lem:instanton-link-integrals} give
$3\beta^2+c_1^2(X)$ and $2\beta\cdot c_1(\mathfrak t')$.
The $\nu_t^2H$ evaluation gives $4n$, while $\nu_tX_0$ gives
both $-4m$ and the $Q_X(h,h)$ term, using $H^2=Q_X(h,h)X_0$.
The $\nu_tHc$ evaluation supplies the term with $J_d'$.
Collecting these terms gives $a_0$ and $a_1$.
The sign and power of two are
$(-1)^{m+1+d}2^{d-\delta}$, as in
\cite[Equations~(6.15)--(6.18)]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}; Proposition
\ref{prop:chart-orientation} introduces no further sign.  This proves the
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[zero-dimensional specialization]
\label{cor:zero-dimensional-pairing}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:pairing}, if
$d_{\mathfrak s}=0$, then $J_0=J_0'=1$ and
\begin{align*}
&\#\bigl(\overline V(h^{\delta-2m}x^m)\cap\overline W^\eta
\cap\overline L_{\mathfrak t',\mathfrak s}\bigr)\\
&\quad=(-1)^{m+1}2^{-\delta}\SW_X(\mathfrak s)
\left(a_0B_h^n+b_0B_h^{n-1}\langle c_1(\mathfrak t'),h\rangle
+a_1B_h^{n-2}Q_X(h,h)\right),
\end{align*}
where $b_0=2n$ and $a_0,a_1$ are as in Corollary~\ref{cor:pairing}.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Blow-up and the degree consequence}
For completeness, write
\[
 c(X)=-\frac14\bigl(7\chi(X)+11\sigma(X)\bigr).
\]
Our series conventions are
\[
 \cD_X^w(h)=D_X^w((1+x/2)e^h),\qquad
 \SW_X^w(h)=\sum_{K}(-1)^{(w^2+w\cdot K)/2}
 \SW_X(K)e^{\langle K,h\rangle},
\]
where $K$ runs over the Seiberg--Witten basic classes.  Congruences are
congruences of formal power series by total degree in $h$; a congruence
modulo $h^k$ imposes no condition when $k\leq0$.

The blow-up specialization below preserves $a_0$.  On $\widetilde X=X\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}$, let $e$ be the
exceptional class and
\[
 c_1(\mathfrak s^\pm)=c_1(\mathfrak s)\pm\operatorname{PD}[e],\qquad
 \SW_{\widetilde X}(\mathfrak s^\pm)=\SW_X(\mathfrak s).
\]
For $\widetilde{\mathfrak t}'$ one has
$c_1(\widetilde{\mathfrak t}')=c_1(\mathfrak t')$ and
$p_1(\widetilde{\mathfrak t}')=p_1(\mathfrak t')-1$.  The exceptional
evaluations are $\mp1$, while~\eqref{eq:lift-orientation} gives exponents
$o_{\widetilde{\mathfrak t}'}(w+\operatorname{PD}[e],\mathfrak s^+)
=o_{\mathfrak t'}(w,\mathfrak s)-1$ and
$o_{\widetilde{\mathfrak t}'}(w+\operatorname{PD}[e],\mathfrak s^-)
=o_{\mathfrak t'}(w,\mathfrak s)$.  The two exceptional contributions
therefore add after orientation weighting.  Their factor two converts
$2^{-(\delta+1)}$ to $2^{-\delta}$.

Algebraically,
\begin{align*}
&3(\beta^2-1)+(c_1^2(X)-1)+2\beta\cdot c_1(\mathfrak t')
 +4(\delta+1-2m)-4m\\
&\qquad=3\beta^2+c_1^2(X)+2\beta\cdot c_1(\mathfrak t')
 +4(\delta-2m)-4m.
\end{align*}
Together with $Q_{\widetilde X}(e,h)=0$ and $e^2=-1$, this is the $k=0$
case of~\cite[Proposition~6.5]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.

Recall that abundance means that the orthogonal complement of the
Seiberg--Witten basic classes contains a hyperbolic sublattice.
Effectiveness is the link-pairing hypothesis of
\cite[Definitions~1.2--1.3]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}; in particular, it
requires vanishing of the relevant link pairings at every level when
the Seiberg--Witten invariant vanishes.  This hypothesis is retained
in the following application.

\begin{corollary}[degree consequence]\label{cor:degree}\label{cor:intro-degree}
Assume the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:pairing} for every level-one
stratum entering the comparison.  Suppose also that $X$ is abundant,
effective, and of
Seiberg--Witten simple type, and choose the characteristic data as in
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  Then
\begin{align}\label{eq:series-congruence}
 \cD_X^w(h)&\equiv0\equiv\SW_X^w(h)
 &&\pmod{h^{c(X)-2}},\notag\\
 \cD_X^w(h)&\equiv2^{2-c(X)}e^{Q_X(h,h)/2}\SW_X^w(h)
 &&\pmod{h^{c(X)+2}}.
\end{align}
The first coefficient identity below applies when $c(X)\geq2$ and
the second when $c(X)\geq0$, with terms of negative exponent omitted:
\begin{align}\label{eq:terminal-degrees}
 D_X^w(h^{c(X)-2}x)
 &=2^{3-c(X)}\sum_{\mathfrak s}
 (-1)^{(w^2+w\cdot c_1(\mathfrak s))/2}\SW_X(\mathfrak s)
 \langle c_1(\mathfrak s),h\rangle^{c(X)-2},\notag\\
 D_X^w(h^{c(X)})
 &=2^{2-c(X)}\sum_{\mathfrak s}
 (-1)^{(w^2+w\cdot c_1(\mathfrak s))/2}\SW_X(\mathfrak s)\notag\\
 &\quad\times\left(\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),h\rangle^{c(X)}
 +\binom{c(X)}2\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),h\rangle^{c(X)-2}
 Q_X(h,h)\right).
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For abundant, effective manifolds of Seiberg--Witten simple type, choose the
class $\Lambda$ and lift $w$ as in
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{FeehanLenessLevelOne}.  The published top-level
pairing~\cite{FeehanLenessII}, Corollary~\ref{cor:pairing}, the Donaldson and
Seiberg--Witten blow-up formulas, and the superconformal-simple-type
vanishing used in~\cite[Section~6]{FeehanLenessLevelOne} give
\eqref{eq:series-congruence}.  Extracting the coefficients of the two final
degrees gives~\eqref{eq:terminal-degrees}.
\end{proof}

As a normalization test, for a K3 surface one has $c(X)=2$ and only the zero
basic class, with Seiberg--Witten invariant one in the standard homology
orientation.  Formula~\eqref{eq:terminal-degrees} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:k3-test}
 D_{K3}(x)=2,\qquad D_{K3}(h^2)=Q_{K3}(h,h),
\end{equation}
the constant and quadratic coefficients of $e^{Q_{K3}(h,h)/2}$ in the
convention with insertion $1+x/2$.  These coefficients agree with the
standard K3 normalization.

\section{Scope and further problems}\label{sec:discussion}
The compactified embedding and reverse uniqueness give the neighborhood
in Theorem~\ref{thm:neighborhood}.  Critical strong approximation for
arbitrary one-bubble sequences follows from uniform local neck gauges
and summability of the coexact tangential modes.  The constant temporal
mode is retained through its endpoint frame.

The weak inverse compares the kernel map with projected cutoff modes
at the $L^2$ scale of the bubble.  Finite stabilization through zero
spinor reduces its orientation to ASD instanton addition and the
complex Dirac orientation.  Proposition~\ref{prop:chart-orientation}
carries this comparison to the corrected chart and its link boundary.
The pairing applications retain the named topological assumptions;
they do not give an unconditional Donaldson--Seiberg--Witten comparison.

The discussion concerns one charge-one bubble over a compact regular
nonzero-spinor background.  Nonregular bases, zero-spinor solutions,
collision strata, and levels two and higher require additional arguments.
